### A-3/A-4 · what a page header owes ON TOP of the recipe — exactly four things

The recipe owns the entire shrink: `scale()` + rise on the compositor, the shadow lift as
content passes under, the quiet, all riding `--chrome-collapse-t`. Nothing was
re-implemented. Four things a page header owes that a dock does not:

1. **A seat.** `position: sticky; inset-block-start: 0`. Without it the header scrolls
   away and "shrink on scroll" has nothing to happen to.
2. **A rung.** `z-index: var(--z-header)` — the scale's own name for this exact thing
   (`scheme-motion.css:202` → 35), above `--z-content` (10) and below `--z-dock` (40)
   and every overlay. This one was CAUGHT BY THE CLOSING CHALLENGE: the first cut used a
   local `z-index: 1`, and a census of `demo/stories/**` found `motion/scroll.vue:71`'s
   sticky progress rail sitting on `z-10`, which would have hairlined an accent line
   across the collapsed title on that route. A page's own chrome does not stack under a
   story's decoration. The literal is gone; the rung is named.
3. **An origin.** The recipe's `transform-origin: top center` is a dock's seat; scaled
   about its centre a left-aligned title DRIFTS inward and the eye reads the drift rather
   than the size. `top left` is the recipe's own documented consumer re-point.
4. **A plate.** Content scrolling under a sticky header with nothing behind it is
   illegible. The plate is a `::before` whose **opacity IS `--chrome-collapse-t`** — absent
   at rest (an uncollapsed page is byte-identical to the page before this wave), full once
   collapsed. Opacity-only: the gradient rasterises once and then only composites, so no
   scroll frame repaints it, and the resting `backdrop-filter` is never touched — the
   recipe's crisp-glass fence, held at the consumer.

Plus one retune with grounds: `--chrome-fade-depth: 0`. The recipe floors a collapsed dock
at 0.72 opacity; this chrome carries the page's `h1` AND its return leg, so it takes the
documented no-quiet retune. **It shrinks; it never dims.**

### A-3 (cont.) · THE WEIGHT — the liquid-weight edict, discharged through #29's law

`--chrome-collapse-t` is a registered `<number>` (`property-regs-specular.css:78`), so it
INTERPOLATES rather than stepping, and a curve put on it is inherited by everything that
reads it: scale, rise, shadow and plate settle as **one body** instead of four.

It takes the **DOCK row, by name**:

```css
transition: --chrome-collapse-t var(--spring-dock-duration) var(--spring-dock);
```

No duration, no bezier, no figure. That is #29's grammar law executed
(`routeGrammar.ts:9-16` — *"IT MINTS NO SPRING FIGURE… A row here NAMES the spring row
that owns its job and the clock it reads that row on"*), and the row whose job is *a
chrome rect settling into a new position* is `dock`. The transition-a-registered-custom
idiom is already on disk at `src/styles/glass/grasp.css:206`.

The result is the edict's own words: the ramp trails the finger with real inertia, and the
scroll-stop snap arrives on the dock settle instead of teleporting.

**PRM-GATED.** The transition sits inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`.
Under reduce the machine already snaps the scalar to a discrete 0/1 and the chrome is never
half-collapsed; an unguarded transition would put the interpolation straight back. The
close battery asserts the bare rule carries no `transition:` at all.
