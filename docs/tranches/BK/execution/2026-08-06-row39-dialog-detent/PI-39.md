## §8 · P7 — THE FPS FALSIFIER · **RED — THE WAVE ESCALATES**

> RECORD §2.1 and §5: *"the fps budget is the falsifier, not a fallback: there is no second
> geometry waiting behind this one… a miss escalates the wave."*

### Method — two independent instruments, disclosed in full

**Method A — in-page rAF frame-delta probe.** One synthetic `pointermove` dispatched **per
animation frame** (which is exactly what a finger produces — the browser coalesces real
pointer input to one move per frame), from the grip, `dy = −420px` over ~450–505 ms, then
`pointerup`, then the settle sampled until the scalar is quiet for 300 ms. Frame deltas are
`requestAnimationFrame` timestamps.

**Method B — chrome-devtools performance trace.** `performance_start_trace` (no reload) →
gesture bracketed by `performance.mark('pi39-gesture-start'/'-end')` → `performance_stop_trace`.
Frame cadence taken from **`DrawFrame`** event deltas inside the user-timing window; long
tasks from `RunTask` durations. This is the renderer's own instrumentation, independent of
my probe.

**Fidelity of the gesture.** The drag verifiably drove the shipped engine: `t` went
**0.25 → 0.7167** under the finger and settled on **0.70** (or 1.00 when the fling carried
further) — i.e. through a **full rung transition** (0.25 → 0.4 → 0.7), released with real
projected velocity. It is the engine's own path, not a scripted scalar write.

**One disclosed instrumentation.** `Element.prototype.setPointerCapture` throws
`NotFoundError` for a synthetic `pointerId`, and the engine calls it **before** attaching
its move/up listeners (`detents/use.ts:189-192`), so an unshimmed synthetic drag aborts at
`pointerdown`. A shim swallows **only** `NotFoundError` from
`setPointerCapture`/`releasePointerCapture`. It touches pointer capture and nothing else —
no layout, no paint, no spring, no geometry. Controls below show it costs nothing.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1440×900 | 108 | **1438 × 106.00** | **inside** (37.7%) | **outside by 14.00px** | **88.33%** | **0.9038** (designed 1.0) | **30.85%** (designed 34%) |
| 390×844 | 101.27 | **388 × 99.27** | inside (40.3%) | outside by 20.73px | 82.73% | 0.8575 | 29.3% |
| Safari, vh 948 | 111.75 | **1438 × 111.75** | inside | outside by 8.25px | 93.13% | 0.9433 | 32.14% |

The halo is `inset: 0` inside the padding box, so the box is the sheet height less its 1px
top and bottom borders. The 1440 figure (**106.00px**) corroborates the record's own
94.72px, which was taken on an 806px viewport (0.12·806 − 2 = 94.72).

### Stops inside or outside, and the clipping/inversion question

Computed value read back verbatim:

```
mask-image: linear-gradient(rgba(0,0,0,0.325) 0px, rgba(0,0,0,0.45) 40px,
                            rgb(0,0,0) 120px, rgb(0,0,0) 100%)
```

- The **40px** stop is **INSIDE** the 106px box.
- The **120px** stop is **OUTSIDE** it, by **14.00px**.
- The trailing `100%` resolves to 106px, which is **less than** the preceding 120px stop.
  CSS requires gradient stop positions to be non-decreasing, so it is **clamped up to
  120px**. **There is therefore no inversion** — the ramp is monotone from the outer edge
  inward for the whole box.
- **No clipping in the sense of a hard cut.** The ramp is **TRUNCATED** at 88.33% of its
