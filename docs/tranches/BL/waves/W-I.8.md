## What a dock is

A dock is one surface whose size follows its content. It is a full stadium in every state and in every frame between: its corner is half its cross extent, a one-seat dock is a circle, and a folded face that holds words is a stadium as wide as its words (O-199, O-340, C2-1). Its content lies inside that shape (C2-6). It has three resting states: collapsed to its essentials, open, and pinned open. Everything else is travel between them or between two sizes of the open state, and all of it is one kind of event: the layout changes once, and what is painted glides from where it was to where the layout now is, on one spring, with its content fading inside the plate's moving edge.

So the dock has one clock that decides when the state changes (R-2) and one carrier that moves the paint when it does (R-1). The site's dock and a page's small controls dock are the same thing at two sizes (R-12).

## Rulings

- **R-1: one carrier moves every size the dock has.** As built it reads alike in both engines: 110 of 114 fold segments pass in Chromium and 109 of 114 in WebKit (P3), and what fails is named in the table. Each row is judged against the preset over that row's own length, and by that reading the two engines draw one curve at two sampling rates. In pixels, the pill's outline is a stadium within 0.75 px at seven held points of a fold (F2, Chromium).
  - **One loop.** One `requestAnimationFrame` loop per dock steps every moving quantity on `springPreset("dock")`, then writes. It parks on the frame its last quantity lands. At rest the dock schedules no frame and writes no style (read: 0 callbacks, 0 mutations, 0 animations).
  - **One measured model**, in the root's own px: each plate's four edges, the regions, the faces, and each face's units. On a trigger it reads the new layout once, and each quantity holds painted minus layout and carries it to 0 with the velocity it had. A second change retargets the live quantities and never re-creates them. Every quantity shares the one preset, so from rest they are one curve; each keeps its own position and velocity only so that a change mid-travel turns each from where it is.
  - **Two scalars ride with them.** The posture coordinate `e` (0 collapsed, 1 open) moves on a posture change and on nothing else. The exchange coordinate `f` (0 to 1) runs once per content change (R-5). Neither is derived from the plate's extent.
  - **The plate's box is the painted shape.** The loop writes the plate's four insets and its radius in px, so width and height glide together and nothing is scaled. The plate's `clip-path` is deleted.
  - **The corner (RU-13).** For a pill the loop writes half the plate's painted cross extent, every frame; that is the owner's "Rounded." as arithmetic, and it holds for a two-row open dock as for a one-seat circle. For `shape="rounded"` and `shape="card"` it writes the lerp of the two rests' corners on `e` (a posture travel) or on `f` (a content change), each rest capped at half its cross extent. **The number the loop writes is not the shape that is painted (C2-6).** The corner bound read the plate's computed `border-radius`, which is the loop's own write, so it compared the loop with itself and would have passed over a square child. The outline is now also read in pixels (the bounds, "the outline"): at the two rests and five held points of the pill's fold the painted shape is within 0.56 to 0.73 px of the stadium of its own box; through a layer change it is within 1.03 px at six of seven points and 3.41 px at one, unexplained (F2, Chromium, `outline-f3.chromium.json`).

Nothing clips at rest, and the lane's rounded clip cuts that content during travel, so it pops at the landing. The rule: the open face's content box lies inside the stadium. Its inline inset is at least `r − √(r² − (r − b)²)`, where `r` is half the rest's cross extent and `b` is the block inset. For one row of round seats that is the padding the dock has; for the 108 px dock it is 33.6 px a side (derived). A rule keyed on the posture class cannot know the height, so the carrier writes the inset once, from the rest it has just read, and reads the width after it: a second read on the trigger frame, which G-11 counts. Whether a dock of several rows should rest as a rounded rectangle instead is the owner's (OQ-7): his "Rounded." was said of one-row docks and filter pills, and this text takes it at its word until he says otherwise.

- **R-2: one clock decides the state.** Read in both engines on the third pass: the intent, a still pointer under a scroll, the linger, its growth, the holds, a tap, a press outside, Escape, `release()`, pinned and a persistent press are inside their bounds (P3, `clock2.p3.*.json`).
  - **Three states**, with today's names: `collapsed`, `hover` (open by a pointer, focus or a tap) and `pinned`. Folding and unfolding are travel, not states.
  - **What opens a collapsed dock (RU-3): a pointer move, a press, or keyboard focus. Never a scroll.** A pointer event counts as a move only when its client coordinates differ from the last pointer event this dock saw, an enter included. An enter alone arms nothing. So a page that scrolls a dock under a still pointer opens none, in WebKit as in Chromium: both engines send an enter and a move with unchanged coordinates after a scroll, and WebKit opened the first build at 700 ms and v11.0.1 at 741 ms on those (P1). The last pointer position is kept page-wide, and the real move starts the intent, not the enter that both engines dispatch after it; later moves do not restart it (`6b657870e`). The intent, `--dock-intent` (60 ms), counts from the first such move. Keyboard focus and a press open at once. Read: no open through the scroll in either engine, and open 66 ms (Chromium) and 69 ms (WebKit) after a 2 px move (P3). If 11.1.1 lands this cure first, the carrier carries it as law.

By hover it opens on a move. By touch, a tap on Play fires Play and nothing opens the dock. In Chromium the tap also focuses the button, and focus opens it; in WebKit, as in Safari, a tap does not focus a button, so the dock never opens: no row moves (F2, `p3-fold-transport-coarse`). The third pass listed this cell as opening 52 ms after the tap, and that tap was a mouse press (C2-9). The rule: a dock with no `#collapsed` face does not fold under `(hover: none)`; it rests open. A host that wants it folded on a phone gives it a face.
  - **The linger.** A dock opened by hover, focus or a tap never folds on the frame the pointer or focus leaves. Read: 6516 and 6527 ms after a 1 s visit, 12006 ms after a 12 s visit in both engines (P3). A consumer sets the two tokens equal to turn the growth off. Any re-entry cancels the linger, a scrolled one included: a dock does not fold under the pointer.

Three things follow, in every way of opening the dock, `expandOn="press"` included: a press inside the overlay is not a press outside; focus moving into it is not focus leaving the dock; and when it closes, focus returns to the control that opened it, which is live because the dock never folded. A surface that is not glass's takes the hold itself with the context's `keepOpen()` and `release()`, as today. Built for Dialog and Sheet (`13177b08f`) and read in both engines, the press-opened dock included (P3, G-8).

## Gates

Thirteen live gates and one retired, and the list is closed. The second fold adds no gate and loosens none. It adds three bounds (the outline in pixels, the strongest face, a return), tightens three clauses that passed by construction (a corner compared with the loop's own write; a face at 0.12 counted as showing; a shadow counted by declaration), and adds cells for C2-1 to C2-17 and O-358. Each gate is read once RED and once GREEN, with its bite read once. RED is master at the cut unless a record is named. A gate is GREEN only when it is GREEN in both engines.

**How they are read.** With the measuring pass's harness (`../design/dock/wi8/measure/harness/`): `probe.mjs` writes one row per rendered frame, the sampler reads each unit's visible box through every ancestor's clip, and `judge.mjs` applies the bounds. Chromium is the headless shell, about 8.3 ms a row; WebKit is Playwright's build 2287, about 16.7 ms a row. Pixel readings are crops of a travel held at a given point: `crop.mjs` gates `requestAnimationFrame` in the page, so the carrier's loop stops on the frame that crosses a threshold, and it shoots the clip with the dock and with the dock hidden; their difference is what the dock paints. A coarse cell taps by touch in both engines, and the judge fails a coarse cell whose pointer log holds no touch event (C2-9). A response is counted from the event that causes it (a tap's lift, a click, a key), never from the probe's mark. One browser per run, a new context per cell, every run through `guarded`, every WebKit run through `guarded --exclusive webkit`.

**Every bound is stated in time, not in rows (RU-2).** The two engines sample one curve at two rates. A bound that counts rows or caps a row's step at a number read at 120 Hz forbids the spring itself at 60 Hz: the preset's own first step there is 4.6 to 5.7 % against the old 4 % cap, so no carrier could pass in WebKit. What the eye sees is how far the edge has gone at a given time, and that is the same in both. The judge computes every bound from `springPreset("dock")` as the build exports it, at each row's own length; it carries no number of its own. For the preset as it stands the derived values are 1.4 % of travel at 8.3 ms from rest, 5.0 % at 16.7 ms and 16 % at 33 ms, and a largest step of 7.0 % in 8.3 ms and 13.8 % in 16.7 ms.

- **The floor (RU-19):** a row fails an edge bound below only when it is over the bound by more than 0.25 px as well. Read: every "fail" on a vertical pill and on a layer drill in the second build is 0.1 % of a 3 to 6 px travel, three to six thousandths of a pixel. On any travel it admits at most a quarter of a pixel beyond the preset's own bound, which is under what an eye resolves in a moving edge; a step a viewer would call a jump is tens of pixels and still fails.
- **What the travel bounds judge:** a spring travel. A window resize and every change under reduced motion land in one frame by R-1 and R-11, and they are read by their own clauses (the next travel starts from the rest; R-11's clock), never by the bounds below.
- **From rest:** at every row of the first 34 ms after an edge leaves rest, the edge has covered no more than 1.15 times what the preset has covered in that time. The row before it paints the old box exactly, and in the first moving row the leaving face and every unit in it move no more than 0.5 px.
- **Any row:** no row moves an edge by more than 1.15 times the preset's largest displacement over that row's length. A row is never credited more than 33 ms.
- **Landing:** the last moving row moves an edge by no more than 1 px and no more than 2 % of travel, and the row after it equals the rest within 0.5 px.
- **No reversal:** no edge steps against its net direction, except after a `reverse-*` mark, and except the preset's own return from its pass of the rest: one consecutive run of rows at the end, summing to no more than 1.15 times the preset's overshoot for that travel (0.3 % of it, derived).
- **The corner:** a pill's corner is half the plate's painted cross extent within 0.5 px in every row and at both rests, in every scroll position; the two corners of an end are equal within 0.25 px. A rounded or card dock's corner is the lerp of its two rests within 0.5 px. This reads the value the loop writes, so it shows the loop is consistent and nothing more; the next bound reads the shape.
- **The outline (C2-6):** in a crop over a striped ground, the pixels the dock changes by more than 40 of 255 form a stadium: each row's first and last pixel is within 1 px of the stadium of the mask's own bounding box, and that box is the plate's within 1 px, at both rests and at five held points of a fold and of a layer change. A rounded or card dock is read the same way against its lerped corner. An engine whose glass cannot be masked over that ground is read over one where it can; until then its outline is unread, not passed.
- **Inside the plate (C2-6):** no unit has any part of its clipped shape outside the plate's painted shape while its effective opacity is above 0.1. Both are rounded shapes: the unit's box with its own corner, the plate's box with its corner. The sampler ignored rounding until now, so a box corner 13.9 px outside the curve passed.
- **Visible until covered:** a unit whose effective opacity is above 0.1 and whose box lies inside the plate's painted box has a non-empty visible box.
- **No overlap:** no two units of different regions intersect while both are above 0.1.
- **A fade:** from its last sample at one end to its first at the other a fade lasts at least 75 ms (P0a's shortest exit), and no two samples 16.7 ms apart differ by more than 0.4. In Chromium that compares every second row. Under reduced motion R-11's clock replaces the first clause.
- **A response (RU-19):** where a gate says "at once", the first row that shows the change is no later than 34 ms after its cause. For a travel that is turned while it moves, the change is the edge leaving the old travel's path: its step differs from what the unturned preset would have drawn. The edge's direction then reverses no later than the preset's own stopping time from the velocity it had, times 1.15, plus one row; the judge derives that time, and for a turn at a quarter of a fold it is about 19 ms (derived). A carrier that reverses direction in one row at that speed would be cutting the velocity, which the same gate forbids.
- **A blank plate:** no run of rows in which every face is under 0.1 lasts longer than 17 ms, or 34 ms after a turn. R-3's window gives about 3 ms of it by construction.
- **The strongest face (C2-4, C2-5):** on any travel, a turn included, the strongest face on the plate is under 0.3 for no longer than 50 ms. A face at 0.12 is not something an eye reads, and the bound above called it showing. Read at R-3's window: 25 to 35 ms on a fold; 34 to 58 ms on a quick third change; 60 to 98 ms on a turn made inside the dip.
- **A return (C2-3):** across a `return-*` mark made while the face returned to is above 0.1, no face above 0.1 moves more than 0.5 px in a row, and the face returned to falls or holds for no more than 34 ms before it rises.
- **The window's close (C2-18):** `[data-morphing]` is removed no later than one row after every quantity is within 0.25 px of its rest.
- **No dropped frame of glass's own:** no gap over 25 ms between rows overlaps a long task whose script is in glass's bundle, on a machine at a load of 20 or less.
