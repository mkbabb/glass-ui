# Horizontal bounding: the study

The owner, 2026-11-23, in atlas's dark mode: "Many items can horizontally scroll, like the fiters view, which should not--a design study should be done for every interactive element for this bounding error, too--communicate to value.js for their pages, glass-ui, etc".

Three faults were looked for in every interactive part:

- **(a)** content wider than its box: a `scrollWidth` over `clientWidth` where no sideways scroll is meant, or a child past its parent's edge;
- **(b)** a scroller that pans sideways under a vertical swipe, or accepts a horizontal pan it should not;
- **(c)** a page wider than the viewport.

A deliberate horizontal strip (a carousel, a long dock run, a table, a code block) may pan by a horizontal gesture, never by a vertical one. The cure is at the root and the smallest that works. `overflow-x: hidden` or `clip` on a wrapper to hide a box that is too wide is a mask, not a cure, unless the record shows the box is right and only its paint spills.

Read on glass `release/11.1.1` (4f1fa667e; the branch's tip at this reading, 14506233e, has the same `src` and `tests`). `cure.patch` (v7) applies to it with `git apply` and is what 11.1.2 takes from this study: round 1's cure (v4, kept as `cure-v4.patch`), value.js's items 1 to 3 (round 2), round 3's answers to the second critique (v6), and the answers to the third critique and atlas's GL-16 (v7, "v7: the third critique" below). Round 2's patch, with value.js's item 4, is kept as `cure-v5.patch`; item 4 is now the owner's choice 9. **Chromium** is Playwright's Chromium headless shell 149.0.7827.55; **WebKit** is Playwright's WebKit 26.5, not Safari; **Safari** was not run, and no iPhone was read. A number with no engine named is Chromium's. The method, every table and the record are in `SWEEP.md`; the critique of the first cure and the rulings on it are in `critique.md` and `fold.md`.

### Item 1: a dock run whose seats all show

The census placed the cause at the reserve boxes (`run.css:426-432`). Those boxes are right; the run around them is 4 px short. A horizontal run's width is its content box (`width: 100%` on the full layer, `layers.css:395-397`, with `box-sizing: content-box`, `run.css:355`). Its padding gives the ring its room and its negative margins hand that room back at both ends (`run.css:356-357`), so its clip reaches the reserve past its slot. Under the reserve boxes the end padding was set to 0 (`run.css:422-424`), and the end margin still took the reserve back: the clip stopped at the slot's end. A run whose seats fill their slot then had the trailing box's 4 px as a scroll range, and its last seat's ring was cut at rest.

v5 keeps the end padding and gives the trailing box an end margin of its own length, so the box lies in the end padding, the padding's twin, as the leading box is the leading padding's. The run's outer box is its slot again.

## Not read

- **Safari, and the iPhone.** Every WebKit reading is Playwright's WebKit build. Whether a vertical swipe with drift pans a sideways scroller on iOS was not measured.
- **(b) in WebKit by touch.** Playwright's WebKit has no touch-move input. Round 2 asked WebKit for sideways pans by mouse wheel instead.
- **Dark.** The base pass of all 80 routes in Chromium matched light's; the Filters drawer was read in dark in both engines. The other passes, rounds 2 and 3, ran in light, except round 3's reading of atlas's live page and of the Slider, which ran in dark.
- **Consumer pages.** Atlas's drawer and value.js's two LabeledField cases were built from their source on the study's page. Round 3 loaded atlas's live page (atlas 7.0.0 on glass 7.0.0) in Chromium, dark; not atlas's source on 11.1.2, so the gear-then-toggle opener SITES.md gives was read from source and not run. value.js's pages were not loaded. `check-overflow.mjs` is for the sites to run on them.
- **The build targets' scrollbars.** Chrome 120 and Safari 16.4 lack `scrollbar-width`; the strip's `::-webkit-scrollbar` rule for them was not read.
- **v7 by touch in WebKit, and on an iPhone.** v7's touch cells (R4-1, GL-16) are Chromium's. WebKit read v7's sizes, computed `touch-action` and `overflow`, the flag and the wheel; it did not read the side sheet, and it read the header rule in its top-and-bottom form. Safari 16.4's `overflow-y: clip` beside a visible inline axis (R4-3) was not read; Playwright's WebKit computes it.
- **v7's sweep.** The four passes were not rerun on v7; `check-overflow.mjs` read v7's fold and stress pages (SWEEP §8).
- **Not driven:** resizing while a part is open, keyboard scrolling. v7 drove the detented Sheet's drag from its header strips, on a bottom sheet and a right one.
