Three findings shape pass 2.

1. **Every family that held legibility over any ground did it with α at or near the any-input bound, or did not hold it.**
   - A's chrome sits at 0.89/0.86 (S-16: bound 0.871/0.854).
   - B's unpublished surfaces paint 0.894/0.853 (B-proto §3).
   - C's chrome over the field reads effective opacity 0.85-0.88, equal to its own opaque escape at 0.86 (C-crit §5). Its fail-safe default is the same bound (C-crit §6).
   - D solves 0.61-0.94 in light and 0.75-0.96 in dark (D-proto §2).
   - E never needed the plate (E-crit §3.1). F holds only over library substrates (F-crit §5 item 1).

   The bound is the floor, not a family. What differs between routes is **the knowledge that licenses a surface to go thinner than the bound**: a declaration (C), a publication (B), a yielding field (F), a corpus band (D), or a blend that pins lightness without α (G, new). Every critic's gestalt read of the bound is the same: legible because it stopped being glass (A-crit §5, C-crit §5, D-crit §4).
2. **The veil had readers, and no witness read them.** A `--card` pole turned about 20 `var(--glass-plate-*)` state fills and dims into same-hue no-ops or brightenings (A-crit §4.4, B-crit §4.3-4.4, C-proto break 4, D-proto break 2):
   - active pills vanished in A (first pass), B, C (before its cure), E and F;
   - the focus veil brightens instead of dimming in A, B and D;
   - the grasp register (`grasp.css:133`, 0.6 × rest) sheds legibility under every signal-free ladder that was measured: A dock 1.77/1.61, B dock 2.92-3.28, D wash 1.97/1.95 (35 rows).

   These are D-2 regressions the owner sees before any number. The pass-2 battery reads them (§3.3).
3. **Every family's own witness converged partly by construction.**
   - E's M-2 green is the stroke's token pair (E-crit §3.1).
   - C's dead solve passes M-2 unchanged (C-crit §3).
   - D's W-D leg 1 passes hand edits (D-crit §3).
   - A's W-A leg 1 reads a name (A-crit §3).
   - B's W-B reads a mean the solve never uses (B-crit §3).
   - F's W-F leg 2 is blind to a narrow halo on an isolated surface (F-crit §2).
   - M-5's readback half is vacuous for every family that deletes the sampler.

   In pass 2, a witness counts only if it fails under the plant that removes the route's own mechanism (§7.5).

### D3-E · The self-grounded ink: RETIRE, 40%

**Refutation** (E-crit §3, §7), on three counts:
1. **Its green is the instrument's geometry.** With no plate at all (`--glass-veil-base: 0; --glass-veil-step: 0`), M-2 passes 560/560 and M-3 266/266. W-E improves, 2 red rows against the prototype's 4. The stroke was sized to the harness ring (E-res §0.1: "a centred stroke needs 5 px"). One pixel further out (ring R 2) the same captures read 1.25-3.03 on every non-trivial ground.
2. **Its only passing width is the look the owner rejected.** At 5 px the ground reads as sticker lettering in light and as embossed meme type in dark (E-proto B-2, E-crit §3.7). Between 1 and 2 px it reads worse than no stroke, 1.05-1.14 over black (E-res §0.2). Halos are not portable across engines (E-res §0.3).
3. **Inheritance has an open-ended reset list, and one entry cannot exist.** Neither engine honours a stroke property inside `::selection` (E-proto §2.2). 33 text runs per theme on opaque nested hosts, plus 29 inputs, still carry the card stroke (E-crit §3.4).

**A note on WCAG.** A 5 px halo counts as the glyph's background under WCAG's own border rule (W §5), so the retirement does not rest on a WCAG reading. It rests on the gestalt, and on the material carrying no legibility role, which a material family cannot concede.

**Not BLOCK:** no missing primitive would change the outcome. **Not BANK:** the research measured every width from 1 to 6 px and every halo stack.

**Harvest** (E-crit §7), banked in §3.4:
- `saturate(0.6)` at blur ≥ 16 px, the thin-veil route to M-8's chroma half (S-15);
- the opaque `--perimeter-ink` with a card band, one of the three indicator registers (§5 Q-6);
- the 4× `drop-shadow` icon dilation (4.27/4.37 in Chromium, 3.81/3.74 in WebKit), resolved at the element with nothing mounted.

The `--card` frost is D3-A's move, and the duplicate `--neutral-5` declaration goes to FL-11.

## 6 · New family: D3-G · the luminosity pole (hue through, lightness pinned)

**Source.** W §1 finding 2 and §9.2 (probes T8-T8d), Fluent acrylic's luminosity layer (W §3.1), X §0 finding 6 (consumer isolation), A-res §0.8 (the dark card turns red fields brown). No family adopted it (S-21).

**Charter.** A glass surface composes a luminosity layer beneath its content: a pole colour with `mix-blend-mode: luminosity` over the blurred backdrop. The composite takes the pole's luminosity and the backdrop's hue and saturation. So:
- the ink's contrast is fixed by the pole's luminosity on any ground, with no α bound, no declaration and no signal;
- the field's colour carries through the glass, up to the gamut ceiling at the pinned lightness (P §2.3).

Rungs differ by pole luminosity and blur. The rooms (floating, overlay, dialog), where the consumers ask for calm (M-8), stay at the bound (FL-4, FL-8). The content rungs and the dock take the pole (§5 Q-3 option b).

**Centre:** hue-carrying glass with a signal-free legibility floor. **What it would buy over A′:**
- in dark, a plate lifted to L 0.30-0.35 that keeps a red field red where A's card turns it brown (Q-4);
- a dock that follows the field's colour live with no signal, which speaks to D-2 and the retired G2 edict;
- luminance bleed decoupled from blur, which frees F48's subtle radii.

**Measured so far** (W T8b, a standalone page, not the library):
- **Light:** a pole of `rgb(235 232 228)` lands composites at L 0.927-0.948 over orange, ochre, violet, green, black, white and the stripes. Light muted reads 5.14-5.73, and C runs 0.037-0.092 (median).
- **Dark:** a pole of `rgb(52 44 38)` gives L 0.30-0.40 and dark muted 5.29-7.06.
- WebKit agrees within 0.07.

**Failure mode.** The pole goes flat when the plate or an ancestor between plate and field carries `transform`, `isolation`, `z-index`, `opacity < 1`, `filter` or `will-change`, or when the pole is a child of the `backdrop-filter` element. It also goes flat when the ground is a root-propagated background.

Flat means the pole's own colour. On a card-luminosity pole that is A's frosted card: legible, with no hue, and silent (W §9.2). So the failure degrades to the floor's look, not to illegibility. Whether that is E-2-clean is §5 Q-7.

**Known risks, stated before research.**
1. **M-1 caps the light pole.** Over paper the composite takes the pole's luminosity with paper's near-zero saturation, so a light pole under L 0.975 paints exactly O-62's grey. At L ≥ 0.975 the gamut leaves little chroma for red-orange hues. Analytically that is small, but it is unmeasured. The light arm may therefore collapse to A′, and G would be a dark and dock mechanism.
2. **Isolation is the common case.** Portalled overlays carry `z-index` and floating-ui transforms. D2's advancing routes move seats and possibly the plate by transform (D2 REGISTRY §7.1, D2-G). fourier's app root is `isolate`, and value.js's ground is root-propagated (X §0.6), so value.js would get flat poles everywhere without an addendum.
3. **D2's floor row 6** makes `.dock-plate` the one element that composes the plate and carries the dock's one `backdrop-filter`. The pole must be its sibling, not its child, which is a D2 interface.
4. **The Lum formula** (`0.3R + 0.59G + 0.11B` on encoded channels) is not WCAG Y. L spreads by about 0.02 across hues, so a margin is owed.

**Pass-2 questions, in order.** A BLOCK or RETIRE is declared at the first named failure.
