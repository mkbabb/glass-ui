- **H12-1 landed.** Every M-9 veil row now also prints `oklch <cell>: raw #… L C h → veiled #… L C h`. ## 1 · What landed

### 1.1 · `m9-state-registers.mjs`

- **The print.** After H5-1's depth rows, each veil row gets one more line, `oklch <tag>: raw #rrggbb L C h → veiled #rrggbb L C h`. It covers every theme and ground, and a non-gate engine's line is marked `[info]`. The colours are the probe's mean `raw` and `full` pixels, which are the ones M-9 already reads, converted by `lib.mjs lch` (OKLab → L, C, h).
- **The R-32 rows,** for each engine in the gate list or in the capture:
  - `R-32 <tag> (the spot)` covers the dark aurora state cell. It prints raw → veiled (L, C, h), then (i) `|Δh|` (from `lib.mjs hueDiff`) against 3°, (ii) `C_v/C_r = k × raw` against 0.95, and (iii) `ΔL` against 0.
  - `R-32 <tag> (the page)` covers the dark paper state cell. It prints raw → veiled and (iii) only. Hue and chroma are not read there, because at C ≈ 0.003 one 8-bit step moves h by tens of degrees. The R-2 page reads h 68.6° → 107.3° in Chromium, which is noise and says nothing about paint.
  - `R-32 <engine> dark ΔL, reported, never gated: spot … · page … · beside R-2's spot -0.225`. The constant cites REGISTRY P11-0 finding 4 and P11-2.2.
  - A missing spot or page prints `R-32 <engine> dark spot …: not read`. Under `--r32` that line is a FAIL row (`✗`) for a gate engine.
- **`--r32`** turns the rows into gated rows (four per gate engine). Without the flag they print `· REPORTED (gated under --r32)` and add no row to the tally. M-9's verdict on any build is unchanged unless a seat passes the flag. This follows H5-1's `--dim-max` and H5-4's `--q11`: the ruling gates where a seat names it. The minor does not carry R-2 and stays W-H.2's (P11-3.1), so its M-9 reads as before.

## 2 · Readings and departures

### 2.1 · The fixture P11-5 named does not hold, and why

P11-5 (H12-1) and P11-4 (F-1) give frame 1 as `#ec9642 → #99560a`, L 0.746 → 0.521, C 0.142 → 0.118, h 61.6° → 60.4°. Those are the Chromium cells' numbers on the **headless shell's ground** (the aurora's `css` arm), as FV6 measured them in pass 7. Frame 1, the PNG the delegate saw and declined, was banked in **pass 5** on the WebGPU field's ground (`#e87237`), before H6-0. Its pixels read L 0.682 → 0.482, C 0.163 → 0.134, h 44.6° → 44.4° (Chromium), and no reading of the banked PNG reproduces the registry's pair. The FRAME1 record row measures the gap at every run. **No banked pixels of the shell-ground pair exist:** the pass-6 and pass-7 capture directories were in seat scratch, and the only banked state frames in `material/` are `pass-5/frames-H5-1/`.

What this changes:
- **The plant's verdict does not change.** Both grounds read (i) PASS, (ii) FAIL and (iii) PASS, in both engines. (ii) is 0.832 or 0.822 in Chromium and 0.662 in Playwright WebKit, which is lower there: WebKit's GL ground is more saturated, and R-2 takes a larger share of its chroma.
- **For the F-1 frame (owed by the floor v11 seat):** the delegate declined a pass-5, WebGPU-ground frame. The new frame should pair R-2 against R-32 on **one** ground, as `…-veil-r2-vs-r32.png` (P11-4) already implies. Its sheet should print that ground's numbers. If the delegate is shown frame 1 again beside the new arm, the sheet should give frame 1's own `#e87237 → #993f09` and not the shell-ground pair. This is a reading for the agglomeration to place. H12 rules nothing.
- R-2's reference ΔL, −0.225, is the shell-ground figure. M-9's logs print −0.226, the mean over the probe, against −0.2256 from the hexes. The printer keeps −0.225 as the record's constant and prints each engine's measured spot ΔL beside it.

### 2.2 · The plant first written was wrong, and the self-test caught it

The first hue plant turned the arm **+5°**. Turned that way, both spots leave sRGB at the kept chroma. In Playwright WebKit the 8-bit clip pulled the hue back to +1.3° (`#bc4f00`, C 0.158), so the plant read PASS where it should FAIL. That run is kept: `H12-records/selftest-h12-run0-plant-out-of-gamut.log`, 2 BAD. The cure has two parts. The plant now turns **−5°**, which stays in gamut on both grounds. A **GAMUT** row also asserts that every planted colour is inside sRGB before encoding. On its first run GAMUT read 10/12, because the fixture's ΔL −0.12 also left the gamut on the WebKit ground (`#be4c00`, blue clipped). The fixture moved to −0.10. One more run then failed one row, an expectation regex that allowed 0.99x but not 1.001 (8-bit rounding of a kept chroma), and the regex was widened to `0.99x | 1.00x`. Both of those runs were overwritten by `run1` and `run2`, and their verdicts (`1 of 32 expectations missed`, each twice) are this paragraph's record. **The instrument was right in all three.** Each red was a fault in how this seat built the plant, and each fix changed the plant, never the gate.

### 2.3 · The gamut bounds a chroma-keeping dim (for the floor seat and the critic)

With `lib.mjs gamutMaxC` (bisection on OKLCh → linear sRGB), at the spot's own hue:

| ground | L, C, h | largest ΔL that keeps C | largest ΔL that keeps 0.95 C | most of C the gamut allows at ΔL −0.225 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Chromium shell, `#ec9642` | 0.746, 0.142, 61.6° | −0.133 | **−0.163** | 0.849 |
| Playwright WebKit GL, `#e87237` | 0.681, 0.163, 44.8° | −0.114 | **−0.142** | 0.803 |

Read under R-32:
- An arm that passes (ii) in both engines dims the spot by **no more than about 0.14 in L** on these grounds. The bound comes from the capture's 8-bit sRGB, not from a ruling. The spot's ΔL stays reported, and the owner's eye decides it (R-32).
- A uniform "lighten-less" dim chosen on one ground will hit the gamut on a more saturated one. This is the same lesson as Reg10 P5-0 finding 4 (no uniform multiplicative dim is ground-relative), now with a number on it.
- The bound applies to what the probe sees: a veil whose `backdrop-filter` `saturate()` pushes C up runs into the same wall. H12 reads paint and does not prescribe the build. Under R-32 the arm changes R-2 alone (`dark-arm.css:294`, `focus-veil.css:72-74`), with no new layer, no second blur and no glow (O-87).
