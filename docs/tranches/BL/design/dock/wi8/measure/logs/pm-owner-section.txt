## Added 2026-11-22, after the eighth build was measured

The eighth build carries 11.1.0's dock inside the redesign. It was measured whole on one build, in Chromium and in Safari's engine (Playwright WebKit; real Safari is still unread): every page and every travel, the older releases' tests, and a fault planted under each test to show the test can fail (`MEASURE-8.md`, part M). The folds, the layer changes, going back mid-change, reduced motion, the overlays and the controls dock pass in both engines. It isn't ready to become 11.2.0. These are what stand in the way:

- **A press on one of the dock's always-shown buttons, then a move off the dock, closes it at once.** 11.1.0 does this on purpose, and the merge brought it in. The redesign's own tests expect the dock to stay for its usual pause, as it does after a press on any other button. One of the two has to give.
- **Some of the older releases' tests fail where 11.1.0 passes them.** Most read the round end's corner as a number, and Chromium writes that number in a form the tests don't accept; the corner on screen is the same. The others are 11.0.2's tests of the old button fills, of the content's box during a fold, of the rail's opening, and of how many layouts an idle fold costs.
- **The dock's code is 505 lines over its budget**: 5,246 lines against 11.1.0's 4,741. Nothing has been cut; `MEASURE-8.md` lists what each block buys.
- **A page that uses the dock loads 2.6 % more** than on 11.0.2 (105,249 bytes compressed against 102,631), where the budget is no growth.
- **In dark, the selected page's name is under the readable floor** on the warm figure page (3.3 to 1 in Chromium and 4.0 in Safari's engine, against 4.5). On a few docks the current and hovered highlights read fainter than today's (questions 18 and 21).
- **Tab buttons are 38.6 px tall** where every other button in a dock is 40 (38 against 44 on a phone).
- **Opening a dock costs a few more layout passes than allowed** on its first frame, and the backdrop's colour sample forces more in the frames after.
- **The dark firmness.** The rule that picks it chooses the second of five steps on this build, and the build carries the first. Neither keeps the selected page's name readable on the warm page in dark (question 10).

Seven of your questions have strips from this build. Each has a default, and the work goes on with it unless you say otherwise.

10. **A firmer dock.** On this build no step of firmness both hides as much of the dark grid page as the light dock hides of the white one and stays clear of the near-solid slab. So the default for dark is the firmest step that stays glass: the second of five. Light keeps the first. The strips show today's dock and the five firmnesses, dark then light, a page dock open and a round dock folded and open, on the dark grid page, the warm figure page and daraksha's ground. Beside each frame: how far the dock stands off the page, and how much of the grid shows through it.
