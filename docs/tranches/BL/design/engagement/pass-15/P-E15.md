**Why a level change at the transformed link needs no paint.** A layout move of a box at level i, by Δᵢ in its parent's frame, moves its painted image by L₍>i₎ · Δᵢ, where L₍>i₎ is the linear part of every transform applied to boxes above level i (the box's own transform is applied about its own box, so its own layout move is a pure translation of everything it paints). The host's compensating `translate` t paints as J · t, where J = z_host · Π L over every carrier between the host and the root, the host's own scale excluded (`translate` composes outside it). For an affine map the compensation is exact and needs no paint read:

> **t = −J⁻¹ · Σᵢ L₍>i₎ · Δᵢ**, over the levels that changed.

Below the nearest transformed link every carrier is above the level, so L₍>i₎ = J / z_host and t = −Δᵢ / z_host: cand14's −Δ, with Δ in root px (§S15-b). At or above it, each level's change is carried through the carriers above that level only (Ch-F11's lead, in the form that is exact: "J · Δ is exact only below the nearest transformed link", S14 b.1 step 4, because above it the maps differ; L₍>i₎ is that difference). **No term holds the carrier's frame-to-frame motion**: Δᵢ is a layout difference, and L₍>i₎ is the carriers' linear part at this frame's one animation time. ~~A carrier written every frame, by any writer, enters only as its current linear map, so its acceleration cannot reach the glide.~~ **(Folded, F-5.)** The act's frame is exact. The glide is a `translate` on the host, so on each later frame it paints through J(τ), while the step it answers paints through L₍>i₎(τ). Below the nearest transformed link the two maps are one map (L₍>i₎ = J / z_host), and the glide stays on the step's own path. At or above it they are different maps, so a glide written once leaves that path whenever a carrier between the step's level and the host changes during the glide. X9 reads 10.00-90.00 px off for `scale` 0.5 / 0.25 / 0.1 → 1 and 15.31 px sideways for `rotate` 0 → 90°. So rule 4 below re-derives the glide on each frame a carrier's computed map changes. With it, the glide paints the step's own path under the carriers' current map, and no carrier motion enters it.

**The map, read from computed values.** On a step frame only, for each link that carries a transform-family value, its linear part is `rotate` · `scale` · the linear part of `transform` (the resolved `matrix()`), in that order (`translate` has none). K2 reads z_host · Π L against the engine's own three-probe map (`mapAt`'s form) on 14 carrier kinds: **max |difference| 0.00000 in every cell, Chromium**, including `translate: -50% -50%` with `rotate: 30deg` and `transform: scale(1.2)`, `transform: rotate(10deg) scale(0.8) skewX(15deg)`, a `matrix()`, three nested carriers, `rotate3d(0,0,1,20deg)`, `rotateX(40deg)` flattened (no perspective), a zoomed carrier (`zoom: 1.25` with `rotate`, J = 1.2310) and the host's own `scale: 1.06` (excluded, J = I). **WebKit reads the same 14 maps, max |difference| 0.00000 in every cell** (`facts15-webkit.log`).

**The rule, amended (S14 b.1 step 4):**
1. **Affine, on the chain (the default):** every level that changed, at any depth, is mapped by the formula above, with L read from computed values on that frame. No probe. The measure's resolution is the chain's, one layout px × the link's zoom (§S15-b).
2. **On paint (kept, narrowed; five routes as folded):** (i) a step on a level with a **projective** carrier above it (a `perspective` property or function, or a 3D transform under a perspective: the resolved matrix's `m14`, `m24` or `m34` non-zero; K2 flags it); (ii) a level across a carrier the chain cannot see (§S15-h A1, with the detector of F-2); (iii) an `offset-path` carrier (its rotation follows the path's tangent, which no computed value states); **(iv) (F-3) a level with a link at or above it that takes part in a 3D rendering context**, meaning its own or its parent's computed `transform-style` is `preserve-3d`. There the 4 × 4 matrices compose before they are flattened, and every link prints m14 = m24 = m34 = 0, so route (i) cannot see it. X3 reads the Π of flattened parts off by up to 0.75 in a map entry, and a rule-1 step off by 36.52 px and 14.53 px. Where a grouping property flattens the used value, the route still fires; paint is exact there and only costs more; **(v) (F-4) a zoomed level**: its link's offsetParent has an effective zoom (`currentCSSZoom`) ≠ 1, or the link's own effective zoom changed across the frame (§S15-b). Newton on paint as S14 states, with M2's and M6's limits; M6's trigger narrows to these five. **On every paint route the step is the displacement of the host's reference point, not its centre (F-8):** the alignment point of §S15-h A3, taken in each frame's own box and mapped by that frame's probe map, s = P°₋₁(ref₋₁) − P°₀(ref₀).
