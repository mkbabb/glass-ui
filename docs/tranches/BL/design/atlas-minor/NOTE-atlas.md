# To atlas: the data table and the data view's dialog

Ready to send.

---

**What you asked for.** Your list of November 23 asked for four things in the data view before your December 9 build: a header and a first column that stay put inside the table's own scroller, a way to fit the 2,911 px table into a 1,202 px region at 1440 (and 344 px at 390) without sideways scrolling, a caption and a row-header column, and a dialog that grows out of the control that opened it and goes back to it. All four are built against glass 11.1.1 and are going through review now. They ship in the first minor release after 11.1.2. I'll tell you its number as soon as it's fixed, and whether it lands before your build.

**What I measured.** I measured with a test page that holds a 2,897 px, 20-column, 120-row DataTable in a 1,202 px region (1440) and a 344 px region (390), as your data view does, and a dialog the size of yours. I read it in Playwright's Chromium and in Playwright's WebKit, which is not Safari, on glass 11.1.1 and on the new build. No phone was measured.
- **The header.** On 11.1.1 the table's container grows to the full height of its rows inside your region, so your region is what scrolls, and the header scrolls away with the rows (1,500 px up after a 1,500 px wheel). The new DataTable scrolls its own rows. After the same wheel and 800 px sideways, the header row and the first column sit at the region's top and left edges to the pixel, in both engines and at both widths. The region and the page don't move.
- **Narrow.** Under a 640 px container the rows become cards, which now read to a screen reader as the same table. In Chromium's accessibility tree the cards are a `grid` (or a `table`) named by the caption, with a header row of five column headers and one row per card, whose first cell is a row header. WebKit's ARIA snapshot reads the same tree. On 11.1.1 Chromium left the cards out of the tree's table structure altogether, and WebKit read them as loose text with term and definition pairs.
- **Virtualized cards.** With your own `useVirtualWindow` (copied with only its import paths changed and the gap in step 5 below), 300 cards of 20 columns in a 344 × 600 region keep the full scroll range, 32,850 px, and a 4,000 px wheel lands at 4,000 with five cards mounted and none overlapping, in both engines.
- **Wide.** With `fit`, the test table shows the lead column and the six columns that fit the 1,202 px region, reports the other 13, and has no sideways scroll left.
- **The dialog.** Opened from a 160 × 40 button, the plate's first frame is exactly the button's box at zero opacity. It grows to its place with one rebound: about 15 px on a 512 px plate, and about 46 px of width and 33 px of height on a plate your size. On close it shrinks back without overshoot, and its last frame before it leaves is under 5 % of the way from the button's box, at the same low opacity. Under reduced motion it fades in and out where it rests, without moving or scaling.
- **A slow first open.** Your data view's first open loads the browser and walks every row, which holds the page for a moment while the dialog is growing. I measured that case on a plate your size: a body that arrives 100 ms after the click and runs a 90 ms task, plus a 120 ms stall later in the opening and another in the close. glass's springs now advance by at most one 60 Hz frame per frame, so a stall slows the motion instead of skipping part of it. No step between two painted frames is larger than the spring's own step on a smooth frame (about 82 px at its fastest), in both engines. The cost is time: on a busy machine painting 15 to 20 frames a second, the opening took 3.3 s instead of 0.8 s.
- **No opener.** Opened with no origin, as a shared link opens it, the dialog blooms in place as it does today.

**What I'd ask atlas to change once you take the release.** All of this is in `SourceDataBrowser.vue`, `DataDialog.vue` and your doors.
1. **Let the table own the scroller.** Remove the `.source-browser__viewport` wrapper and its rules (the overflow, the block sizes, the `overflow: visible` overrides), and the `.data-dialog__body .source-browser__viewport` rule. The DataTable becomes the browser's third child, so set `.data-dialog__body { grid-template-rows: auto auto minmax(0, 1fr) }`. That gives the table the body's free height (today's four tracks leave the last one empty). Name the table with `aria-label`, or with the new `#caption` slot if you'd like the name visible.
2. **Point your virtualizer at glass's scroller.** Pass `:viewport-ref="(el) => (viewport = el)"`, and `useVirtualWindow` reads the element that now scrolls the rows. It's handed `null` when the table goes away.
3. **Move your column widths into the columns.** Give the lead column `width: "var(--source-lead-col, 16rem)"` and each other column `` width: `max(9rem, calc(${figureCh}ch + 1.5rem + 1px))` ``, and drop the computed table width and the first-column width rules. Mark the first column `rowHeader: true`, so each row is announced by its district's name.
4. **Turn on `fit`.** Listen to `@fold`. It hands you the columns that don't fit, and you can offer a column picker (reorder `columns`, and the table keeps the leading ones) and a row disclosure in the `#row-actions` slot that lists a row's folded values. `fit` counts each column at its `width`, so it holds as long as every cell stays inside its width, which your fixed layout and ellipsis already ensure.
5. **Turn the cards back on.** Drop `:responsive="false"` and keep `role="grid"`, and the cards keep the grid. To virtualize them, give `.data-table-card-list` your total size (`position: relative; block-size: var(--source-total-size)`), place each card absolutely (`position: absolute; inset-block-start: 0; inline-size: 100%`) with the same `getRowAttrs` transform as a row, and pass each card to `observe` through `rowRef`. Placed that way the cards lose the list's gap and touch, so add your own space between them: in the test page I added 8 px to each measured size inside `useVirtualWindow`, which is three lines.
6. **Keep one rule of your own while your rows are absolutely placed.** Your layout turns the `thead` and each row into its own table box, and a header cell can't stick past a `thead` that is its own table. So keep your rule that makes the `thead` itself sticky. The first column sticks without it.
7. **Grow the dialog from the door that opened it.** Pass `Dialog` an `:origin`, and make it the element the reader clicked, not `document.activeElement`. On a page loaded with `?browse=` already in the address, nothing has focus, so `activeElement` is the page's body, and the dialog would grow from the whole page and shrink back to it. Safari also doesn't focus a button when it's clicked. Your doors push `?browse=` without handing over their element today (`openSourceData` in `useVizPlate.ts` and `ChapterStage.vue`, a row's View data through `openFigureRows`, and the place sheet's View data and hops through `openDatasetView`). One way: a `shallowRef` beside `registerDataView` in `data-view.ts` that each door sets to `event.currentTarget` as it pushes, and that `DataDialog` takes and clears when the view opens. A view opened with no click (a shared link, Back or Forward) finds it empty and passes `null`, and the dialog blooms in place. The same element can serve your focus return. The phone's Sheet ignores `origin`, so 390 is unchanged.

**What I'd like back.** Once your data view runs on the release, please send me two readings: at 1440, the number of columns `fit` shows for your widest dashboard, and at 390, what VoiceOver says for the first card. I'll check both against these numbers. If the column picker or the row disclosure is awkward to build on what's here, tell me which part, and I'll look at moving it into glass. And if the dialog's rebound reads as a wobble on your plate, say so, and I'll look at a calmer spring for plates that size.
