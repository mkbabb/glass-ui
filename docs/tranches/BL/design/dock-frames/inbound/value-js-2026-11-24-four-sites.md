## Item 15 (2026-11-24, from daraksha's repair seat via value.js): a dock rule restyles the whole page on every insertion

`:empty` inside `:has()` can't be invalidated by features, so any node inserted or removed anywhere in the document schedules the `:has` invalidation set on the root. Where the set's descendant part ends in a universal `> *`, the whole subtree is invalidated, and the next style read restyles every element. Chromium's trace, with invalidation tracking, logs "Invalidation set invalidates subtree | .glass-dock.collapsed:has(.dock-layer--summary:empty) .dock-persistent > *". On daraksha's reader and Home it fires 16 to 30 times in a few seconds of ordinary use, at 8 to 30 ms each, and more under a 4x CPU throttle. The reader's Verses-to-Focus frame takes two forced restyles of about 1,150 elements each: 112.5 against 83.5 ms (median of 4).

**Read on release/11.1.1.** Seven rules in `dock/styles/morph.css` key on the summary's emptiness through `:has()`: `:187` (`:not(:empty)`), `:277`, `:280`, `:285`, `:302`, `:306` (`.dock-persistent > *`) and `:311`. The dock-frames cure touches none of them.

**Placed: add, 11.1.2.** GlassDock renders the summary, so it knows when the summary is empty. It stamps that on its root, as `data-summary="empty"` or `"filled"`, and stamps `data-persistent` when it has persistent seats. The seven rules read those attributes in place of `:has()`. The challenge also sweeps `src/` for any other `:has()` whose argument features can't invalidate (`:empty` and structural pseudo-classes) or whose subject part ends in a universal, and cures each the same way. Witness: a Chromium trace with invalidation tracking shows no subtree invalidation from a dock rule on a DOM insertion. The collapsed dock's geometry is unchanged, to 0 px, in both engines: summary empty or filled, horizontal and vertical, pill and rounded.
