## The ruling this executes

Six stability rounds (7-12) plateaued at 9-13 material findings and never converged. The mechanism
was not corpus decay — it was the corpus's own shape: **derived data was duplicated.** Wave counts,
per-band roster tables, phase enumerations and the standing-gate arithmetic were copied into
`PLAN.md`, `APOTHEOSIS.md`, the execution cursor and the band files. Every round that chartered a
wave left three or four copies stale, and the next round's critics correctly reported them — the
cures manufactured the next round's findings. An unbounded adversarial loop over duplicated derived
data cannot converge by construction.

The cure is structural. The **SUPERSESSION LAW** already makes each band file's roster THE spec of
record, so every duplicate outside it was non-authoritative **by our own law**. This pass struck the
duplicates and cited the source. The finding class is retired, not re-synchronised.

**The three rules applied:**

1. A **roster** is stated once, in the file that owns it. Everywhere else: a pointer.
2. A **count** derived from a roster is not stated outside that roster at all. The corpus keeps no
   band totals and no tranche total — a total is what goes stale the instant a wave is chartered.
3. An **acceptance figure** (the gate keep-band, the ≤60 ceiling) is stated **once**, at a site
   named authoritative in its own text, and every other site points at it.

**What was deliberately NOT struck:** DAG edges, ordering laws, same-cut laws, one-owner-per-file
seams, user fences, and the dated correction brackets of files whose text survives. Edges are real
content; only counts and restated rosters went.
