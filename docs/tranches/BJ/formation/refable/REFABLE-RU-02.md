generation rate are equal** — and the only thing that generates findings at the same rate you cure
them is the curing.

## 4. The convergence-mechanism finding

> **An unbounded adversarial loop over duplicated derived data cannot converge by construction.**

The lead's structural ruling, which the de-duplication pass executed and which this seat's evidence
independently supports. Stated as a mechanism:

1. **Derived data was duplicated.** Wave counts, per-band rosters, phase enumerations and the
   standing-gate arithmetic were copied into `PLAN.md`, `APOTHEOSIS.md`, the execution cursor and the
   band files — four to five copies of facts owned by one file.
2. **Every cure mutated the corpus.** A stability round that charters a wave, corrects a figure or
   re-seats a phase changes a roster. That is what a cure IS.
3. **Every mutation staled the copies.** One mint left three or four sites wrong — not eventually,
   immediately, and without contradicting anything locally readable.
4. **The next round's critics correctly reported them.** They were not wrong. The findings were real.
5. **So the cure rate equals the generation rate,** and the finding count goes flat. Exactly the
   table in §1.

The corpus was in a stable equilibrium: every round removed ~12 findings and authored ~12. Six more
rounds would have produced six more plateaus, which is why the chain closed by adjudication rather
than by exhaustion. **The loop was not failing to find the truth. It was manufacturing its own
subject matter.**

### 4.1 The sharper form — what STAB1-6's convergence actually measured

This is the finding the redo makes that neither chain could make alone, and it is uncomfortable.

STAB1-6 converged. But look at how STAB5 justified its clean, in its own method section:

> "nothing in the corpus has changed since (verified: `git log e56517b1..HEAD -- waves/ PLAN.md
> ASK.md ASK-REDUCTION.md formation/` is **EMPTY**)"

STAB5's clean was purchased by **stasis**, not by structure. The duplication disease was already
fully present in that corpus — STAB2 and STAB4 both describe walking `PLAN` §2 roster bullets
against their band files, which is copy-checking. The copies matched because nothing was moving.
The moment the REFABLE campaign rewrote all nine band files and began minting waves, the same
corpus with the same disease produced 75 findings in six rounds.

**So a two-consecutive-clean over duplicated derived data measures whether the corpus is currently
frozen, not whether it is sound.** It is a valid gate only under a freeze — and a formation chain
that is still chartering waves is by definition not frozen. STAB1-6's convergence and STAB7-12's
plateau are the SAME corpus property observed under two different rates of change.

### 4.2 Why the cure had to be structural

The SUPERSESSION LAW already made every duplicate non-authoritative **by the corpus's own law** —
each band file's roster IS the spec of record. So every copy outside a band file was, formally,
already not a source. Twelve rounds of critics kept reporting drift between an authoritative source
and a text the corpus's own law said was not one, and twelve rounds of fixers kept re-synchronising
them. Nobody applied the law they had.

The de-duplication pass applied it: 35 duplicates struck, 35 pointers written, zero bare deletions,
four sites named authoritative in their own text. **The finding class is retired, not
re-synchronised.** A future critic reporting "PLAN says N, the band file says M" has nothing to
report, because PLAN no longer says N.

That is the difference between curing instances and removing a generator, and it is the whole
lesson.

### 4.3 The generalization this seat adds

The RU-01 SCRUTINY pass found the same disease one level down, and found it because the cure
triggered an instance of it (`REFABLE-RU-01.md` N-1/N-2, routing R-7):

**A line number in another file is derived data too.** It is a count of newlines. It goes stale on
any edit above it — silently, without contradicting anything, exactly like a copied wave count.
Striking ~100 lines of duplicated roster out of `PLAN.md` invalidated every external `PLAN.md:NNN`
cite below the cut, including two inside the FROZEN owner ruling surface that cannot be repaired at
their own end.

The corpus had already invented the local cure without generalizing it — `waves/BAND-STORY.md`'s
header binds inbound cites to "SECTION + GATE ID (the gate IDs are stable)". It is now `PLAN` §2's
anchor law: cross-file references anchor to a section plus a stable id, never a line number.

**The pattern worth carrying out of this campaign:** every time this corpus converged on something,
it was by making a class of statement structurally impossible to get wrong — not by getting it
right. Counts: deleted, not synchronised. Rosters: single-sited, not cross-checked. Scheduling:
membership-based, not range-based, so a minted wave is scheduled with no cursor edit and therefore
no cursor edit to forget. Anchors: section+id, not line. Each cure removes a generator. None of them
is a fix.
