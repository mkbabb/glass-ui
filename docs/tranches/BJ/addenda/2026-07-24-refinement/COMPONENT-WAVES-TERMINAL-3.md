## 3 · §THE DESIGN

### 3.1 The frame law — one constant, derived, not minted

> **The plot frame never depends on the curve.** `viewBox = "-0.1 -0.1 1.2 1.2"` — square, constant, both modes, every preset, every panel, every frame of a drag.

The bleed **0.1 is the shipped `VIEW_PAD`** (`constants.ts:32`) and it is the *tight* bound: executed over all 30 `bezierPresets` this seat, the curves span `y ∈ [−0.0969, +1.0927]` — 3% slack, **no value minted**. Six defects fall out of one declaration:

- `preserveAspectRatio` letterboxes **zero px** (viewBox aspect 1 ≡ element aspect 1) ⇒ E1(b)'s 43px and 36px dead bands go to zero and `aspect-ratio: 1` becomes **true** instead of dead.
- Two plots are comparable **by construction**; the blurb stops lying.
- `getScreenCTM()` is invariant during a drag ⇒ the handle tracks the pointer 1:1.
- `viewBox`, `canvasViewBox`, `VIEWBOX_FIT_SAMPLES` and the 17 Newton solves per drag frame all die.
- The `<text font-size="0.05px">` axis labels die outright — the frame rect and the readout already carry 0 and 1, and at 10.85/11.82px they straddle the −4 rung (11.50) without landing on it.
- Every remaining magnitude can be stated in CSS px (§3.4), which is what makes E6 and E7 assertable at all.

**Handles outside the frame pin to it, and the pin is the default.** `MAX_OVERSHOOT` stays **0.6** — the authorable band is unchanged, no preset breaks, value.js untouched — so control points reach `y ±0.55` on shipped presets and `±0.6` under authoring. `DEFAULT_BEZIER_PRESET = "ease-out-back"` has `y1 = 1.56`, so **one handle is pinned at rest on mount, by design**: the affordance is demonstrated on arrival, not hidden. The handle slides along **its own leader** to the frame edge while its value keeps going — the grammar the shipped `Slider` uses at its rail. The pointer→value map stays **linear and unclamped in y**, so pushing further keeps changing the value and pulling back un-pins the handle **exactly** at the frame edge (handle ≡ pointer at that instant). `aria-valuetext` and the literal carry the number. *(Rejected: growing the frame to hold the control points — it shrinks every curve by 32%; §7.)*

### 3.3 The page — one editor, two strokes, one catalogue, zero residue

F31 asks why there is bottom padding. The answer is that a fixed-height square plot stands beside a short control stack in a two-column split, and **a residue is irreducible in that shape**. So the shape goes.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                        │
│      ONE square plot, both curves      │   active stroke at full ink
│      (frame −0.1 −0.1 1.2 1.2)         │   ghost stroke at 0.30
│                                        │
├────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [bezier|steps] [preset ▾] ⌜cubic-bezier(…)⌝⧉ ▶ │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **One `EasingCurve`, two curves overplotted.** The section's own claim is *comparison*; a comparison is one frame with two strokes at one scale, which constant frames make free. The segmented control selects which stroke the handles edit; the other stays a ghost. Two panels become one, so **E4's horizontal grouped-join has no trigger left** and E3's void has nowhere to be. **The blurb re-writes** to name overplotting — "side by side" was describing the defect.
- **A second, read-only section: the catalogue.** An auto-fit cel field of `EasingCurve` thumbnails, one per named curve; the seat wraps each in a `<button>` that loads it into the editor. This gives the display unit a real second site, gives the page the protagonist BD R7 asked for in a form that is not moot, and is the exact artefact value.js (`easingCatalogue.ts`) and keyframes.js (`NAMED_EASING_BEZIER`) each re-derived. It is a strip of published units, **not** BD R6's hand-rolled `.curve-chip` rack.
- **One control row, ~40px, height-invariant.** The literal stops being `<code>` + a separate icon button and becomes **one mono chip that IS the copy control** — killing the second button register (E14), the nameless tab stop (E15), and reducing the literal to **exactly one print per editor**.
- **No plate, no cap, no query.** The two `glass-card`s are struck (E11). The canvas is a **recessed well** on the shipped `.glass-track-well` register (`styles/glass/track-well.css`, "the ONE shared track-WELL register") — `PROPORTION A10`: a plot is a surface you look *into*. The primitive publishes **no `block-size`, no maximum, no `container-type`**: it is inline-size driven with a now-true `aspect-ratio: 1`, and **the seat sets the width** — the demo section at `min(100%, var(--measure-wide))` (LAYOUT's derived φ·cel), value.js at the `min(100%, 19rem)` it already writes. This is what retires its three `!important`s: there is no inline style left to fight.
- **`EasingPicker.vue:327`'s `lg:` two-column split is deleted, not re-keyed.** One column at every width; the control row wraps. LAYOUT kill #14 loses this term (§9).
- **Props 7 → 4** (plus the v-model): `initial?: EasingSeed` (one **reactive, watched** seed — `{ mode?, preset?, points?, steps?, term? }` — replacing four half-controlled props and retiring both consumers' `:key` remounts) · `readout?: boolean` (kept: value.js sets it false) · `playback?: boolean` (all three consumers set it false) · `label?: string`.
- **The Custom preset becomes real:** one `<SelectItem value="custom" v-if="preset === CUSTOM_PRESET">Custom</SelectItem>`. E8 closes in one line.
- **The staircase is drawn analytically** — ≤ 2n+1 ≤ 25 commands, exact risers and treads; `STEP_PLOT_SAMPLES` dies.
