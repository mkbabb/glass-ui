## 3. Artifact identity and MIME audit

The current ignored evidence tree contains 664 files:

- 625 paths end in `.png`; **625/625 have `image/jpeg` magic**;
- 39 paths end in `.jpg`; 39/39 have `image/jpeg` magic;
- 641 unique SHA-256 values exist across the 664 paths;
- 14 hash groups contain duplicates, accounting for 23 redundant paths beyond
  the first byte identity.

These counts describe the evidence tree at this pass, including receipts added
after C48; they are not retroactively a C48 archive manifest. The entire image
tree is ignored by Git (`*.png` and `*.jpg`), so path presence is mutable. A
receipt's full SHA protects a named observation only when the consumer
re-checks it. Commit/tree identity does not freeze the image bytes.

Proof consequences:

1. A `.png` suffix is an opaque historical filename. It may not be described as
   PNG or lossless without MIME/magic verification.
2. C53's truthful `.jpg` copies and same-byte `.png` aliases are one observation
   each. The alias earns zero second credit.
3. More generally, same-hash paths are one pixel state. Distinct timestamps may
   still prove a dwell/no-change interval when their capture event/timing
   receipt is credible, but they are not multiple non-degenerate motion frames.
4. Byte equality across assay families neither proves nor disproves independent
   invocation. It proves only identical retained pixels. Independence requires
   run/session metadata and an immutable served identity.
5. DPR-3 mobile files inspected here are 390 × 844 JPEGs, not 1170 × 2532
   lossless rasters. They are CSS-pixel discovery witnesses, not native-DPR
   glyph-edge, subpixel-rim, blur-kernel, or refraction acceptance.
6. Concatenated previews, contact sheets, DOM snapshots, and stable endpoints
   cannot support frame-local pixel claims. C57's one-file-at-a-time law is
   binding.

Historical image names need not be rewritten. A future immutable evidence
