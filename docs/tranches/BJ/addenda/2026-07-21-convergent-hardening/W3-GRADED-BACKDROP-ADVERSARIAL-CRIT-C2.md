## 3. Ranked findings

### F1 — CRITICAL — ADOPT is issued without the evidence the owning judgment gate requires

The design report explicitly records zero live Safari/Chromium acceptance and no DesignSync surface,
yet promotes H-C from a hypothesis to a binding ADOPT/REDRESS ruling. BAND-MATERIAL W3 defines the
load-bearing obligation as the real graded/flat/F49-F50 comparison on both target engines. ASK-26 calls
this a paint-taste ruling, and PLAN places MATERIAL W3 in the hallmark design lane. Still inspection
and source reasoning can reject H-A and constrain a prototype; they cannot establish that H-C is
effective, restrained, cheaper, or more readable than H-D.

**Decision:** reject the ADOPT disposition. The absence of required evidence weighs against retaining
an already-frozen public experiment; it does not grant an implementation attempt by default.

**Cure:** record DECLINE. Preserve the W3 report as a rejected design study. Do not implement H-C and
then ask browser evidence to decide whether its public API should have existed.

### F2 — CRITICAL — W3 contradicts W2 on PRM and on private stage-role ownership

W2's binding ruling is exact: the immersive stage sample is a private 14px role multiplied by
`--glass-level`, and PRM degrades `immersive` to `dim`, so the immersive attribute and fixed sample are
absent. W3 then makes the public `--glass-halo-blur` default alias that private stage radius while also
ruling that the static apron may remain under PRM.

Those statements cannot all hold:

1. If the apron consumes the W2 stage effect, it is absent when W2 resolves the stage to `dim`.
2. If the apron remains under PRM, it is a distinct W3 material role and cannot claim W2's private
   stage semantics or value ownership.
3. A public token whose default resolves through a private component-scoped token is not a stable
   public contract. Promoting the 14px source to a shared public token would mint a new primitive;
   copying 14px would create the second canon W3 expressly forbids.

The pinned source demonstrates why this matters: `resolvedStage` degrades `immersive` to `dim`, but
`backdrop` is independently forwarded to `ModalOverlay`; a graded halo can therefore remain even when
the immersive stage marker is absent. W3 ratifies that split while describing the halo as the W2
stage-effect consumer.

**Decision:** reject the W2 alias and the W3 PRM posture. This is not Luna-level naming or tuning.

**Cure:** DECLINE removes the conflict. Row 93 consumes W2's private 14px × `--glass-level` effect and
inherits W2's PRM absence unchanged.

### F3 — CRITICAL — the proposed mask is mathematically not radius- or surface-concentric

W3 retains the x/y product:

```text
m(x,y) = s_x(x) * s_y(y)
```

That gives a rectangular hold with square corner lobes, followed by pinched diagonal falloff. Where an
axis ramp is 0.5, a diagonal crossing at the same two normalized distances is `0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25`.
Equal-alpha contours are product contours, not offsets of the Dialog's rounded border. Within the hold,
pixels outside the actual rounded corner but inside both axis holds remain fully opaque. No use of
`--radius-dialog` in the clearance value changes this geometry.

The design report's claim that the product yields a “soft rounded pool concentric with the modal
radius” is therefore mechanically false. A true rounded-rectangle offset requires signed-distance or
equivalent corner-mask geometry keyed to the actual corner radii. That would be a materially different
mask, not a relationship-test rewrite.

**Decision:** reject the static axis product as the selected surface-concentric mechanism.

**Cure:** none inside bounded H-C REDRESS. Either mint and prove a different corner-aware mechanism
through a new Sol design ruling, or DECLINE. This critic selects DECLINE.

### F4 — CRITICAL — `--glass-halo-core: var(--radius-dialog)` is public-to-public but semantically invalid

The exact W1 nuance matters. `--radius-dialog` is not a private token: it is an `@theme` semantic role
and W1 requires the public radius inventory and migration ledger to account for it. The dependency is
therefore not invalid merely because of visibility. It is invalid because the two override semantics
are independent:

- `--radius-dialog` controls corner silhouette;
- `--glass-halo-core` would control material clearance outside the surface before falloff.

A consumer that squares or enlarges Dialog corners would silently collapse or expand the optical
clearance. A 4px corner does not imply a 4px focus margin; a 24px corner does not prove a 24px margin.
The default numerical coincidence at 16px is not a semantic alias. W1's radius owner has no authority
over W3's external material reach.

There is a second public-truth problem at the pin: the three `--glass-halo-*` names are described as
public, but `src/styles/tokens/manifest.ts` does not enumerate them in the material domain. ADOPT would
owe an explicit manifest/public-ledger decision, not only new comments.

**Decision:** reject the radius alias. Do not modify W1's radius contract to rescue W3.

**Cure:** DECLINE removes the public cohort. If a future proposal returns, clearance must own its own
semantics and migration surface; it cannot borrow radius ownership to avoid admitting a token.

### F5 — HIGH — a single apron can avoid the host's filter once, but the no-double claim fails for nesting

There is one narrow feasible claim worth banking as research only: for one settled Dialog, disabling
the host's `.glass-floating` `backdrop-filter` while one apron filters beneath it can leave the host's
background, border, rim, and shadow intact. That avoids two CSS filter declarations on the core ray.
It does not by itself prove equivalent transmission or readability, but it is mechanically plausible.

The report overextends that local truth:

- An inner Dialog apron samples a backdrop that can already include the outer Dialog apron. At an
  overlapping coordinate, two instance-owned filters are intentionally active along one visual ray.
- The report requires nested Dialogs and also says no ready coordinate reports two backdrop-filter
  owners. Those requirements conflict unless opening the inner suppresses or freezes the outer apron,
  a policy the design does not specify.
- The current instance context registers wrapper and scrim only. Nested Dialogs can resolve the same
  page wrapper, so the existing stage attributes are not reference-counted instance state even if
  apron geometry itself is instance-owned.
- H-D is described as “one flat sample,” but the flat W2 scrim and the Dialog body's floating sample
  are two intended visual bodies and can compound at the Dialog. DECLINE wins on simplicity and closed
  ownership, not because it magically provides one filter per pixel.

**Decision:** accept only the single-settled-Dialog feasibility; reject the global no-double claim and
the nested contract.

**Cure:** row 93 must state the honest flat posture: one W2 stage sample plus the ordinary Dialog body
sample, with nested cost/readability measured rather than renamed away. No per-pixel one-sample canon is
claimed.

### F6 — HIGH — the no-zero/no-double-frame handoff is neither specified atomically nor mutation-testable

At the pin, `DialogOverlay` and `DialogContent` are portal siblings. There is no common DOM owner on
which one readiness attribute can simultaneously switch the apron and the host filter. H-C requires
style changes on at least two elements or a structural portal wrapper that the design does not specify.

Synchronous JavaScript writes in one task may normally coalesce before paint, but the proposed W3-R4
mutation—reverse the order of “hide one / enable the other”—does not prove a painted gap or overlap.
Browsers do not paint between ordinary synchronous writes merely because source order changed. A DOM
observer that detects write order can RED while pixels remain correct; a computed-style read after the
task can GREEN while an asynchronous compositor presentation still glitches. Conversely, forcing a
style read between writes would create a synthetic failure not present in the intended path.

The promise becomes harder during first portal mount, rAF-batched resize delivery, close/reopen, and
nested instances. “One style commit” is an aspiration, not a named mechanism.

**Decision:** reject W3-R4 as a valid born-RED detector and reject the absolute no-frame claim.

**Cure:** none within the selected design. A future mechanism would need one instance-owned state locus
whose selector controls both bodies, plus retained presented-frame tracing under forced long tasks and
reopen. Even then the lawful claim is “no observed presented frame,” not mathematical impossibility.

### F7 — HIGH — the veil equations understate effective near-field ink and body attenuation

The report defines a far veil alpha `a_f` and a local apron ink alpha `a_n`, then binds only:

```text
0 < a_f < a_n <= a_flat
```

For a locally flat source, compositing local ink over the far veil yields effective near ink:

```text
a_effective = a_f + (1 - a_f) * a_n
```

Therefore `a_n <= a_flat` does not ensure the local result is no darker than the flat scrim. The
required relation would constrain `a_effective`, not the child alpha. The Dialog body's own 0.68-class
warm plate then attenuates the already veiled/blurred field again. Retaining the tint and rim is easy;
retaining enough recognizable substrate through far veil + local tint + body tint is the unproven part.

The formula also assumes the child backdrop samples the far-veiled scene in the same order in both
engines. An element's CSS box and mask do not establish a portable backdrop-root/compositing proof;
that ordering needs actual pixels over a structured substrate.

**Decision:** reject the near/far inequality and all readability conclusions derived from it.

**Cure:** DECLINE. If revisited, solve the local child alpha from a target total alpha and gate the
actual composited result, including the body plate, rather than checking custom-property literals.

### F8 — HIGH — ResizeObserver is neither sufficient for “actual surface” tracking nor demonstrably parsimonious

The proposed observer sees size changes, not arbitrary position or transform changes. The current
center Dialog can scale/translate during entry; CSS transforms do not trigger ResizeObserver. Consumer
classes, visual viewport movement, keyboard presentation, and future source-continuous motion can move
the border box without changing observed content dimensions. The house wrapper is rAF-coalesced and its
callback receives `contentRect`; a border-box `getBoundingClientRect()` read and an explicit initial
measurement would still be required.

The opposite failure is also possible: a height transition or animated content can emit resize
deliveries over many frames. “No rAF geometry loop” does not mean no continuous geometry writes when the
shared observer itself batches through rAF. W3 specifies zero writes after settle but gives no settle
definition, position invalidation set, or write counter threshold.

The observer may be a reasonable implementation after a mechanism is selected. It is not evidence that
the mechanism is KISS, and it cannot support the broader “tracks the actual surface” claim as written.
A wrapper- or pseudo-element-relative solution might remove measurement but has different backdrop
stacking and FocusScope risks; W3 does not prototype or rule that comparison.

**Decision:** reject the observer contract and the parsimony claim.

**Cure:** no observer is added under DECLINE. Row 93 uses the existing stage seam only.

### F9 — HIGH — the 60–85px comparator band cannot be a universal four-edge Dialog invariant

The 60–85px evidence belongs to a directional floated-control band, not a surface-concentric Dialog
offset. W3 converts it into a universal 5rem bloom without proving that transfer.

At 390px, a common near-full-width Dialog plus two `(core + bloom)` margins exceeds the viewport. The
apron is clipped, its horizontal falloff cannot complete, and the report's required pixel cuts cannot
show a 60–85px falloff beyond all four real edges. The design partly acknowledges viewport clipping but
still binds four-edge completion and calls the sample bounded. On this primary mobile cell the x-axis
often becomes a whole-viewport hold or a truncated few-pixel edge, so the declared signature is absent
or materially different.

**Decision:** reject fixed 5rem as a W3 law and reject the all-four-edge acceptance statement.

**Cure:** DECLINE. A future design would need an explicit insufficient-gutter posture and responsive
geometry, then prove it is still one identity rather than a desktop-only effect.

### F10 — HIGH — bounded element geometry does not prove bounded Safari sampling or cost

Replacing `inset:0` with a smaller CSS box is directionally good. It does not prove the engine's
backdrop capture, blur-kernel outset, damage region, intermediate surface allocation, or layer promotion
is equal to that visible box. Masking also changes visible contribution, not necessarily the sampled or
repainted backing area.

W3 asks to “compare” paint/style cost and invokes a bounded-area budget but supplies no pass/fail
threshold: no maximum area ratio, frame-time percentile, long-frame count, memory/layer ceiling, or
allowed regression against H-D. A kill switch without a number cannot turn RED. Safari is the engine
for which this matters most, yet the report has no Safari prototype trace.

**Decision:** reject the cost claim and the cost gate as non-testable.

**Cure:** row 93 inherits the already-ruled flat role and records its real baseline. A future apron
proposal must arrive with numeric Safari/Chromium budgets before, not after, public adoption.

### F11 — MEDIUM — PRT/forced-colors intent is sound, but the exact fallback is underspecified

W3 correctly distinguishes PRM from reduced transparency and correctly rejects the current “hide halo
while all flat arms remain gated off” hole. The pinned global glass fallback already supplies useful
machinery: `--glass-level:0` under reduced transparency/forced colors and `Canvas`/`CanvasText` structure
for floating surfaces.

The proposed apron contract nevertheless lacks exact ownership for the full-viewport barrier. “Forced
colors uses system colors” does not say which element paints `Canvas`, whether it is opaque, whether it
intercepts nothing, or how nested modal barriers distinguish. “Opaque/translucent veil remains” under
PRT also does not specify whether the apron mask remains decorative or disappears. Those are testable
only after the CSS branch is explicit.

**Decision:** accept the fallback principle; reject it as an implemented/born-RED contract.

**Cure:** DECLINE keeps the W2 level path. Row 93 adds exact actual-receiver assertions for level 0,
forced system colors, focus containment, outside inertness, and pointer non-interception. PRM follows
W2 and removes immersive staging.

### F12 — MEDIUM — side-sheet preservation needs an exact ownership split before the W3 strip

The design report correctly catches a dangerous coupling: centered FORM 2 and side-sheet FORM 1 share
`data-slot="glass-graded-halo"`, and BAND-MATERIAL's DECLINE prose says to remove both graded tests even
though the side-sheet sampler is independently valid. A broad `glass-halo`/graded deletion can erase or
orphan the sheet mechanism.

“Separate its descriptive name and tests” is directionally right but not exact enough. DECLINE needs an
affirmative survivor contract, not only a warning against grep.

**Decision:** accept this part of the W3 report and carry it into row 93.

**Cure:** before or in the same atomic strip, rename the internal side slot to an unambiguous
sheet-owned name, preserve its one-sampler host suppression and four-placement/scroll tests, then remove
only centered FORM 2, its axis, tokens, and tests. This is not a new Glass primitive or W3 adoption.
