## Conflict reconciliation

### 1. Foreground reveal blur is removed; the material does not need a rescue layer

Critics 1 and 2 allow either removal of the own-pixel blur or isolation onto a
decorative carrier. The parsimonious ruling is stricter: a Dialog, Toast or
other content-bearing reveal root never receives `filter: blur(...)`. Text,
icons, focus rings and hit geometry remain sharp from their first actionable
frame. Transmissive frost remains the plate's `backdrop-filter`, tint, rim and
underlay relation.

No new pseudo-element, optical plane or component is required merely to
preserve the old blur effect. If an existing noninteractive material carrier
already has a useful optical modulation, it may consume the same reveal scalar
only after sharp-ink and layer-budget proof; it is not part of the minimum
repair. This resolves the shared Dialog/Toast glitch at its source rather than
adding another blur knob or reveal engine.

### 2. Fast response remains lawful; glitch-then-snap does not

The critics agree that an 80ms settle is not intrinsically defective. This
adjudication does not impose a slower duration or an animation quota. The
defect is the observed sequence: destination-like blurred duplicate at click-0,
then a crisp terminal frame with no readable spatial continuity.

Where geometry travels, one generation must expose a sharp onset, at least one
non-degenerate current-value midcourse, and a sharp settle; interruption and
reversal begin from the painted position and velocity. Where the selected
motion policy is intentionally immediate, it snaps once with no blur,
duplicated ink, stale inert layer or delayed focus side effect. Natural origin
is centered for centered Dialogs and anchor/edge-derived for anchored or side
surfaces. Arbitrary top-corner growth is not the fallback.

### 3. Dialog paths owe equivalent outcomes, not one literal implementation

The default CSS reveal, explicit-center spring, side spring, scrim and stage
channels are not automatically rival engines. They may remain distinct
implementation paths only while one instance has one semantic open/close
generation and every supported branch satisfies the same origin, sharpness,
actionability, interruption, inertness, focus and PRM outcomes. A branch switch
cannot alter those outcomes. Source reduction is preferred where it removes a
writer, but this adjudication does not require a third shared abstraction.

### 4. Drawer retains its detent engine; cancel is not release

Drawer owns a legitimate problem that Dialog does not: drag, detents, pointer
capture and a keyboard slider. Preserve its one `SpringProgress`/sheet scalar,
logical direction projection, atomic stage composition and existing focus/PRM
kernels. Do not fold it into Dialog or delete the detent system.

Critic 3's source finding is binding: `pointercancel` cannot call the velocity
commit path used by `pointerup`; lost capture, handle replacement and unmount
must retire the transaction. Non-primary buttons/pointers do not begin it. A
canceled gesture rolls back or deterministically seats without using canceled
release velocity. The exact rollback/nearest-seat choice stays with the
existing Drawer owner, but cancellation and cleanup outcomes do not.

### 5. One subtree is banked; it is not yet one continuous expansion

ExpandableContainer's single teleported Vue subtree, FocusScope, body-lock
depth, Escape registry and normal close focus restoration are real GREEN. An
instant relative-to-fixed coordinate change is still spatially discontinuous.

The smallest target is observable, generation-correct geometry rather than a
mandatory public FLIP or `settle` API: the same subtree travels source bounds
to stable viewport bounds and back; live reversal/resize starts from current
geometry; context, canvas, config and focus identity survive; and the component
knows when the current reparented geometry is stable before dependent focus or
receiver measurement runs. The eventual internal/public spelling is left to
the existing owner. Atlas ChartFrame's stale native/synthetic-settle prose and
uncancelled rAF are conditionally reconciled only if the exported lifecycle
changes.

Critic 3 adds one exact terminal edge: unmount while open restores focus to a
connected invoker or defined safe fallback while releasing the matching body
lock and topmost Escape registration. Normal close-only restoration is
insufficient.

### 6. Blob's two policy questions were already answered by existing authority

Critic 3 presents pause/action and body/satellite hit ownership as owner-held.
The earlier normative Glass absorption already resolves both and therefore
controls:

- `paused` parks ambient/render motion; it is not `disabled`. The existing
  `disabled` axis alone owns action unavailability. An interactive Blob retains
  one named native action and emits its semantic action while paused; a visual
  impulse may remain parked. No second control or loop follows.
- the one action follows the bounded creature silhouette, including detached
  outboard satellites. Transparent corners and inter-satellite gaps fall
  through. Core-only clipping, per-satellite buttons and a rectangular
  transparent trap remain rejected.

The source-proven count truncation remains RED: live satellite identity must be
total through grow, shrink, replacement, fission, neck, merge and
re-separation. Preserve one component, simulator, renderer acquisition and
substrate clock.

### 7. The Spring story banks cancellation ownership, not endpoint reset

The managed preview's stop/generation guard is a useful lifecycle kernel; the
projected curve remains a useful reference. Critic 1 correctly finds that a
mid-flight `play()` which writes progress zero teleports before restarting.
That is not MOVEMENT OF MOMENTUM.

`Play`, `reverse`, `cancel`, parameter change and replay must have distinct,
truthful behavior. Mid-flight replay/retarget starts from the painted current
value and suppresses stale completion. If an explicit Replay includes return to
zero, that return is itself an authored visible phase rather than a hidden
endpoint write. The story must bite the same curve/PRM mutations as production
`SpringProgress`, but no new public spring class or forced shared
implementation is selected.

### 8. Toast has exactly one announcement owner

Current Reka ToastRoot already renders its hidden `ToastAnnounce`, including
foreground/assertive versus background/polite urgency. Wrapping the teleported
viewport in a second `aria-live="polite"` region creates a source-proven double-
announcement risk. This forward adjudication supersedes only the historical
`Toaster` polite-live-region KEEP clause; frozen historical text is not
back-edited.

The minimum target keeps Reka's one announcement owner and a non-live named
viewport landmark. Title, description and action alternate text announce once;
close content remains excluded as intended. A future replacement would have to
replace, not surround, that owner and prove equal urgency semantics.

### 9. A quiet close may be visually outboard; its actual owner may not vanish

Dialog's bare glyph and Toast's hover-revealed `p-1` close fail the house coarse
contract. The owner-requested partially outboard corner-X remains admissible as
a restrained visual composition, not a reason to shrink or hide its action.
Dialog and Toast each owe a named, focus-visible, discoverable no-hover action
whose actual non-overlapping coarse envelope is at least `44×44px`.

Use a logical safe corner, preserve RTL/writing-mode and viewport safety, and
keep the envelope isolated from Toast swipe/action ownership. No consumer
padding override, private selector or universal loud close style is selected.

## Apotheosis contracts

### A. Dialog, Toast and the shared reveal — existing overlay, A11Y,
`BJ.W-TOAST-DIALOG-PARITY` and `BJ.W-FEEDBACK-MOTION-TUNE`

The shared reveal's job is to make a material surface arrive from a meaningful
origin while its useful foreground remains immediately trustworthy.

1. One instance has one semantic open/close generation across plate, scrim,
   stage, focus, inertness and exit hold. Multiple paint mechanisms cannot
   expose contradictory logical state.
2. Content-bearing pixels remain sharp for the full lifecycle. Frost belongs
   behind content; focus rings and hit geometry never pass through a blurred
   duplicate.
3. Center, anchor and logical edge origins are explicit and stable. Current-
   value reversal does not remount, rebase or restart from an endpoint.
4. The first visually actionable frame accepts its intended action exactly
   once. Closing content is inert, and reopening cancels stale focus return.
5. Dialog and Toast close actions satisfy the separate visible-paint versus
   actual-hit proof. Toast remains discoverable on coarse/no-hover.
6. Toast retains one Reka queue/provider/viewport and exactly one announce
   path. Close, timeout, Escape, action, swipe end/cancel and exit completion
   share one retirement generation; no transparent held record survives.
7. Toast's house dissolve/vaporize exit may be opacity/material-led and faster
   than entry, but useful text remains legible until dismissal begins, the hit
   layer retires atomically, and PRM reaches the same semantic terminal.
8. The global PRM fallback suppresses rather than invents motion. Exact
   producer-owned exceptions remain bounded under the existing row-12 policy.

No new reveal component, notification system, blur axis, opacity axis or
consumer reskin is authorized.
