
The ledger also passed the surrounding DOM matching attacks:

- a different callback identity could not remove the entry;

one is mandatory, so an always-zero, wrong-owner or uninstrumented ledger
cannot satisfy the baseline.

## Quantifier and counterexample audit

C19 makes the negative conjunction total enough for this frozen seat:

- C18's `DIV`/`HTMLSpanElement.prototype`/`SPAN` counterexample is inadmissible
  because the decoy identity is fixed, null-prototype and proved absent from
  the second `SPAN` chain as well as the discovery chain;
- choosing a different decoy after either proof violates the one-fixed-object
  and no-substitution clauses;
- replacing the verification element after proof violates its already-created
  retained identity;
- inserting the decoy into either chain after proof violates the no-prototype-
  rewrite clause and the required post-instrumentation identity recheck;
- transiently or permanently patching an actual or element-local registration
  method violates "instrument only the decoy" and "actual owners unpatched";
- manually invoking the decoy cannot counterfeit interposition because decoy
  traversal is required to remain zero; and
- satisfying `0→0→0` is not itself success: the same bytes expressly require
  the independently established positive interposition predicate to turn RED.

Callback substitution and capture mismatch cannot make the negative trace
look decisive while weakening the positive proof: the positive baseline owns
the exact callback identity and cross-form false-capture removal, and the
negative mutation uses the already-created verification element under the
same active-count predicate.
