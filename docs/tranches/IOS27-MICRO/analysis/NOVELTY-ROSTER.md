## 1. SURVEY — what the platform honestly affords (Safari 2026 floor)

On-disk paint truth (WebKit 26.5, `safari-arm.md`):

- **Blur rides element opacity smoothly** (F1 H4 / F3 #8 / F5 U2)—an opacity-ramped
  backdrop-filter layer is the cheap, safe medium mechanism; no pre-blurred-overlay fallbacks
  needed (`safari-arm.md:148-150`). This is the load-bearing enabler for every candidate below
  that modulates blur: animate LAYER OPACITY under fixed blur radii, never the radius.
- **`@supports (backdrop-filter: url(…))` lies**—true while paint drops the WHOLE value; the
  shipped `glass-refract.css` gate is a live 7.0.0 defect routed to BJ (`safari-arm.md:91-102`,
  `cures-F5.md` G7). No SVG-filter composite on any glass hot path; runtime paint-probe gates only.
- **Scroll-driven animations bind and resample exactly** on WebKit 26.5 (`animation-timeline:
  scroll()` → tx exact at 0/150/300/450/600, frosting live); threading under jam is TOOL-DEFER
  (`safari-arm.md:128-133`). Safari 26.5 additionally shipped four SDA reliability fixes
  (timeline range names, play-state pausing, view-timeline progress, bfcache restoration).
- **The physics stack is engine-portable to the sub-pixel** (battery numbers, depth grading
  1.20, +28px carry, Chrome↔WebKit parity, `safari-arm.md:151-153`)—spring/scalar mechanisms
  need no per-engine arms.
- **`inherits:false` invalidation stays micro-scale on WebKit** (35µs vs 960µs inheriting over
  500 children, `safari-arm.md:80-82`); inheriting channel vars hold a 60Hz budget through
  ~1000 consumers (`cures-F3.md` G2). @property scalars are the sanctioned wiring — and per
  MECH M5, prototype scalars publish on COMPONENT ROOTS, never `:root`.
- **`scrollend` fires per discrete step on BOTH engines** (89/89)—debounce-primary is law for
  every scroll channel (`safari-arm.md:83-86,143-145`).
- **WebKit quantizes `performance.now()` to 1ms**—frame-gap statistics are the honest cost
  readout (`safari-arm.md:33-35`).

Platform census beyond the corpus (post-cutoff check, 2026-07-18):

- Safari 26.4 (2026-03) shipped CSS Grid Lanes (masonry), WebTransport, Keyboard Lock; 26.5
  matured anchor positioning and SDA fixes. Nothing in 26.4/26.5 changes the glass hot-path
  vocabulary: still transform/opacity/filter/clip-path + masked backdrop-filter stacks.
- **No native gradient blur exists**: `linear-blur()` is a csswg-drafts proposal
  (w3c/csswg-drafts#13285), unshipped. Progressive blur = 3-4 stacked backdrop-filter layers
  under static gradient masks, radius ~doubling per band—exactly codex law 1's web recipe
  (`IOS27-CODEX.md:11`). Community measurements agree with our discipline: animating blur radii
  recalculates per frame (ban it—animate layer opacity instead); Chromium struggles with stacked
  backdrop-filters + masks + `overflow:hidden` wrappers (cap the stack at 3, no clipping wrapper
  on the stack—the NO-CHROME-SPECIAL law cuts both ways: one mechanism that holds on both).
- Sources: [WebKit Features for Safari 26.4](https://webkit.org/blog/17862/webkit-features-for-safari-26-4/),
  [WebKit Features for Safari 26.5](https://webkit.org/blog/17938/webkit-features-for-safari-26-5/),
  [csswg-drafts #13285 linear-blur()](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13285),
  [progressive blur in css (kennethnym)](https://kennethnym.com/blog/progressive-blur-in-css/),
  [Making Apple Progressive Blur on the Web](https://www.devslovecoffee.com/blog/making-apple-progressive-blur-on-web).
