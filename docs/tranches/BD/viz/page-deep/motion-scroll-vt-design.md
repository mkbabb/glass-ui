# motion/scroll-vt — FRONTEND-DESIGN critique (Pass-E, design lens)

Page: `demo/stories/motion/scroll-vt.vue` · live `http://localhost:5173/motion/scroll-vt`
Captures: `_cap-scroll-vt-light.png` · `_cap-scroll-vt-dark.png` · `_cap-scroll-vt-full.png` · `_cap-scroll-vt-list.png`
North star: DESIGN.md (§L1 six-layer composite · §L2 spring physics · the 7 tiers · glass-cannot-sample-glass) · motion-canon.md (P1–P6) · affordance-map.md (the five primitives) · design-idioms.md · the dock APIs.

Verdict in one line: a *correct and honest* capability demo that ships its primitives faithfully but performs them inertly — a page about motion timelines whose own surfaces have no entrance, no hover, no press, wrapped in one undifferentiated chassis card over a near-dead constellation, contradicting the iOS-26 north star it documents.

---

## 1. VISUAL HIERARCHY — clean title, then flat-caption sprawl

- **The masthead is CORRECT (no double-title defect).** Unlike `motion/springs`, this page does NOT hand-roll an in-SFC `<header>` — `StoryPage`/`StoryHeader` owns the lone `<h1>` "Scroll & View Transitions" (display ink), the eyebrow `MOTION · SCROLL & VIEW TRANSITIONS`, the import chip `@mkbabb/glass-ui/motion-core`. The eye lands once, correctly, on the big ink title. KEEP this scaffold; it is the W-HIERARCHY2 model the springs page violates.
- **The audacious √φ ladder is entirely unspent below the masthead.** DESIGN.md is TYPOGRAPHY-FORWARD; after the `<h1>` the page is a flat field of `text-small`/`text-sm` body and mono `section-label` eyebrows. There is no secondary `text-subheading` heading rung, no `text-display` numeric anchor. A capability page has an obvious typographic protagonist it ignores: the **"supported / fallback" verdict** is the hero datum — it deserves a `text-display-3`-scale GREEN/violet readout, not a 14px pill. The most load-bearing word on the page ("supported") is among the smallest type on it.
- **Every section is a flat caption, never a heading.** All four `StorySection`s use `label=` (mono eyebrow) only — no `heading` rung (the AZ.W-HIERARCHY caption-not-heading trap). "CAPABILITY", "SCROLL PROGRESS", "VIEW-DRIVEN REVEAL", "VIEW TRANSITIONS" all read at the same 11px mono weight as a token caption. Four equal-weight eyebrows + four equal-weight body blurbs = no hierarchy; the eye has no rung to climb. Each section needs the canonical `text-subheading` `<h2>` so the four demos read as four distinct sections.

## 2. STRUCTURE — one big card, four stacked rows; the user's "own glassy sub-card" ask unmet

The user's binding directive: *each sub-section in its OWN glassy card; the main card area BIGGER.* This page does the opposite — it is the single-chassis-card-with-hairlines pattern.

- **Four sections live in ONE `StoryPage` chassis card,** separated only by the auto-hairline `delimited` rule (visible in the captures as the faint horizontal lines). Capability badges, the scroll-progress panel, the reveal grid, and the VT list are visually one undifferentiated column. Each is a *distinct teaching object* and should be its OWN `glass-resting`/`glass-quiet` sub-card (the W-STORY-PAGE-STANDARD `<DemoSpecimen>`/`<DemoInteraction>` taxonomy) so the four read as four objects, not four paragraphs.
- **The demo surfaces are bordered boxes, not glass tiles.** The scroll-progress panel and the reveal grid are `rounded-card border border-border/60` plain boxes (lines 85, 107) — a hairline-bordered rectangle, NOT a glass tier. On a glass library the *container the demo happens inside* should itself be a `glass-quiet`/`field` surface so the live field reads through it. Right now they are inert white/charcoal frames.
- **The protagonist surfaces read as flat grey slabs (the BG-2 black-plate class, live-confirmed).** The reveal cards and the VT list rows ARE `glass-card` (`oklab(0.726 … / 0.6)` + `blur(8px) saturate(1.05)` — measured), so the *tier* is right. But they sit over a near-flat constellation page with **nothing behind to refract**, so they render as opaque grey pills (see `_cap-scroll-vt-list.png` — Alpha–Echo are grey bars). DESIGN.md §L1: a glass surface with no kinetic backdrop "reads iOS-7-flat, not iOS-26-liquid." The fix is not the tier (correct) — it is the BACKDROP (§3) plus dropping the demo-container frames to `field` so the field reaches the cards.
- **The main card area is NOT bigger.** It's the standard StoryPage width with the four demos crammed top-to-bottom; the two genuinely interactive stages (the scroll-progress `h-64` panel and the reveal `h-72` grid) are SMALL fixed-height scroll boxes. The protagonist — the live scroll-driven progress bar — gets 256px of height. Per the ask, the demo stages should be the BIGGER focal area.

## 3. BACKGROUND — a near-dead constellation, not the colorful aurora the ask names

- The live background is `constellation-canvas` (verified) — sparse grey dots, near-invisible in light (`_cap-scroll-vt-light.png`), faint scatter in dark. The user's ask: *glass demos over COLORFUL aurora backgrounds.* This page demonstrates SCROLL + VIEW-TRANSITION motion — the natural backdrop is a **violet-tuned `<Aurora>`** seeded on the motion family's `--motion-accent`/`--viz-legendre` identity (the violet dots already used for the badges and VT-row markers). A live aurora would (a) give the `glass-card` tiles a real field to refract — turning the grey slabs into liquid glass — and (b) make the scroll-progress bar's compositor motion legible against a moving field. One GL context per route (offscreen-paused) is the budget; constellation already costs one, so this is a swap, not an addition.

## 4. ANIMATION AFFORDANCE — a *motion-timeline* page that is itself mostly static (the deepest failure)

Per the brief: *every element ALIVE at the iOS-27 bar — entrance, hover, press, state.* The cruel irony: this is the page that ships native scroll/view animation, and nearly nothing on it answers the pointer. Audit, control by control, against affordance-map.md's five primitives:

- **Entrance:** the page rides `.scroll-build` from the chassis (free). But the four section BODIES never ride `.scroll-cascade` — the badges, the panel, the reveal grid, and the VT rows do not stagger in. A page demonstrating `view()`-timeline reveals should make its OWN sections reveal on `view()` — the demo and the chrome should be the same idiom.
- **Capability badges (lines 60–77):** dead `<span>` pills. No HOVER-LIFT, no entrance, no state animation — they snap to "supported/fallback" with zero motion. The single most important state on the page (the capability verdict) has no animation affordance at all.
- **The reveal grid (lines 110–116):** the cards DO fade+lift on `[data-scroll-reveal]` view() entry — this is the ONE genuinely-alive surface, and it's correct (implicit stagger, no setTimeout). But the cards have NO hover-lift, NO press, NO gleam — they are inert grey rectangles the moment they've resolved. PRESS-SQUISH / HOVER-LIFT (affordance-map primitives 1+3) are absent.
- **The VT list rows (lines 127–135):** `glass-card` rows that reorder via View Transitions (correct, the protagonist demo) — but at rest they are dead full-width bars. No HOVER-LIFT on the row, no PRESS-SQUISH, no GLEAM-TRACK. A row that *animates between slots* should also feel touchable AT rest. The single violet dot is the only color event and it never pulses.
- **"Rotate order" button (line 125):** stock `variant="default"` — carries the library press floor (the only true four-state control on the page), and it's the least distinctive. It should be the gold/primary CTA of this demo, and the rotation it triggers should be visibly spring-clocked.
- **The scroll-progress bar (lines 87–90):** correct and honest (compositor scroll() timeline, no rAF) — the best primitive on the page. But it's a 4px hairline at the top of a small panel; it's *technically* alive and *visually* invisible. The protagonist motion deserves to be SEEN — thicker, on the violet, ideally as a `<BorderProgress>` ring tracing the panel's edge (progress-IS-the-border, the BB.W-BORDER-PROGRESS register) so the scroll motion reads as the card's living chrome.

**The dock APIs the user named are entirely absent.** The page never composes a `<GlassDock>` as a demo surface, never uses contextual switching, never demonstrates dock-morph. This page has FOUR independent demos stacked vertically — the textbook case for a **`<DockLayerGroup>` rail or `<DockStack mode="facets">`**: pick "Scroll progress / Reveal / View Transitions / Capability" from a dock rail, the dock morphs to the selected facet's demo on the `DOCK_SPRING` clock. That single move (a) leverages the dock APIs the ask demands, (b) makes the main demo area BIGGER (one focal stage instead of four small ones), and (c) demonstrates contextual switching — itself a *transition* the page is nominally about.

## 5. POLISH + DISTINCTIVENESS — competent template, not bespoke-premium

- The layout is the generic-AI documentation shape: title, four labeled prose+demo blocks stacked top-to-bottom, each a bordered box. Nothing about the COMPOSITION says "this is THE motion library." A world-class scroll/VT page would let the facilities AUTHOR the layout: a capability state that springs its verdict in, a reveal grid that cascades on the very `view()` timeline it documents, a VT list whose reorder is the page's centerpiece (big, on aurora, with rows that overshoot into their new slots).
- **The scroll-progress motion is invisible by design.** A page literally named "Scroll progress" hides its progress in a 4px top hairline. Make it the hero: a thick `<BorderProgress>` ring on the panel, or a large violet bar with a coupled brightness leg (motion-canon P3 fade-coupled-to-transform) so the scroll energy reads.
- **Color suffusion is correct but minimal.** The violet `--motion-accent` appears on the badge dots, the progress bar, and the VT-row dots — within the one-color-event rule (good, `proof:suffuse`-clean). But it's an accent dab, never the page's identity. Lifted to an aurora seed it would become the page's color story instead of four scattered purple pixels.
- **Distinctive opportunity missed: the VT reorder is genuinely cool and buried at the bottom.** The `.gl-list-item` group reorder is the most visually-interesting thing the page does — and it's the LAST section, in grey bars, with a grey button. Promote it: make it the focal demo, over aurora, with glass rows that overshoot.

## 6. SPACING / RHYTHM

- Generally fine — `gap-3`/`gap-4`/`gap-6` on the √φ-adjacent scale, the BB.W-CARD-PAD chassis ladder owns the card padding. The reveal grid `sm:grid-cols-2` and the `h-64`/`h-72` fixed scroller heights are arbitrary, not φ-derived; on a golden-ratio library the demo stage heights and the grid splits could be deliberately φ-stepped. Minor relative to §§2–4.

## 7. COPY — accurate but jargon-dense; the "tighten language" ask partly unmet

- The blurbs are *honest* but speak to a compiler, not a user. Capability (lines 56–57): *"Where the engine lacks animation-timeline, the JS composables are the ≤20-LOC fallback (single writer, no double-run)."* — a consumer does not care about LOC count or double-run. → "Native where the browser supports it; a tiny JS fallback otherwise."
- Scroll-progress (line 83): *"no rAF, no scroll listener … costs the main thread nothing"* repeated again in every paragraph body ("it costs the main thread nothing" × 14 paragraphs, line 92–96) — the filler paragraphs literally repeat the same sentence. → make the filler real lorem or 3–4 distinct lines; state the perf claim ONCE.
- VT (line 122): *"through the startViewTransition substrate + the .gl-list-item group recipe"* — internal symbol names on the rendered surface. → "Reorder the list — each row glides to its new slot. Engines without View Transitions just snap."
- **Import label IS standardized — KEEP.** The chrome chip reads `@mkbabb/glass-ui/motion-core` (verified), the correct subpath convention, no `/motion/scroll-vt` local-label drift. This page is on the right convention.

## TOP DESIGN MOVES (ranked, concrete)

1. **Live violet aurora field.** Swap `constellation` for a `--motion-accent`-seeded `<Aurora>` (offscreen-paused, one-GL-per-route), and drop the two demo-container frames (`rounded-card border`) + the reveal grid to `field`/transparent so the aurora refracts THROUGH the `glass-card` tiles — turning the grey slabs into real liquid glass (DESIGN.md §L1; BG-2 fix). Single highest-leverage move; it makes every existing glass surface read correctly at zero structural cost.
2. **Bind the dock APIs — `<DockStack mode="facets">` / `<DockLayerGroup>` rail.** Replace the four-sections-stacked-vertically layout with ONE focal demo stage + a dock rail that switches facets (Capability / Scroll progress / Reveal / View Transitions). The dock morphs to the selected facet on `DOCK_SPRING` — leverages contextual switching (the ask), makes the demo area BIGGER (the ask), and is itself a transition demo. Closes the user's two structural asks at once.
3. **Each demo → its OWN glassy sub-card; enlarge the focal stage.** Wrap each facet in a `glass-resting`/`glass-quiet` `<DemoSpecimen>`/`<DemoInteraction>` (W-STORY-PAGE-STANDARD). The protagonist (the VT reorder + the scroll-progress) gets the bigger frame.
4. **Make the scroll-progress motion VISIBLE — `<BorderProgress>`.** Replace the 4px top hairline with a `<BorderProgress coverage>` ring tracing the panel edge (progress-IS-the-border, BB.W-BORDER-PROGRESS) on the violet, with the coupled brightness leg. The page named "Scroll progress" should let you SEE the progress.
5. **Animate every control to the affordance-map bar.** Capability badges → entrance + HOVER-LIFT + a spring pop when state flips; reveal/VT cards → HOVER-LIFT (primitive 1) + PRESS-SQUISH (primitive 3); section bodies → `.scroll-cascade` stagger-in (the page should reveal on the `view()` timeline it documents); "Rotate order" → primary/gold CTA, spring-clocked rotation with rows that overshoot into slots.
6. **Headline the capability verdict.** Promote "supported / fallback" to a large `text-display-3`-scale GREEN/violet readout — the hero datum deserves the audacious ladder, not a 14px pill.
7. **Add the `heading` rung.** Give each section a canonical `text-subheading` `<h2>` so the four demos read as sections, not four equal-weight captions.
8. **Tighten copy.** One human sentence per blurb; the perf claim stated ONCE; the 14 repeated filler paragraphs replaced; internal symbol names (`startViewTransition`, `.gl-list-item`, "≤20-LOC", "no double-run") moved to code comments, off the rendered surface.
9. **Promote the VT reorder to the centerpiece.** It's the coolest thing the page does and it's buried last in grey — make it the focal facet, over aurora, glass rows overshooting into their new slots.
