# motion/scroll-system — FRONTEND-DESIGN critique (Pass-E, design lens)

Page: `demo/stories/motion/scroll-system.vue` · live `http://localhost:5173/motion/scroll-system`
Captures: `_cap-scroll-system-light.png` · `_cap-scroll-system-dark.png` · `_cap-scroll-system-mid.png` (scroll-port + log) · `_cap-scroll-system-lower.png` (floating-chrome collapse)
North star: DESIGN.md (§L1 the six-layer optical composite · §L2 spring physics · the 7 glass tiers · glass-cannot-sample-glass) · motion-canon.md (P1–P6) · affordance-map.md (the five primitives + the befitting fence) · design-idioms.md · the dock APIs (contextual switching / morph / silhouette).

Verdict in one line: an HONEST, structurally-correct capability demo of `useScrollTrigger`/`useScrollChrome` that ships its reader faithfully — but performs it over a DEAD constellation in ONE undifferentiated chassis card, with grey-slab rows that never answer the pointer, so a page literally about *scroll-driven motion* is itself almost entirely static and flat, contradicting the iOS-26/27 north star it documents.

---

## 1. VISUAL HIERARCHY — the masthead is right; the body is a caption-and-prose field

- **The masthead is CORRECT and is the model to keep.** `StoryPage`/`StoryHeader` owns the lone display `<h1>` "Scroll System" (large warm ink), the mono eyebrow `MOTION · SCROLL SYSTEM`, and the import chip `@mkbabb/glass-ui/motion-core`. The eye lands once, on the title. No double-`<h1>` defect (W-HIERARCHY2 clean). KEEP this scaffold.
- **Section headings ARE present and correctly ranked — a genuine improvement over sibling motion pages.** All three `StorySection`s use `heading=` → semantic `<h2 class="text-subheading">` (measured 20.35px / 600, the AZ.W-HIERARCHY canonical section rung). "The one scroll reader", "Scroll the port", "Shrink the floating chrome on scroll" read as sections, not as captions. This page is NOT the four-equal-eyebrow trap the scroll-vt page falls into. CREDIT where due.
- **The audacious √φ ladder is otherwise unspent.** DESIGN.md is TYPOGRAPHY-FORWARD; below the `<h1>` and the three `text-subheading` `<h2>`s the page is a flat field of `text-prose`/`text-small` body and `text-admin-label` mono pills. There is NO `text-display` numeric protagonist. The page's hero DATA — the live `progress %`, the `velocity px/s`, the `collapse %` — are the most kinetic numbers in the library and they sit in 11px mono pills (`text-admin-label`). A scroll-progress readout that updates every frame is the textbook case for a `text-display-2`/`-3` reel (the `<AnimatedDigit>` register) — the live number should be BIG and ALIVE, not a tiny static badge. The most load-bearing, fastest-changing values on the page are among the smallest type on it.
- **The "Scroll me" cue inside the port (`text-heading`, line 115) out-ranks the readout.** A decorative in-port prompt is bigger than the live `progress`/`velocity` telemetry. The hierarchy is inverted: the instruction shouts, the data whispers.

## 2. STRUCTURE — one chassis card, three hairline-delimited rows; the user's "own glassy sub-card" ask UNMET

The user's binding directive: *each sub-section in its OWN glassy card; the main card area BIGGER.* This page is the single-chassis-card-with-hairlines pattern — the exact shape the ask rejects.

- **All three sections live inside ONE `StoryPage` chassis card** (measured: 1 `.glass-card`), separated only by the auto `delimited` hairline rule (visible in `_cap-scroll-system-mid.png` as the faint horizontal line under "The one scroll reader"). The reader explainer, the live scroll-port demo, and the floating-chrome collapse demo are visually ONE undifferentiated column. Each is a *distinct teaching object* (a continuous reader, a discrete-crossing logger, a collapse-state machine) and should be its OWN `glass-resting`/`glass-quiet` sub-card so the three read as three objects, not three stacked paragraphs.
- **The protagonist surfaces are grey slabs — the BG-2 black-plate class, live-confirmed in BOTH modes.** The scroll-port rows, the 20 collapse-demo rows, and the 14 "Row N" tiles are `.glass-quiet` (measured 35 of them) — the *tier is right*. But they sit over a near-dead `constellation-canvas` (verified: sparse grey dots, opacity 1 but almost no luminance) with **nothing kinetic behind to refract**, so they render as opaque grey bars in light (`_cap-scroll-system-mid.png`) and flat charcoal bars in dark (`_cap-scroll-system-dark.png`). DESIGN.md §L1: a glass surface with no kinetic backdrop reads iOS-7-flat, not iOS-26-liquid. Worse, **glass-quiet rows stacked inside a glass-card chassis is the glass-cannot-sample-glass violation** (DESIGN.md) — translucent tiles over a translucent plate over a dead field = three layers of grey, no refraction anywhere. The dark mode is the most damning: a near-black void where W-DARK-MATERIAL promises a "luminous transmissive material."
- **The demo stages are SMALL and the card area is NOT bigger.** The scroll-port is a fixed `h-[24rem]` box; the collapse demo `h-[26rem]`. The protagonist — the live scroll-driven readout + the floating-chrome collapse — gets ~400px each, crammed two-up with a 14rem log panel. Per the ask, the demo stage should be the BIGGER focal area; instead it's a pair of modest fixed-height scroll boxes inside a standard-width chassis.

## 3. BACKGROUND — a dead constellation, not the colorful aurora the ask names

- The live background is `constellation-canvas` (verified) — sparse, near-invisible in light, a faint scatter over a black void in dark. The user's ask: *glass demos over COLORFUL aurora backgrounds.* This page demonstrates SCROLL-DRIVEN motion — the natural backdrop is a **violet-tuned `<Aurora>`** seeded on the motion family's `--motion-accent` (`light-dark(oklch(0.532 0.180 317.5), oklch(0.739 0.134 318.1))` — the `--viz-legendre` violet already used for the in-port dots and the onCross log highlight). A live aurora would (a) give every `.glass-quiet` row a real field to refract — turning the grey slabs into liquid glass (DESIGN.md §L1), (b) make the compositor scroll/collapse motion legible against a moving field, and (c) give the scroll-progress motion something to read AGAINST. One GL context per route (offscreen-paused) is the budget; constellation already spends one, so this is a swap, not an addition. This is the single highest-leverage move on the page.

## 4. ANIMATION AFFORDANCE — a SCROLL-MOTION page that is itself mostly static (the deepest failure)

Per the brief: *every element ALIVE at the iOS-27 bar — entrance, hover, press, state.* The cruel irony repeats: the page that ships the scroll reader has almost nothing that answers the pointer. Audit, control by control, against affordance-map.md's five primitives (HOVER-LIFT · GLEAM-TRACK · PRESS-SQUISH · DRAG-MORPH · FOCUS-RING):

- **Entrance:** the page rides `.scroll-build` from the chassis (free). But the three section BODIES never ride `.scroll-cascade` — the explainer, the port, the log, and the collapse rows do not stagger in. A page literally about `view()`/`scroll()` timelines should make its OWN sections reveal on the very `.scroll-cascade` idiom it documents — the chrome and the demo should be the SAME motion.
- **The readout pills (lines 93–103, 190–200):** dead `<span>` badges. `transition: all` is set but nothing drives it — they snap their text with zero motion. The `progress %` / `velocity px/s` / `collapse %` values update every frame and the BADGES never react: no scale pulse on change, no color-temperature shift, no fill. The most kinetic data on the page has zero animation affordance. These should be `<AnimatedDigit>` reels or at minimum a `--*-press-t`-style brightness pulse keyed to the live value.
- **The 14 "Row N" port tiles + the 20 collapse rows (lines 119–131, 225–235):** inert `.glass-quiet` rectangles. NO HOVER-LIFT (affordance-map primitive 1), NO PRESS-SQUISH (primitive 3), NO GLEAM-TRACK. They are the bulk of the page's surface area and every one of them is dead. A `.glass-quiet` content row is befitting of a `—` on GLEAM (a quiet surface, gleam reserved for the trigger) — but HOVER-LIFT is the cheap, correct cue that says "this is a live demo surface," and it is absent on every tile.
- **The "Scroll me" dot + the floating-header dot (lines 116, 211):** static `--motion-accent` violet pips. They never pulse. On a page about live scroll telemetry, the accent dot should breathe/pulse with `velocity` (the one-color-event made ALIVE) — right now it's a dead 8px circle.
- **The floating-chrome header (lines 207–216):** this IS the genuinely-alive surface and it's CORRECT — `useScrollChrome` writes `--chrome-collapse-t`, the recipe paints a compositor `scale/translateY` + opacity quiet, flick-to-endpoint, snap-to-nearest, no reflow (motion-canon P5 compositor-only, P6 PRM). This is the best primitive on the page and it deserves to be the centerpiece, not the third section. But the header itself, at rest, has no HOVER-LIFT and no GLEAM — a `.glass-floating` chrome bar should gleam (it's the one surface here that earns the tier-root specular auto-arm, BB.W-LIQUIDHOVER / design-idioms §12).
- **`clear` / `recalculate` / the in-port buttons (lines 141–171):** bare `text-admin-label` text buttons with a `hover:text-foreground` color swap — NO press, NO scale, NO focus-ring affordance visible. They are below the library's own four-state floor. `recalculate` and `clear` should be real `<Button variant="ghost">`/`secondary` carrying `.tap-squish` + `useSpringPress` + `.focus-ring` (the affordance-map Button row), not raw `<button>` text.

**The dock APIs the user named are entirely absent.** The page never composes a `<GlassDock>`, never uses contextual switching, never demonstrates dock-morph. It has THREE independent demos stacked vertically — the textbook case for a **`<DockStack mode="facets">` / `<DockLayerGroup>` rail**: pick "Reader / Crossings / Collapse" from a dock rail, the focal stage morphs to the selected facet on the `DOCK_SPRING` clock. That one move (a) leverages the dock APIs the ask demands, (b) makes the main demo area BIGGER (one focal stage instead of three small ones), and (c) is ITSELF a scroll-adjacent transition — perfectly on-theme for a motion page. And the floating-chrome collapse demo (section 3) is conceptually a *dock that shrinks on scroll* — it should literally be demonstrated ON a real `<GlassDock collapseOnScroll>`, closing the loop between the reader and the dock system the library is built around.

## 5. POLISH + DISTINCTIVENESS — competent, honest, but generic-AI documentation shape

- The composition is the textbook AI-doc layout: title, three labeled prose+demo blocks stacked top-to-bottom inside one bordered chassis. Nothing about the COMPOSITION says "THIS is the motion library." A world-class scroll page would let the facility AUTHOR the layout — the scroll-progress should drive a visible hero ring, the crossing log should animate each entry in on the `--spring-snappy` clock as it lands, the collapse demo should be a real dock collapsing.
- **The onCross log is a missed distinctiveness moment.** Each crossing appends an entry (line 41) — and the entries just *appear* with no motion. A live event log on a motion library should have each new row SLIDE/FADE in on the spring (the `view()`/`.scroll-cascade` entrance the page documents), the newest row pulsing the violet. Right now it's a static `<ul>` that mutates silently — the LEAST alive treatment of the most "live" concept (real-time events) on the page.
- **Color suffusion is correct but minimal.** The violet `--motion-accent` appears on the two dots and the log `e.id` highlight — within the one-color-event rule (`proof:suffuse`-clean, good). But it's an accent dab, never the page's identity. Lifted to an aurora seed (§3) it becomes the page's color STORY instead of three scattered violet pixels.
- **Distinctive opportunity buried: the floating-chrome collapse is genuinely premium and it's the LAST, smallest section.** The flick-to-endpoint / snap-to-nearest collapse over a scrolling port is the coolest, most iOS-27 thing the page does — and it's third, in grey, with a grey header. Promote it: make it the focal facet, over aurora, on a real dock.

## 6. SPACING / RHYTHM — fine, with arbitrary stage heights

- Generally fine — `gap-3`/`gap-4` on the √φ-adjacent scale, BB.W-CARD-PAD owns the chassis padding ladder. The fixed `h-[24rem]`/`h-[26rem]` stage heights and the `min-w-[14rem]` log panel are arbitrary, not φ-derived; on a golden-ratio library the demo-stage heights could be deliberately φ-stepped (e.g. the port and collapse stage in a φ ratio). The `pt-[5.5rem]` header-clearance offset (line 222) is a magic number that should derive from the floating-header's reserved block extent, not a hardcoded literal. Minor relative to §§2–4.

## 7. COPY — accurate but compiler-facing; the "tighten language" ask largely UNMET

- The blurbs are honest but speak to the compiler, not a user. Reader (lines 80–90): *"off the SAME rAF-coalesced read — the dual-path single-writer … the `flipDeltaPx = 8` anti-thrash."* — a consumer does not care about rAF-coalescing or the flip-delta constant. → "Scroll the port: the readout tracks live, and each trigger logs once — a jitter at the edge fires nothing."
- The in-port filler (lines 126–128) repeats *"Crossing the 120px / 50% / marker trigger-points fires onCross once each"* on every one of 14 rows — the same sentence × 14 (visible in `_cap-scroll-system-mid.png`). → vary the filler or state it once on row 1.
- Collapse (lines 177–187): *"the box never reflows. PERSISTENT by default — `collapseOnScroll: true` is the explicit opt-in."* — internal API/perf detail on the rendered surface. → "Scroll down to shrink the header; up to expand; stop to snap. Off by default."
- Internal symbol names (`flipDeltaPx`, `--chrome-collapse-t`, `velocityGate`, "dual-path single-writer") are rendered ON the surface. Move them to code comments; the visible copy should read like product, not source.
- **Import label IS standardized — KEEP.** The chip reads `@mkbabb/glass-ui/motion-core` (verified), the correct subpath convention, no `/motion/scroll-system` local-label drift. On the right convention.

## TOP DESIGN MOVES (ranked, concrete)

1. **Live violet aurora field.** Swap `constellation` for a `--motion-accent`/`--viz-legendre`-seeded `<Aurora>` (offscreen-paused, one-GL-per-route). Drop the chassis card and the demo-stage frames so the aurora refracts THROUGH the `.glass-quiet` rows — turning every grey slab into real liquid glass and rescuing the dead dark-mode void (DESIGN.md §L1 + §L2; BG-2 fix; W-DARK-MATERIAL realized). Single highest-leverage move; corrects every existing glass surface at zero structural cost.
2. **Bind the dock APIs — `<DockStack mode="facets">` / `<DockLayerGroup>` rail.** Replace three-sections-stacked with ONE focal stage + a dock rail switching facets (Reader / Crossings / Collapse) — the stage morphs on `DOCK_SPRING`. Leverages contextual switching (the ask), makes the demo area BIGGER (the ask), and is itself a transition demo. Closes both structural asks at once.
3. **Demonstrate the collapse ON a real `<GlassDock collapseOnScroll>`.** The floating-chrome section IS conceptually a dock that shrinks on scroll — make it a live `<GlassDock>` collapsing, closing the loop between `useScrollChrome` and the dock system. Promote it to the focal facet, over aurora.
4. **Each demo → its OWN glassy sub-card; enlarge the focal stage.** Wrap each facet in a `glass-resting`/`glass-quiet` sub-card; the protagonist stage (the live readout + collapse) gets the bigger frame. Fix the glass-cannot-sample-glass stacking: a glass sub-card over aurora, opaque content INSIDE it.
5. **Make the live readout BIG and ALIVE — `<AnimatedDigit>` on the √φ ladder.** Promote `progress %` / `velocity px/s` / `collapse %` from 11px mono pills to `text-display-2`/`-3` reels (`<AnimatedDigit>`), pulsing on change. The fastest-changing data on the page deserves the audacious ladder, not a static badge.
6. **Animate every surface to the affordance-map bar.** Section bodies → `.scroll-cascade` stagger (the page reveals on the `view()` timeline it documents); port/collapse rows → HOVER-LIFT (primitive 1) + PRESS-SQUISH (primitive 3); the floating header → tier-root gleam (BB.W-LIQUIDHOVER); the accent dots → pulse keyed to `velocity`.
7. **Animate the onCross log.** Each new crossing entry slides/fades in on `--spring-snappy`, the newest pulsing violet — the most "live" concept on the page should be its most kinetic surface.
8. **Real Buttons for `clear`/`recalculate`/in-port controls.** Replace raw `<button>` text with `<Button variant="ghost"|"secondary">` carrying `.tap-squish` + `useSpringPress` + `.focus-ring` (the library's own four-state floor).
9. **Tighten copy.** One human sentence per blurb; the perf/internal claims to code comments; the 14× repeated in-port filler varied or stated once; `flipDeltaPx`/`--chrome-collapse-t`/`velocityGate` off the rendered surface.
