## R1 (TOP REFUTATION — KISS/DRY + deft-integration) — `.overlay-trigger` is a PARALLEL FORK of `.dock-trigger`, not a union

§5 proposes a **NEW** `.overlay-trigger` register in a **NEW** file (`glass/overlay-trigger.css`) that "mirrors the SHIPPED `.dock-trigger` discipline." But §6's own table lists it as **"NEW recipe (1)"**. This is the exact bolt-on the BINDING LAW forbids: a second register that re-declares the same contract (no-hover-scale, `[data-state=open]` pressed plate, edge floor, bloom-from-trigger) the shipped `.dock-trigger` already encodes.

Reading `dock-controls/triggers.css` (BD.W-DOCK-CORE A8) confirms the danger: `.dock-trigger` already unified DockSelect/Dropdown/Popover triggers byte-identically with the no-hover-scale discipline and a documented "scaling trigger shifts its portaled anchor mid-open" reason — **the identical rationale §5 re-states verbatim.** The GOLDEN spawns a sibling that duplicates that reasoning and that ruleset. When the dock trigger's open-plate token evolves, `.overlay-trigger` silently drifts — the precise "can never drift" failure §3 brags about avoiding for the panel, reintroduced at the trigger.

The only thing keeping `.dock-trigger` from being directly reused is that it bakes `--dock-*` tokens (`--dock-trigger-gap`, `--dock-control-radius`, `--dock-fg-on-aurora`, `--dock-press-spring`). That is a **token-namespace** divergence, not a behavior divergence — and it is solvable by EXTRACTING the shared behavior, not by forking it.

**Hardening:** Do NOT mint `.overlay-trigger` as a parallel recipe. Extract the shared trigger contract into ONE base utility (`@utility trigger-anchor` or a shared placeholder in glass-material) carrying the geometry + no-hover-scale + `[data-state=open]` plate, parameterized by custom-property knobs (`--trigger-gap`, `--trigger-radius`, `--trigger-fg`, `--trigger-press-spring`). `.dock-trigger` composes it with `--dock-*` values; the non-dock overlay triggers compose it with the overlay defaults. That is a UNION (one source of truth, two configurations). If extraction is too costly this tranche, the honest move is to scope it as `BD.W-DOCK-CORE` follow-up and have the overlay triggers compose `.dock-trigger` directly with overridden `--dock-*` knobs — NOT a new class. As written, §5 is a fork and fails the union law.

## R2 (DRY + correctness) — the `--stage-t` ↔ `--glass-drawer-t` mirror is a DUAL SCALAR for ONE quantity

§2e drives the spine by `--stage-t: var(--glass-drawer-t)` on the content root. But `--glass-drawer-t` is `inherits: false` (§2 says so explicitly — "it is the SHEET's translate; --stage-t is the SCENE scalar") and `--stage-t` is `inherits: true`. So the system now carries **two registered scalars that must hold the same value every frame**, mirrored by hand. That is a DRY violation dressed as a coupling: any code path that writes `--glass-drawer-t` without re-mirroring (a future drag-cancel, a velocity-fling overshoot past 1, an interrupted snap) desyncs the scene from the sheet — the page recede and the sheet translate fall out of lockstep. The §8 acceptance bar ("all in lockstep on the ONE snap clock") is then a LIE under interruption, and no born-RED bite tests the desync (G3-G5 sample steady detents, not an interrupted drag).

There is also an unstated overshoot hazard: `useDrawerSnap` SpringProgress can overshoot >1 on a fling (the cartoon-punch ease the GOLDEN itself wants has `1.18` overshoot). `--sheet-freeze: clamp(0, (t-0.85)/0.15, 1)` clamps fine, but `[data-stage-wrapper] scale: 1 - 0.05*t` does NOT clamp — a t=1.18 fling scales the page to **0.94 and keeps shrinking past the spec floor**, and `border-radius: var(--radius-panel) * 1.18` blooms past the panel radius. The spike's own `--ease-cartoon-punch` linear() proves overshoot is in-band.

**Hardening:** (a) Make `--stage-t` the SINGLE registered scalar the snap engine writes directly (rename/retire `--glass-drawer-t`, or define `--glass-drawer-t` as a `calc()` read OF `--stage-t` so there is one writer, one source). No-legacy law actively WANTS this: don't keep two. (b) `clamp(0,…,1)` every consumer of `--stage-t` that is not freeze-clamped already — wrap the page-scale/radius/scrim reads in `clamp()` so a fling overshoot cannot drive the scene past its staged floor. (c) Add a born-RED bite: **interrupt a drag mid-snap and assert sheet-translate and page-scale stay coupled** (the lockstep claim must be tested, not asserted).

## R3 (correctness / Safari-specific) — coupling (C) `filter: saturate()` on `[data-stage-wrapper]` is an ANCESTOR filter over the whole app

§2c/§7 insists the staging transform is "transform ONLY, NO filter on an ancestor of the glass panel (§L7)." But the spike AND §2c BOTH put `filter: saturate(calc(1 - 0.18 * var(--stage-t)))` directly on `[data-stage-wrapper]` (spike line 50; GOLDEN §2c line 175). The page-wrapper is the app root. The scrim and sheet portal OUTSIDE that wrapper (different z-layer) so they aren't filtered — OK — but:

1. The GOLDEN's own §7 row for "page recede" says "scale+filter:saturate on page-root" while the cell two rows up swears "NO filter on an ancestor of the glass panel." **These two cells contradict each other.** If any glass surface ever lives INSIDE the staged wrapper (a non-portaled inline popover, a tooltip rendered in-place, a glass card the page itself paints), the ancestor `filter` collapses its `backdrop-filter` to garbage in Safari — the exact dock-hub goo-tear lesson the GOLDEN cites.
2. `filter` on the app root forces an offscreen buffer for the ENTIRE page every frame the value animates — on a large page that is the opposite of "compositor-cheap." Safari is worse here than Chrome.

**Hardening:** Drop the `saturate()` desaturation from coupling (C) entirely (it is a nice-to-have "scene recedes" flourish, not load-bearing — the scale + radius + scrim already sell the depth) OR move it to a `::before` overlay-tint on the wrapper that does NOT filter descendants. Reconcile the §7 table: state plainly that coupling (C) carries `scale` + `border-radius` ONLY on the wrapper, and that the desaturation rides a separate non-filtering layer. As written §7 self-contradicts and §2c plants a real Safari trap.

## R4 (precept / gray-glass risk in the freeze) — the freeze lerps toward `--card`, but the dark-arm `--card` is a near-gray-brown

§2a freezes the sheet by `color-mix(... var(--card) calc(sheet-freeze*100%))`. The spike's light `--card: hsl(30 85% 96%)` is warm-cream — fine. But the real dark `--card` (`glass.css`) and the spike's own `.dark --card: hsl(26 22% 17%)` is a LOW-chroma brown that at the opaque escape (sheet-freeze=1) leaves the full sheet sitting at ~`oklab(0.35 .01 .02)` — barely above the BA.W-NO-GRAY warm floor. The GOLDEN's G3 born-RED tests opacity-at-full but **G1 warm-not-gray explicitly carves out the opaque path** ("opaque-at-full SURVIVES" §7/§10) without asserting the opaque plate is still C≥0.02 warm. So a fully-frozen dark drawer could read charcoal and PASS the gate.

**Hardening:** G3 (or G1) must assert the **frozen full sheet** is still C ≥ 0.02 warm (H ∈ [16,110]), dark mode required — the freeze endpoint is a painted region the warm-not-gray rule must cover, not exempt. If the dark `--card` alone can't clear the floor, the freeze must lerp toward a warm-tinted card-mix (`color-mix(--card, --glass-tint-source X%)`), not bare `--card`. The §3 "never gray" precept does not get a holiday at full opacity.

## R5 (DRY / overfit) — the four `color-mix` nesting in §2a is 3-deep with a no-op outer wrap

§2a / spike line 94-98: the freeze background is a triple-nested `color-mix`, and the OUTERMOST wrap is `color-mix(in oklab, <inner>, var(--glass-tint-source) 0%)` — a **0% mix, i.e. a literal no-op** that just returns `<inner>`. That is dead code shipped into the canonical recipe. It looks like a leftover from a tint-strength experiment. KISS violation: a reader cannot tell if the 0% is intentional (a knob meant to be turned up) or a mistake.

**Hardening:** Either delete the no-op outer wrap (KISS) or, if the intent is "a tint knob the band can dial," make it `var(--sheet-freeze-tint, 0%)` with a comment naming it a knob — not a bare `0%` that reads as a bug. Two-deep mix suffices for the transmissive→solid lerp.

## R6 (a11y / PRM gap) — `--stage-t` "still FLIPS under PRM" desaturates + scales the page with NO motion-reduce escape for `prefers-reduced-transparency` + the page-scale itself

§7 PRM carve says the page-scale "value applies but does not animate" and the scene "jumps to staged state." Fine for vestibular motion. BUT: a user on `prefers-reduced-motion` who opens a dialog now gets the **entire page snap-scaled to 0.95 + desaturated 18% + corner-bloomed in one frame** with no opt-out. That is a jarring full-viewport visual jolt that PRM users (often the most jolt-sensitive) did not consent to — "reduce motion" does not mean "I want the page to teleport-shrink." The GOLDEN treats staging as "structure, not motion" and exempts it, but a sudden full-page transform IS perceived motion even at one frame.

**Hardening:** Under PRM, `stage="scale"` should degrade to `stage="dim"` (scrim only, no page transform) — the depth cue downgrades to a luminance cue. Add this to the §7 carve and a born-RED bite (G10): PRM + `stage="scale"` → page-wrapper `transform: none`, scrim present. The current carve "page-scale value applies but does not animate" should be "page-scale is SUPPRESSED under PRM; scrim-dim carries the affordance."

## R7 (soft — overfit/proportion nit) — the φ pad ladder `× 1.272` is √φ, not φ

§3 comments `--overlay-pad-block: calc(--overlay-pad-inline * 1.272)` as "(√φ·√φ ≈ φ)". But 1.272 ≈ √φ (φ≈1.618; √φ≈1.272). √φ·√φ = φ, yes — but the MULTIPLIER applied is 1.272 = √φ, so pad-block = pad-inline × **√φ**, not × φ. The comment's "≈ φ" is wrong about what the ratio between the two paddings IS (it's √φ). Either the intent was φ (then use 1.618) or √φ (then fix the comment). The Aristotelian-proportion law wants this stated correctly.

**Hardening:** Decide: if pad-block:pad-inline should be φ, use `1.618`. If √φ (a gentler step), keep `1.272` and fix the comment to "× √φ (the gentler φ-family rung)". As written the comment misdescribes the ratio.
