## TOP REFUTATION (load-bearing) — the spike DE-RISKS the cast on `::after`, the spec FORBIDS it

§3 states, correctly and as the load-bearing pseudo-element fact: *"The follow-through cast rides an
inert `aria-hidden` `.cartoon-cast` child (the cartoon-shadow H2 lesson — `::before`/`::after` are
OCCUPIED by the glass catch-light + grain)."* I confirmed this in `src/`: `glass/material.css`
occupies BOTH `::before` (moving-specular catch-light, AW.W22) AND `::after` (grain overlay + folded
rim) on every `.glass-material`/glass-family element. The `.glass-atom` body composes
`.glass-capsule` → it inherits that occupancy.

**But `golden/spike.html:80` puts the cel cast on `.fill::after`** —
`box-shadow: 5px 4px 0 0 var(--cartoon-ink)` with the lag `translateX` on the SAME pseudo. The
spike's `.fill` is a bare standalone div with NO `.glass-material` underneath, so the `::after` is
free and the spike "passes." **The spike therefore de-risks the EASY case and silently skips the
exact collision the spec calls load-bearing.** It proves "compositor-only transform lag is possible"
— which was never in doubt — and proves NOTHING about the inert-child path that ships. The two
boldest mechanisms (cast-lag on a glass body, arc-stagger on glass pucks) are validated on
non-glass divs; the moment the cast rides a real `.glass-capsule` the spike's `::after` recipe is
illegal. This is the gap a USER hits first: the slider fill IS a glass cylinder, and its cast cannot
live where the spike put it.

**Hardening:** re-cut the spike so `.fill` and `.puck` carry a `.glass-material`-equivalent
`::before`+`::after` (grain + specular) and the cel cast rides a THIRD inert `<i class="cartoon-cast"
aria-hidden>` child exactly as §3 ships — proving the lag/overshoot survive when the pseudos are
already spent AND that a third compositor layer doesn't blow the paint-fence. Until then, mark §3's
"inert child" path as UN-SPIKED in the GATE preamble (A5/A8 currently cite spike numbers that were
measured on the wrong DOM shape).

## Secondary refutations + hardening

**R2 — A4's "warm-not-gray" is asserted on the TOKEN, but the USER sees the COMPOSITE (which is
already not-black).** The slider fill is `oklab(0.216 …/0.88)` over `backdrop-filter: blur(8px)
saturate(1.4) brightness(1.02)`. The `saturate(1.4)` pulls the transmitted field's chroma through;
the painted composite over a warm host is a dark-amber-tinted glass, NOT the "black fill" the
GESTALT bar (§5: *"the slider's black fill … dies"*) claims. As a user the current slider is
*muddy/dark*, not *gray* and not *black* — the verdict over-dramatizes to manufacture a cleaner
kill. The real defect is **L too low + chroma too low**, not "black." If A4 tests only the
`background-color` token C it will RED today and falsely-GREEN the day someone sets a saturated-dark
fill that is STILL too dark to read warm over the field.
**Hardening:** A4 must test the PAINTED composite over the real field (same `drawImage`+`getImageData`
method A1 already mandates), with BOTH an L floor (the fill must lift, ≥ some warm-mid L) AND a C
floor — not the isolated `getComputedStyle().backgroundColor`. Re-word §5 "black fill" → "dark
low-chroma muddy fill." Precision, not theater.

**R3 — "morph MORE the faster you move" has NO upper governor → a fast flick reads as a glitch, not
weight.** §B.2 + spike map velocity 0..1 to `scaleX(1 + v·0.06)` linearly. A real heavy object does
NOT deform unboundedly with speed — past a threshold it *resists* (mass dominates). Linear
unbounded smear at high `--atom-drag-v` is the *tight-springy* failure mode inverted: a rubber-band,
not a weight. The spike's `vel = Math.max(vel, …)` even RATCHETS velocity up and only decays at
0.86/frame, so a fast flick pins the smear near max and holds it — visually a stuck stretch.
**Hardening:** cap the displacement with an ease/asymptote (`v_eff = tanh(k·v)` or a clamp at ~0.7)
so the smear *saturates* — the object loads, then mass wins. Add a GATE assertion: `scaleX` delta is
monotone-increasing but BOUNDED (a high-velocity sample is < 1.3× a mid-velocity sample, not 2×).
That is the difference between weight and elastic.

**R4 — the ARC `rotate` is unbounded per-index and clips the cluster's gestalt at N>4.** Spike:
`rotate(calc(var(--i) * 2.4deg))` and `translateY(--i²·-1.1px)`. At index 5 that is 12° + 27.5px
lift; a 7-avatar stack fans into a visibly *crooked, tilting-away* hand — past golden proportion
into chaos, and the parabola `--i²` makes the back pucks LEAVE the visual band. The spec says "like
a dealt hand of cards" but a dealt hand fans on a SHARED pivot (constant angular step from one
origin), not per-puck local rotation that compounds. The current math is a per-element tilt, which
is the cheaper, less-golden read.
**Hardening:** clamp the fan to a fixed total sweep (e.g. ≤ 18° across the whole cluster regardless
of N → `rotate(calc(var(--i) / var(--n) * 18deg))`) and bound the arc lift, so the gestalt holds its
√φ proportion at 3 OR 9 avatars. Add the `--n` count to the consumer. A GATE arm: the outermost
puck's rotation is independent of N.

**R5 — the loud-register cel cast is OPT-IN (`data-cast`), which under-delivers the cartoon-punch
edict for the DEFAULT badge.** The binding law elevates §L4 cartoon shadowing *toward universal*;
the GOLDEN gates the loud pill's cast behind `data-cast`/hover "a status pill is information, not a
card." Defensible for KISS — but a USER on `/display/badge` sees FOUR flat saturated pills with no
layered-offset shadow at all by default; the "1940s technicolor bold layered-offset shadow" register
is INVISIBLE on the page's headline atoms unless a consumer opts in. The two-register split is
correct; making the cel cast *default-off on the loud register* means the cartoon-punch edict is
satisfied only in the QUIET register + on hover — the opposite of "toward universal."
**Hardening:** keep `surface="glass"` quiet, but make the loud register cast a **soft idle cel by
default** (the warm `--shadow-cartoon-sm`, already the §L4 medium principle) with `data-cast`
*intensifying* it rather than introducing it. The pill gets its defined edge + a whisper of cel at
rest; `data-cast` is the loud upgrade. That moves the register toward universal without making every
status badge a sticker.

**R6 (minor) — the GATE's self-test bites are asserted but the spike doesn't run them.** A1
("MUST fail on a flat-base field, PASS on the gradient field"), A8 ("a planted animated-box-shadow
MUST red") are the right anti-fraud guards, but they live only in prose; the spike has no harness
arm exercising them. Given the recurring "live-π false-FAIL / getComputedStyle returns oklab()"
lesson, the A1 composite read must parse `oklab()`/`color(srgb …)` outputs (the slider fill is
already emitted as `oklab(...)`, the `+N` as `color(srgb …)`) — a naive hex parse will throw.
**Hardening:** state in the GATE that the painted-composite reader handles `oklab()` + `color(srgb)`
+ `oklch()` serializations (cite the paint-arm.mjs precedent), and ship the two self-test fields as
fixtures in the spec, not prose.

  K5 fix is a token swap, not a filter. Honest WebKit parity claim.

  glass/punch arms until DEPENDs merge. The DEPENDs are real files. No inflation.
- **PRM/PRT carve** — `--motion-weight:0` zeroes the train in one assignment; warm floor + rim +
  static cast persist; `useDockHold` byte-preserved. Sound.

## Bottom line

SURVIVES. The architecture is fit and the honesty is real — this is not a bolt-on, not gray-glass
fraud, not legacy. The one thing a maintainer must NOT skip: **the spike validates the cast/arc on
the wrong DOM shape** (free `::after` vs the spec's occupied-pseudo + inert-child reality). Re-spike
on a real glass body before trusting A5/A8. Then bound the smear (R3) and the arc (R4) so "weight"
doesn't degrade into "rubber-band," lift the slider-fill test to the painted composite (R2), and
give the loud register an idle cel (R5) to actually honor cartoon-punch-toward-universal.
