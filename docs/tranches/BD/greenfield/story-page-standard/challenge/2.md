## R1 (LANDS — TOP REFUTATION) — the field path is NOT sRGB-pinned; the warm→`transparent` stop GRAYS the field in BOTH engines, WORSE in Safari

The §4 cross-engine section asserts: *"sRGB interp pinned (the spike pins `background-color` so the
Safari-default-oklab interpolation does not muddy the warm stops)."* **This is false on two counts,
verified live.**

1. **`background-color` does NOT govern gradient-stop interpolation.** It is a separate paint layer
   behind the gradients. The live computed `background-image` is:
   ```
   radial-gradient(60% 50% at 22% 18%, oklch(0.92 0.11 48), rgba(0,0,0,0) 70%), …
   conic-gradient(from 120deg at 50% 80%, oklch(0.95 0.07 92), oklch(0.92 0.11 48), …)
   ```
   There is **NO `in srgb` / `in oklch` interpolation hint** on ANY of the three gradients
   (`gradientHasInterpHint: false`, live). The "pin" is a no-op for the thing it claims to pin.

2. **Every radial stop fades warm → `transparent`, and `transparent` computes to `rgba(0,0,0,0)`**
   (live, in the computed image). Interpolating `oklch(warm)` → transparent-BLACK without a `in srgb`
   premultiplied hint runs the midtones through low-lightness, low-chroma territory — the classic
   "transparent-black fade muddies to gray" trap. Chrome's default for unhinted gradients with mixed
   `oklch`+sRGB stops and Safari's default (oklab) **diverge**, and BOTH push the warm field toward a
   muddy edge precisely where the §3 "defined edge" is supposed to live. This is the SAME gray-glass
   root cause the page-background GOLDEN's "honest-raster" lesson exists to catch — the spike's own
   field path re-commits it.

   Visually (Chrome light screenshot): the field reads as a near-uniform flat amber wash with low
   structural variation; the glass-cel transmit-delta is faint. The "COLORFUL FIELD with a DEFINED
   EDGE" (§3 law) is not achieved — it is a smooth single-hue gradient, not a structured field, and
   the warm→transparent fades are exactly where it loses chroma.

**HARDENING (R1):**
- Add an explicit `in oklch` (or `in oklch longer hue` for the conic) interpolation hint to **every**
  gradient in the field path, AND replace the bare `transparent` end-stops with a **chroma-preserving
  warm transparent** — `oklch(0.92 0.11 58 / 0)` (same hue/chroma, zero alpha) instead of
  `transparent`/`rgba(0,0,0,0)`. This keeps the fade IN the warm wedge in both engines and kills the
  gray edge. (This is binding for the page-background GOLDEN it DEPENDS on; restate it here as an
  acceptance precondition so the demo-side host cannot inherit a gray field.)
- The S2/S8 gate must raster the FADE REGION (the radial→transparent boundary), not just the warm
  core, or it false-passes a field that is warm at center and gray at the edges. Add an assert:
  **tealFrac 0 AND grayFrac 0 across the full field raster (every 16×16 tile C ≥ 0.03)**, not just
  meanC over the whole plane.

## R2 (LANDS) — the cel-slam NEVER fires for above-the-fold content — the entire FIRST SCREEN (incl. the protagonist STAGE) loads static

`animation-range: entry 0% entry 42%` on a `view()` timeline only animates an element **as it crosses
into the viewport**. An element **already in the viewport at load is PAST its entry range → it paints
at the terminal frame (no slam).** Live on the spike (1440×806):

| cel | top | above fold? | slams on LOAD? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — the protagonist STAGE** | 313 | YES | **NO** |
| 1 — specimen | 819 | no | yes (on scroll) |
| 2 — matrix | 1010 | no | yes |
| 3 — interaction | 1381 | no | yes |

So the headline claim — *"the page paints itself like a 1940s technicolor cel sequence flipping into
place — the most ALIVE storybook in the set"* (§1, §7.5) — is **FALSE for the entire first screen the
user actually sees on load, including the φ² protagonist.** The cel-slam is a SCROLL effect mislabeled
as a LOAD entrance. §0 even grades "alive entrance" GREEN-ish citing the shipped `.scroll-build` —
which is a MOUNT clock — then the mechanism §2d/§4 makes `view()` the PRIMARY and `.scroll-build` only
the *fallback*. That inverts which mechanism owns the load entrance.

**HARDENING (R2):**
- The LOAD entrance must be the mount-clock register (`.scroll-build` / `.scroll-cascade`'s
  on-mount keyframe stagger with `--i` index) for cels **in the initial viewport**; the `view()`
  timeline is the *scroll-reveal* layer for cels below the fold. Two roles, not one — make the
  primary on-load animation a mount stagger, and gate the `view()` cel-slam to `animation-range`
  that starts where the fold ends. (The shipped `.scroll-build` already does on-mount; consume it
  as the PRIMARY, not the fallback.)
- The S6 gate as written ("a frame-series shows each cel building on the punch arc") must specify it
  asserts the **on-LOAD** build of the above-the-fold protagonist, captured from `t=0` of paint —
  otherwise it green-passes a page whose first screen is frozen.

## R3 (LANDS) — on the Safari installed base the BOLDEST motion (punch arc · lagging cast · overlap) simply DOES NOT EXIST

`animation-timeline: view()` shipped in **Safari 26** (late-2025). In mid-2026 a large fraction of the
Safari installed base is ≤ 18.x, where `@supports (animation-timeline: view())` is FALSE → the whole
§2d block (cel-slam, the cast-lag `::after`, the overlapping stagger) is **skipped**, falling to the
plain `.scroll-build` fade. So on most Safari today the page has **NO punch arc, NO lagging cast, NO
overlapping action** — the three cartoon-law mechanisms the golden sells as its keystone (lens-c). The
golden frames this as a graceful "byte-comparable both engines" fallback, but it is not byte-comparable:
it is the *complete absence of the headline aesthetic* on a major engine. That violates the BINDING LAW
"liquid animations PERFECT in CHROME *and* SAFARI" — a plain fade is not the punch arc.

**HARDENING (R3):**
- Re-floor the cartoon punch + lagging cast onto a **`@supports`-independent** mechanism for the LOAD
  entrance: a pure on-mount `@keyframes` stagger with `--i` (works on every engine back to Safari 15)
  carrying the SAME squash→overshoot→settle arc + the cast-lag (a second `@keyframes` on the `::after`
  with `animation-delay` offset, no timeline needed). Reserve `view()` ONLY for the scroll-reveal
  enhancement. Then the punch/cast EXIST on all engines; `view()` just upgrades the scroll behavior.
- The paired-engine π (§6) must explicitly capture a **WebKit-WITHOUT-view() matrix** (emulate the
  `@supports` false branch), assert the punch arc + cast lag are STILL present via the mount clock,
  and FAIL if the WebKit capture shows a plain fade. Otherwise the gate green-passes the degraded path.

## R4 (hardening) — the DOUBLE cartoon shadow: the cel AND its cast both paint `--shadow-cartoon`

`.demo-cel { box-shadow: var(--shadow-cartoon) }` (spike L135) AND
`.demo-cel::after { box-shadow: var(--shadow-cartoon) }` (L184). Both are outset (`4px 5px 0`). At rest
(and on every engine where the cast doesn't animate — see R3) there are **TWO identical offset shadows
stacked** = a doubled/heavier cast than designed, and the "weight follows the body" read only works
during the brief lag window on Chrome. On the static/PRM/non-view() path it's just a doubled shadow.

**HARDENING:** the cel itself must carry NO `box-shadow` when the cast `::after` is active (the cast IS
the shadow). Move the static shadow ONTO the `::after` only; the cel's own `box-shadow` applies solely
on the `@supports not (animation-timeline: view())` / PRM branch (one or the other paints the cast,
never both).

## R5 (hardening) — `will-change: transform` PERMANENT on the field `::before` + `contain: strict` on a 118-route universal mount = a per-route compositor-layer + paint cost

The field `::before` carries `will-change: transform` + a 42s infinite `field-drift`. `will-change`
promotes a permanent compositor layer for the LIFE of every page; combined with the saturate(1.05)
`filter` (a filter layer) this is a non-trivial per-route GPU cost on a backdrop that is mounted on ALL
118 routes behind glass that ALSO compositor-blurs it. On a `DemoStage` route that ALSO runs an
`<Aurora field>` GL canvas, the field mesh + GL + multiple `backdrop-filter` cels can blow the
one-GL-per-route budget into jank. The golden never measures field-mount FPS.

**HARDENING:** drop the permanent `will-change` (let the compositor promote on-demand, or scope
`will-change` to the drift only via a `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` and remove it
once-settled); the `saturate()` filter belongs as a gradient-stop chroma bake, not a runtime filter
layer. Add a **perf assert** to the gate: the field-mount route holds ≥ 55fps during scroll with N
backdrop-filter cels on screen (Chrome trace), and the `DemoStage`-with-Aurora route does not stack
field-mesh + GL + heavy backdrop simultaneously (the GL field REPLACES the CSS mesh on that route,
per §2b's stated "one-GL-per-route budget" — make it a gate, not prose).

## R8 (hardening) — MEATBALL: correctly carved OUT, but the carve has a hole on the `DemoStage` host

§4 correctly keeps goo/`filter:url()`/metaball OUT of the FRAME (no naive ellipsoids in the chassis —
good, this is the right call and survives). BUT a `DemoStage` HOSTS the dock-fission/blob goo as
content, and that goo uses a static-SVG `filter:url()` metaball. The chassis puts a `backdrop-filter:
blur()` glass `Card` AROUND that goo content. **`backdrop-filter` on an ancestor of an SVG-`filter:url`
goo is a known WebKit compositing hazard** — the filter region + the backdrop region can mis-composite
(the goo's `filter:url` output may not feed the parent's backdrop sample correctly in WebKit). The
golden asserts "the FRAME carries ZERO goo" but doesn't address the FRAME-WRAPS-GOO nesting.

**HARDENING:** the `DemoStage` stage-canvas that hosts an SVG-goo demo must NOT sit inside the cel's
`backdrop-filter` blur context — give the goo content its own non-backdrop sub-surface (the glass cel
frames it, the goo paints on an opaque/own-isolation inner stage), and add a paired-engine assert that
a goo-hosting `DemoStage` composites identically Chrome↔Safari (the meatball merge stays a real
blob↔meatball, no ellipsoid fallback, no backdrop bleed).

## R9 (hardening) — `prefers-reduced-transparency` path is asserted but NOT in the gate; the field-chroma gate may CONFLICT with it

§5 says PRT drops `--field-intensity: 0` → solid floor + opaque glass tier. Good in prose. But the S2
gate demands the field raster C ≥ 0.045 warm — under PRT that field is INTENTIONALLY desaturated to a
solid. The gate has no PRT branch, so either it's not run under PRT (untested path) or it would FALSE-
FAIL the correct PRT behavior.

**HARDENING:** add an explicit PRT gate arm: under `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` the field
collapses to the warm-solid floor (C may drop but stays warm-tinted, NOT gray — assert H ∈ [25,95]
still, C ≥ 0.012), the glass cels go opaque-warm, and conformity (structure/heading/caption) survives.
PRM and PRT are SEPARATE gate arms, both required.
