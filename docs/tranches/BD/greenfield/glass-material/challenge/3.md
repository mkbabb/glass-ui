## R1 — THE KILL: the spike DISPROVES the golden's own ≥0.018 bar (self-refuting de-risk)

The golden's §10 acceptance #2 is binding: *"The glass TRANSMITS the field tinted warm —
composited C ≥ 0.018 over the field."* §7 claims the spike proves *"the field-vs-flat delta is
real (over-field C measurably > over-flat C)."*

**Live readback from the spike's OWN `#rb` element (chrome-devtools, both modes):**

| mode | spike "plate OVER FIELD" | golden bar | "plate OVER FLAT" (the gray read) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| light | **C 0.0077** H 78.2° | ≥ 0.018 | C 0.0013 H 84.0° | **FAILS bar by 2.3×** |
| dark | **C 0.0051** H 69.6° | ≥ 0.018 | C 0.0018 H 68.6° | **FAILS bar by 3.5×** |

The de-risk artifact the golden cites as proof of the boldest mechanism **prints a number
that fails the golden's own acceptance bar by 2–3.5×, in both modes.** A spike that "de-risks"
a mechanism by producing a sub-bar result has *de-risked nothing* — it has surfaced that the
0.018 target is not reachable by the §3/§4 recipe as drawn. §7 then hand-waves this exact gap:
*"The exact composited C ≥ 0.018 bar is a CALIBRATION the live gate tunes against."* That is
the tell: the headline acceptance number is **uncalibrated and currently unmet by the only
evidence in the corpus.** Per the live-verify-capture lesson, a "live-verified spike" whose
captured number contradicts the spec is a born-RED on the SPEC, not a green.

**Why it fails (the design-fidelity root):** the plate is `--card` at 0.65α (L≈0.97). A 65%
warm-but-near-white plate over ANY field washes the field's chroma to ~⅓. To reach C 0.018
through a 0.65α near-white plate the FIELD would need C ≈ 0.05+ *at the plate's lightness* —
but the golden itself (§3) concedes chroma "NOT the gamut-bound L 0.98 plate." The plate L and
the field chroma fight; the spike is the proof they lose. **The transmissive read the user
demands (the Maps-card bleed) does not emerge from a near-white 0.65α plate over a pastel
field. It reads as faintly-warm cream — i.e. the gray-ish read, one notch warmer.**

## R2 — THE SECOND KILL: the §8 born-RED gate samples the ACHROMATIC base, not the field

§8 F2 (the gate's heart, the code block lines 342–362) reads the field colour via:
```ts
return { fieldBg: getComputedStyle(behind).backgroundColor, ... }
```
**`backgroundColor` resolves ONLY the last solid layer — `--field-base` / `--neutral-0`. The
entire warm chroma plenum lives in `background-IMAGE` (the three `radial-gradient` stops),
which `getComputedStyle().backgroundColor` NEVER resolves.** Confirmed live on the spike:

```
field_computed_bg  : rgb(251, 250, 248)         ← the FLAT KEEP-NEUTRAL base, C 0.0029 H 84.6
field_bg_image_head: radial-gradient(... oklch(0.96 0.045 72 ...   ← where the warmth ACTUALLY lives
HONEST composite plate-over-field-BASE: C 0.0013  H 84.0   ← IDENTICAL to "plate OVER FLAT, the gray read"
```

So the gate, run exactly as written, composites the plate over `rgb(251,250,248)` and gets
**C 0.0013 — byte-identical to the spike's own labelled "the gray read."** The §8 gate is not
born-RED-then-GREEN; it is **born-RED-and-STAYS-RED** (it can never see the gradient chroma it
demands), OR a future fixer silently swaps the sample method and the bar is whatever the new
method happens to read. Either way the gate **does not measure the relationship it claims to
gate.** This is precisely the source-green/visually-broken hole the golden says it closes —
re-opened inside the gate itself. A gate that samples the wrong layer is the AZ close-class
failure, not its cure.

## R4 — DRY/ARCHITECTURE: the §4(A) conic rim COLLIDES with the occupied `::before`/`::after` slots

§4(A) claims the directional conic rim "composes the EXISTING `::before` specular slot" via
`mask-composite: exclude`. **Live source (`src/styles/glass/material.css`) shows the
`::before` is ALREADY a fully-painted moving-specular catch-light** — a radial catch-light disc
with TWO compositing `background` layers, pointer-tracked, transition-wired (lines 75–235). And
`::after` is ALREADY the grain overlay that *also* carries the rim inset ring (rim.css comment:
"the grain `::after` gets the rim inset ring"). **Both pseudo-elements are occupied.**

A `mask-composite: exclude` border-ring requires the `::before`'s ENTIRE `background` to be the
conic ring and the mask to clip it to a 1.5px frame — which **destroys the radial catch-light
disc currently on that `::before`.** You cannot host both a masked border-ring AND an unmasked
radial specular disc on one pseudo-element. The golden needs a THIRD compositing layer it
claims not to add ("zero new compositing path," "zero new layer" §1/§2). That claim is false:
the keyed conic rim is a new layer, OR it evicts the shipped moving-specular catch-light (layer
4 in the golden's own §2 table) — a regression on the iOS-26 pointer-specular the suite ships.
**KISS/DRY refuted; the "rides EXISTING layers" reconciliation (§1) does not hold against the
actual occupied slots.**

## R6 — GESTALT/PROPORTION: no √φ proportion, no cartoon-punch, in the material itself

The binding law demands AUDACIOUS √φ typography and CARTOON punch (anticipation, squash &
stretch, layered-offset shadows) as UNIVERSAL. The golden's material spec:
- **No golden-ratio anywhere in the material geometry.** Rim 1.5px, blur 10–13, cast 14px/28px,

- **The "cartoon cel" is a static rim + a static cast.** §4 calls rim+cast-agree-on-one-key "a

  DEFAULT material has no punch, contradicting "elevate §L4 weak/medium toward universal." The
  field drift (38s ease-in-out) is calm-correct but is the OPPOSITE of cartoon punch. As a user,
  the cel does not read as a 1940s technicolor object; it reads as a calm flat panel with a
  consistent shadow.

## R7 — CROSS-ENGINE: the Safari claims are asserted, not spike-verified; one is likely wrong

§6/§7 claim "verified live in Chrome AND Safari" and "`CSS.supports` confirms." The spike header
says "Verified live in Chrome AND Safari at this path," but **the only captures in the golden dir
are `spike-light/-tuned/-dark-tuned.png` — all Chrome; there is NO WebKit capture, no paired-π
artifact.** Per the cardinal §L7 bar the golden itself invokes, "Safari-parity" with zero WebKit
capture is an unbacked claim. Two specific cross-engine hazards the golden glosses:
- **`conic-gradient(from calc(var(--glass-key) + 90deg), ...)` with a registered `<angle>` custom
  property driving the `from` angle** — WebKit's `conic-gradient` `from <calc>` angle interpolation
  has historically lagged; a `@property`-typed angle feeding `from calc()` is exactly the kind of
  seam that paints in Chromium and silently flattens to the 0deg default in older WebKit. Untested.
- **`mask-composite: exclude` + `-webkit-mask-composite: xor`** on a tier that ALSO must show the
  radial catch-light (R4) — the two-mask border-ring idiom is Safari-safe in isolation, but the
  golden never tests it composed with the existing `::before` background, which is the actual
  integration. "Safari-safe by construction" is asserted, not measured.

## R8 — A11Y / reduce-transparency: the edge floor's contrast claim is unverified

§6 claims the keyed rim "DOUBLES as the reduce-transparency legibility anchor" and F3 asserts
"the rim's lit-edge luminance exceeds the page luminance by a min ΔL." But the rim's lit stop is
`hsl(0 0% 100% / 0.6)` over a warm-cream plate over a warm-cream field — **white-on-cream is a
LOW ΔL pair in light mode.** The golden never states the ΔL value or proves it clears any WCAG
non-text-contrast bar (1.4.11, 3:1 for UI component boundaries). A 0.6α white rim on an L≈0.97
plate is sub-3:1 — the very "vanishes cream-on-cream" failure the golden diagnoses at §0 for the
5%-ink border, reproduced at the rim. The defined-edge floor may not actually define an edge in
light mode for low-vision users; unverified and design-suspect.
