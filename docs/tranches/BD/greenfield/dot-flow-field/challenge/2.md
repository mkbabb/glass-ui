# CHALLENGE #2 — dot-flow-field "AURORA CURRENT" GOLDEN

**Lens:** cross-engine (Chrome+Safari) · perf · naive-ellipsoid/meatball · a11y/PRM · overfit.
**Disposition:** SURVIVES — but **CONDITIONALLY**, with two refutations that LAND hard (one
cross-engine, one precept) and must be answered before the amendment is built. The core mechanism
(advection + trail-feedback + vortex) is sound and live-verified to paint flowing braided streaks
in Chrome; the GOLDEN is NOT a fraud. But its central de-risking claim is **Chrome-only dressed in
Safari language**, and its headline "technicolor" gate **false-greens on a monochrome-amber field**.

All findings are LIVE (chrome-devtools-mcp), both the spike and the real route, with canvas readback.

## TOP REFUTATION (R-A) — "Safari-real" is a Chrome-only verification. The §8 de-risk is INFLATED.

The GOLDEN's risk-retirement rests entirely on §8: *"VERIFIED LIVE … the path Safari actually
runs … green on the WebGL2 / Safari-real path."* This is the cardinal **live-verify inflation**
the user keeps flagging (`feedback_live_verify_capture`, the cardinal-lesson note).

- The ONLY artifact is `golden/spike-capture.png` — captured in **Chrome via DevTools MCP** (§8's
  own words). There is **NO** Safari screenshot, **NO** WebKit run, **NO** paired-engine artifact
  anywhere under the golden dir (verified: only `spike.html` + `spike-capture.png`). "Safari-real
  path" is a euphemism for "WebGL2, which Safari also speaks" — but **WebGL2-in-Chrome ≠ WebKit.**
- The fragile cross-engine bits the spike leans on are exactly the ones WebKit historically breaks
  and that a Chrome run CANNOT exercise:
  - **`RGBA32F`-renderable** (the state-texture ping-pong's format) is the *historically fragile*
    one on iOS/older WebKit — `EXT_color_buffer_float` exposes it but iOS GPUs have shipped it
    incomplete. The spike's entire particle population dies if `RGBA32F` is not color-renderable.
  - **`OES_texture_half_float_linear` is FALSE even in Chrome here** (live-probed). So RGBA16F can
    only be NEAREST-sampled. The spike gets away with NEAREST on the trail, but the GOLDEN §4 calls
    the trail a "screen-res texture" — any LINEAR sample (a resize-rescale, a future blur-blit)
    silently degrades or no-ops on WebKit. This is an undocumented landmine.
  - **`premultipliedAlpha:false` + `blendFunc(ONE,ONE)` additive** color management differs subtly
    Chrome↔WebKit at composite; the warm-floor blit `floorc + c` can clip/halo differently.
- **R6 (paired-engine WebKit parity) is SPECCED BUT NEVER RUN.** The gate that would actually
  de-risk Safari is deferred to the build. So the GOLDEN's "boldest mechanism is de-risked" is
  **false as stated** — it is de-risked in Chrome, hypothesized in Safari.

**HARDENING (R-A):** Before the amendment is greenlit, run `spike.html` in **real WebKit**
(`playwright webkit` or Safari Technology Preview) and capture a paired Chrome|WebKit artifact +
the four π numbers from BOTH. Explicitly probe `checkFramebufferStatus` on RGBA32F **and** RGBA16F
on WebKit and gate a graceful fallback: if `RGBA32F` is not renderable, encode particle state in
**RGBA16F** (half-float, sufficient for `[-1.8,1.8]` positions + age) or a packed-RGBA8 fixed-point
state — never assume RGBA32F. Pin the trail to NEAREST in the spec (no LINEAR sample). Re-word §8
to "de-risked in Chromium; WebKit pending R6" until the WebKit capture exists.

## R-B — the "technicolor" gate FALSE-GREENS on a monochrome-amber field. Precept violation.

The headline lever is *"a bold 4-stop technicolor ladder — slow magenta-violet → electric cyan →
hot amber → near-white bloom."* Live hue-histogram of the spike's lit pixels (readback, 128×72):

| hue bucket | share of lit |
|---|---|
| **white** (R,G,B all high — blowout) | **67%** |
| **amber** (warm) | **33%** |
| cyan | **0%** |
| magenta | **0%** |

The field is **amber + white. The cool half of the ramp — the magenta and cyan stops, the visually
distinctive ones — is ENTIRELY ABSENT.** The screenshot confirms it: a tangled mass of sepia/amber
threads on near-black — a **plate of brown noodles**, not "ribbons of amber/cyan/magenta light."

`meanChroma 21.8` PASSES the `>18` gate anyway, because **amber alone has high `max−min` chroma.**
The gate measures *saturation*, not *hue-variety* — it is gameable by a single warm hue and does
**NOT** enforce the technicolor precept (the 1940s-technicolor PUNCH, the audacious vivid-field
law). This is the SAME failure mode the GOLDEN charges the current viz with ("monochrome warm") —
the spike is **also effectively monochrome warm, just brighter.** The GOLDEN's own §8 calibration
note ("chroma sits at the floor because the curl field is slow-dominated… a tuning lever") admits
the symptom but mislabels it: it is not a chroma-magnitude tuning problem, it is a **hue-collapse
architecture problem** — the curl speed distribution is unimodal, so motes never sample the ramp
ends, so two of four stops never paint. Pushing `speedScale`/saturation makes the amber MORE
saturated, not more multi-hued. The "tuning lever, not architecture change" claim is **wrong.**

**HARDENING (R-B):** (1) Replace the gate-R4 `meanChromaOfLit > 18` with a **hue-spread** metric:
bin lit-pixel hue into ≥3 buckets and require each of ≥2 non-warm buckets to clear a floor (e.g.
cyan-share + magenta-share each ≥ 8% of lit) — a single-hue field REDs. (2) Fix the architecture:
either widen the speed→hue input so the ramp ends are reached (normalize hue against the *observed*
speed quantiles, not raw `|V|`), OR drive hue off a **second field axis** (curl-direction angle, or
a low-freq spatial hue field) so adjacent streamlines carry different hues regardless of speed —
the only way to get genuine amber/cyan/magenta braiding. (3) Tame the 67% white blowout (additive
saturation): the near-white stop is eating the field; cap additive accumulation or pull the 4th
stop back so highlights read as *hot cores*, not a white wash.

## R-C — "deft UNION, no fork" overstates it: the WebGL2 path is a GROUND-UP new renderer.

§3 sells the rebuild as extending one substrate, reusing primitives. Verified against `src/`:

- The current WebGL2 flow path is a **single fullscreen-triangle fragment shader**
  (`flow-field.glsl.ts`: `in vec2 aPosition` / `in vec2 vUv`; `useFlowParticles.ts:448`
  `gl.drawArrays(gl.TRIANGLES, 0, 3)`). There is **no particle population on WebGL2 today** — it
  evaluates the field as a screen image. The WGPU path has a particle buffer but "NO advection, NO
  re-seed" (its own comment, line 103).
- The GOLDEN replaces this with: a **GPGPU state-texture ping-pong** (advect pass) + **two RGBA16F
  trail FBOs** + **point-sprite instanced draw** (`gl_VertexID`, `gl.POINTS`). That is **three new
  passes, two new FBO pairs, and a new draw topology** — net-new to the codebase: `grep` for
  `EXT_color_buffer_float|RGBA16F|RGBA32F|HALF_FLOAT` across `src/` returns **ZERO**. No viz in the
  library uses float render targets today. This is not "RE-INVENT only the broken parts" — for
  WebGL2 it is a **from-scratch GPGPU renderer** the "deft union" framing hides.
- **Substrate lifecycle gap (real integration cost):** the consumer gets ONE `frame(t)` hook +
  `resize`/`teardown` (`useWebGLCanvas.ts`). The trail/state FBOs must be **re-created on every
  `webglcontextrestored` AND every `resize`** (screen-res targets) — the spike rebuilds trails on
  resize (`mkTrails()`), but the substrate's restore path calls `setup(gl)` fresh, so the FBO pair,
  the float-extension re-probe, and the ping-pong re-seed all need explicit teardown/rebuild wiring
  the GOLDEN's integration checklist (§7) does not enumerate. The §7 line "wire the trail ping-pong
  into the existing `onFrame`" undersells a multi-FBO, resize-sensitive, restore-sensitive
  sub-renderer.

**HARDENING (R-C):** Re-label the disposition honestly: WebGL2 is a **RE-INVENT** of the flow path
(the fullscreen-fragment evaluator is retired for `flow`-mode, kept only for `field`-mode — state
that no-legacy clean break explicitly). Add to §7: (a) FBO lifecycle owned in `setup`/`teardown`
(rebuild on restore + resize), (b) a float-extension capability probe with the R-A fallback, (c)
a named `proof:webgl-substrate-single` check that the new float-target path doesn't violate the
single-webgl2-creator clause. Reuse-claim audit: name EXACTLY which primitives survive byte-for-
byte (`curlFBM`, `samplePaletteLin`, `usePointerVelocityField`, the picker) vs which are new (the
entire GPGPU + trail + point-sprite stack) so the "union" isn't rhetorical.

## R-D — perf: 16384 motes × 60fps is the spike's *idle* cost; the field-of-trails has a fillrate wall.

The spike ran 16384 points at 60fps in Chrome — but:

- The trail is a **screen-resolution RGBA16F ping-pong**, decayed+composited **every frame**. At
  the route's live canvas `2066×920` (measured) on DPR 2, that is a **full-screen-res float texture
  read + write + a second decay-blit + the final floor blit = 3 full-screen float passes/frame**,
  before the 16384 additive point-sprites. On an iGPU / iOS GPU this is a **fillrate wall**, not a
  vertex-count wall — the spike's "16384 at 60fps" measures the cheap axis (verts) and hides the
  expensive one (float fillrate). No perf number exists for a 4K display or a thermally-throttled
  iPad — the targets the user actually flags (iOS-27 reference is an iPad).
- The GOLDEN adds a **WebGPU-only cartoon-cast dual-billboard shadow** ("the WebGPU more"). So the
  two engines render a **different gestalt** (WebGPU has shadows, WebGL2 doesn't) — contradicting
  §1.2's own claim "the gestalt is identical on both engines." Either the shadow is parity-breaking
  (drop the claim) or it's load-bearing (then WebGL2 is degraded, a precept gap on the path most
  users see, per lens-c's own "WebGL2 is the real path" correction — which the GOLDEN then ignores
  for the headline cartoon-cast).

**HARDENING (R-D):** Gate the trail target to **half-res RGBA16F** (a blurred trail reads fine at
half-res and quarters the float fillrate), expose a `trailScale` lever, and capture a perf trace on
a throttled profile (CPU 4×/GPU-limited) + a real iPad if reachable. Resolve the §1.2 contradiction:
either bring a cheap directional-disc shadow to the WebGL2 point path (in-frag, free) so the gestalt
IS identical, or strike the "identical on both engines" claim and accept WebGL2 as the lesser-but-
complete register explicitly.

## R-E — a11y/PRM + ground/edge precepts: mostly sound, two gaps.

- **PRM:** "ONE static advected+trailed frame then park" is correct and the substrate supports it.
  But a **single** advected frame with a trail buffer that was never accumulated reads as **sparse
  dots, not ribbons** — the trail is a *temporal* accumulation; freezing at frame 0 yields no
  streaks. The PRM still-frame must be rendered after **N pre-rolled trail accumulation steps**
  (run the loop headless for ~30 frames into the trail buffer, THEN park) or PRM users see a dot
  scatter, not the gestalt. The GOLDEN doesn't specify the pre-roll.
- **`prefers-reduced-transparency`/`prefers-contrast:more`:** "brightness floors UP, trail-decay
  shortens" is good, but with R-B's 67%-white blowout already at baseline, *raising* brightness
  under `prefers-contrast:more` pushes further into white-clip — the opposite of legibility. The
  contrast arm must **increase hue-separation**, not luminance.
- **Ground/edge (§3 colorful-field law):** the spike shows a **centered square field with hard
  black margins** (lit columns 9→118 of 128 — ~14% dead margin each side), **no corner-bloom, no
  defined edge.** §2.1 specs the bloom+edge but the de-risk artifact doesn't carry it, so the §3
  "colorful field behind glass + defined edge" law is **unverified** for this viz. The full-bleed
  claim is contradicted by the spike's own framing (`p.x/aspect * 0.55` insets the field).

**HARDENING (R-E):** (1) PRM still-frame = pre-roll the trail ~30 frames then freeze. (2) the
contrast arm widens hue-separation + caps white, not raises luminance. (3) the de-risk capture
must show the full-bleed ground + corner-bloom + defined edge, or §2.1/§3 stays a claim; fix the
field-to-screen fit (`*0.55` insets — drop it for true full-bleed) and add the bloom to the blit.

## VERDICT

**SURVIVES — conditionally.** The mechanism is sound and Chrome-verified; the verdict on the
current viz is fair and the born-RED is real. But the GOLDEN ships **two real defects dressed as
strengths**: (R-A) a Chrome-only de-risk mislabeled "Safari-real," and (R-B) a "technicolor" gate
that false-greens on a monochrome-amber/white field. Both are precept violations
(cross-engine-perfect; vivid-technicolor) the user explicitly binds. R-C/R-D/R-E are integration,
perf, and precept hardenings that must land in the amendment. The design is **fit to REFINE, not
to ship as written** — the headline "de-risked" and "technicolor" claims do not survive their own
live evidence.
