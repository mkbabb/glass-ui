## What it builds

A detented sheet becomes a native snap scroller with no per-frame script.

- Its rungs are 1 px tall, which WebKit keeps.
- It commits its rung only at rest, on `scrollend`, and `v-model:detent` changes then.
- The scroller itself takes no pointer, and a device with no coarse pointer draws no grip.
- A side sheet enters by rule 1 with no shear; `--sheet-enter-skew` stays as a token at 0.
- 11.x keeps today's detent engine.

**One fault, stated** (B10-12). The Safari 27.3 beta fixed "scroll snap animation taking a long time to settle on iOS after flicking past the last snap position" (189473962). In every iOS 26 and 27.2 Safari, and in Home Assistant's WKWebView until iOS ships 27.3's engine, a sheet flicked past its last detent can be slow to settle, and `v-model:detent` waits for it. BM-10 ships with this stated, on choice 13's default. An iPhone reading on iOS 26 or 27.2 amends it, including whether a sheet whose top detent is the scroller's end meets the fault at all, and the owner may hold the wave for a 27.3 floor (OPEN §3).
