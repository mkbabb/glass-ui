## 1. Apple's Liquid Glass, by behaviour

### 1.1 The material

- **Lensing, not scattering.** "The primary way Liquid Glass visually defines itself is through something called Lensing." "Where as previous materials scattered light, this new set of materials dynamically bends, shapes, and concentrates light in real time." WWDC25 *Meet Liquid Glass*, chapter Dynamics (1:29), https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/
- **Refraction grows with size.** "When glass flexes and morphs to larger sizes – like when presenting a menu from a toolbar button – its material characteristics change to simulate a thicker, more substantial material. It casts deeper, richer shadows, has more pronounced lensing and refraction effects, and a softer scattering of light." 219, Adaptivity (6:00). UIKit says the same in opacity terms: "Glass adapts the appearance based on its size." "A larger size is more opaque." "A smaller size is clearer, and switches between light and dark mode automatically, to increase contrast." WWDC25 *Build a UIKit app with the new design*, 21:08–21:30, https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/
- **Two variants, never mixed.** Regular "blurs and adjusts the luminosity of background content"; clear "is highly translucent" and is for components "over visually rich backgrounds", with a dimming layer: "If the underlying content is bright, consider adding a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity." HIG Materials, https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials.md (change log: 2025-06-09 added, 2025-09-09 updated). "There are two to choose from: Regular and Clear. They should never be mixed." 219, Principles (10:31). Clear "does not have adaptive behaviors". 219, Principles.
- **Functional layer only.** "Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer." Exception: "sliders and toggles ... the element takes on a Liquid Glass appearance to emphasize its interactivity when a person activates it." "Use Liquid Glass effects sparingly." HIG Materials.
- **The layers.** Highlights: "Light sources inside of this environment shine on the material producing highlights that respond to geometry"; "in some cases, the lighting responds to device motion". Shadow: "increases the opacity of its shadow when it is over text ... lowers the opacity of its shadow when it is over a solid light background." Illumination: "When you interact with Liquid Glass, the material illuminates from within as a form of feedback." 219, Principles (10:31).
- **Light/dark flip by size.** "Small elements like navbars and tabbars ... flip from light to dark based on the background"; "Bigger elements, like menus or sidebars ... don't flip from light to dark. Their surface area is too big"; symbols on glass flip with it. 219, Adaptivity and Principles.

### 1.2 Materialization (how glass appears and leaves)

- "Instead of fading, Liquid Glass objects materialize in and out by gradually modulating the light bending and lensing." 219, Dynamics (1:29).
- UIKit: set `effectView.effect` inside an animation block to materialize; set it to `nil` to dematerialize; "Always prefer setting the effect property over the alpha". 284, 20:28 and 23:20.
- SwiftUI: `GlassEffectTransition.materialize` "will fade in content and animate in or out the glass material but will not attempt to match the geometry of any other glass effects"; `.matchedGeometry`; `.identity`. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glasseffecttransition.md. "The system applies more than opacity changes with the available transition types." https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views.md

### 1.3 Fusion and fission (merge, split, union)

- **Container.** `GlassEffectContainer` "combines multiple Liquid Glass shapes into a single shape that can morph individual shapes into one another"; "SwiftUI renders the effects together, improving rendering performance". iOS 26.0+. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glasseffectcontainer.md
- **Spacing threshold.** "As shapes near one another, their paths start to blend into one another. The higher the spacing, the sooner blending begins." (same page). The worked rule: the eraser morphs into the pencil "when the eraser's nearest edge is less than or equal to the container's spacing." "A spacing value on the container that's larger than the spacing of an interior HStack ... causes Liquid Glass effects to blend together at rest." Applying Liquid Glass to custom views (examples use spacing 40 and 20). UIKit: `UIGlassContainerEffect.spacing` sets "the distance at which they start affecting each other" (example `spacing = 20`). 284, 24:12.
- **Merge.** "As long as there is space between them, they appear as two separate views. Only if they get closer, they start merging like small droplets of water." "When animating into an overlapping frame, glass views combine into a single shape." 284, 23:52–24:27.
- **Split (fission).** "To split glass into multiple elements, first, add them to the same position without animation. Then, animate them out together!" 284, 24:33.
- **Union at rest.** `glassEffectUnion(id:namespace:)`: "All Liquid Glass effects with the same shape and Liquid Glass variant will be combined into a single shape." https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/glasseffectunion(id:namespace:).md
- **Identity morph.** `glassEffectID(_:in:)` with a `@Namespace`; "For effects you want to add or remove that are positioned within the container's assigned spacing, the default transition type is matchedGeometry"; use `materialize` for effects "farther from each other than the container's assigned spacing". Applying Liquid Glass to custom views. Badges expanding from a toolbar button and "re-absorbed gracefully". WWDC25 *Build a SwiftUI app with the new design*, 17:57, https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/323/
- **Glass cannot sample glass.** "This effect is achieved by sampling content from an area larger than itself." "However, glass can not sample other glass, so having nearby glass elements in different containers will result in inconsistent behavior." "Using a glass container allows these elements to share their sampling region". 323, 17:57. UIKit adds: the container "enforces a uniform adaptation". 284, 23:52.
- **Performance rule.** "Creating too many Liquid Glass effect containers and applying too many effects to views outside of containers can degrade performance. Limit the use of Liquid Glass effects onscreen at the same time." Applying Liquid Glass to custom views.

### 1.4 Interaction: the lens, the press, the stretch

- **Interactive glass.** `Glass.interactive(_:)` (iOS 26.0+), https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/glass/interactive(_:).md. "Glass reacts to user interaction by scaling, bouncing, and shimmering, matching the effect provided by toolbar buttons and sliders." 323, 17:57. UIKit: "When tapping the button, it scales and bounces." 284, 23:03.
- **Glow spreads to neighbours.** "Starting right under your fingertips, the glow spreads throughout the element and onto any Liquid Glass elements nearby". 219, Principles.
- **Gel and lift.** "Liquid Glass responds to interaction by instantly flexing and energizing with light." "It has an inherent gel-like flexibility ... as it moves in tandem with your interaction." "Elements can even lift up into Liquid Glass temporarily, such as when you interact with a component." 219, Dynamics.
- **Control thumbs become lenses.** "Control thumbs, like those on switch and segmentedControl, automatically have a new liquid glass appearance for interactions." "With sliders, in addition to the liquid glass effects on the thumb, they now preserve momentum and stretch when they are moved." 284, 17:24. "For controls like sliders and toggles, the knob transforms into Liquid Glass during interaction." Adopting Liquid Glass, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass.md
- **Tab bar selection lens.** "At any moment, you can long-press on an iOS 26 tab bar to turn the highlight shape into a 'selection bubble' ... a Liquid Glass element sporting the familiar lensing and chromatic aberration effects." "When holding down, you can then slide your finger across the tab bar". MacStories iOS 26 review, p.3, https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-26-the-macstories-review/3. A rebuild measured "from Apple's own bar in the iOS 26.5 simulator" reports: "the selection lifts into a clear lens that follows your finger exactly", it "settles on the tab let go over", "the selected tab grows 16% and is lensed through it, as in UIKit", "the bar swells, glows and washes under your finger". Liquidum 0.2.1, https://pub.dev/documentation/liquidum/0.2.1/ (third party; not an Apple spec).
- **Bar stretching toward the finger.** Apple does not document this for tab bars; the slider "stretch" (284, 17:24) and "gel-like flexibility" (219) are the nearest primary statements. The post models it as: "If the user pulls past the bounds of the tab bar, we stretch the bar towards their finger." and "when the user stops touching the controller, we settle the lens on the nearest tab." [blog] L158–L159.
- **Sliders, data side.** Tick marks, `neutralValue` (fill anchored at a value), thumbless style. 323, 14:03; 284, 17:24–18:59.

### 1.5 Presentations that grow from their source

- "Like sheets, other presentations such as menus, alerts, and popovers flow smoothly out of liquid glass controls"; "dialogs also automatically morph out of the buttons that present them!"; sheets morph from a toolbar item via `matchedTransitionSource` + `.navigationTransition(.zoom(...))`. 323, App structure (3:07).
- "When showing a menu, the bubble simply pops open to reveal the content contained within." 219, Dynamics.
- Action sheet "springs from the action itself". WWDC25 *Get to know the new design system*, Structure (6:16), https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/
- Sheets: "when focus shifts, like dragging a sheet upward, Liquid Glass subtly recedes, becoming more opaque and gently growing in size". 356, Structure.

### 1.6 Bars

- **Floating tab bar, minimize.** "A tab bar floats above content at the bottom of the screen." With an accessory, "you can choose to minimize the tab bar and move the accessory inline with it when a person scrolls down. A person can exit the minimized state by tapping a tab or scrolling to the top". HIG Tab bars, https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars.md (change log 2026-06-08 "Updated terminology and art"). `tabBarMinimizeBehavior(.onScrollDown)`; "the tab bar re-expands when scrolling in the opposite direction". 323, 3:07.
- **Bottom accessory.** "when the tab bar is normal size, the accessory appears above it; when the tab bar is collapsed, the accessory displays inline." https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/tabviewbottomaccessory(content:).md. Rebuild measurements: "Accessory capsule: 48pt tall, 8pt above the bar"; bar padding 4 pt, bar margin 21 pt (Liquidum, above).
- **Toolbar grouping.** "items grouped using the correct API will automatically update to share a background"; "not group symbols with text". 356, Structure. `ToolbarSpacer` fixed and flexible; `sharedBackgroundVisibility`. 323, Toolbars (7:49).
- **Concentric shapes.** "fixed shapes have a constant corner radius"; "Capsules use a radius that's half the height of the container"; "concentric shapes calculate their radius by subtracting padding from the parent's". 356, Design Language (2:06).

### 1.7 Scroll edge effect

- "As content begins to scroll underneath a glass element, the effect gently dissolves the content into the background"; over dark content "the effect intelligently switches to apply a subtle dimming instead". 219, Adaptivity.
- "scroll edge effects are not decorative. They don't block or darken like overlays." Two styles, soft (default on iOS) and hard; "Apply one scroll edge effect per view." 356, Structure.
- iOS 27: "the .automatic style no longer switches between the existing soft and hard styles but provides its own visuals for additional clarity". WWDC26 *Modernize your UIKit app*, Navigation bars (10:52), https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/278/

### 1.8 Tint and colour

- "Selecting a color generates a range of tones that are mapped to content brightness underneath the tinted element." "Tinting should only be used to bring emphasis to primary elements and actions". "Avoid tinting all your elements." 219, Principles.
- "Tinted glass color, automatically adapts to a vibrant version." 284, 22:33.

### 1.9 Accessibility

- "Reduced Transparency, makes Liquid Glass frostier and obscures more of the content behind it." "Increased contrast, makes elements predominantly black or white and highlights them with a contrasting border". "Reduced Motion decreases the intensity of some effects and disables any elastic properties for the material." 219, Principles.
- Under Reduce Motion "the selection slides under the finger without lifting into a lens, and nothing swells or flexes." Liquidum (third-party rebuild).

### 2.2 SVG filters and refraction in WebKit

- **`backdrop-filter: url()` does nothing in WebKit.** The composited backdrop path refuses reference filters: `m_canCompositeBackdropFilters = !style.backdropFilter().hasReferenceFilter() && ...setBackdropFilters(...)`. [W] Source/WebCore/rendering/RenderLayerBacking.cpp:L1051-L1054. Backdrop filters in WebKit are drawn by a Core Animation `CABackdropLayer`. [W] Source/WebCore/platform/graphics/ca/cocoa/PlatformCALayerCocoa.mm:L244-L245. So there is no non-composited fallback. The open bug, "backdrop-filter: url(#some-svg-filter) doesn't work with SVG filters like feDisplacementMap", reported 2022-09-21, is NEW; a contributor posted PRs 68613/68614 in 2026-07 and 2026-09 that are not landed. https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=245510. Safari parses the value, so `@supports` cannot detect the gap (a third-party report seen only in a search summary, https://dev.to/devyatov/liquid-glass-on-the-web-6-ways-to-build-it-with-css-and-svg-3m07, page not fetched; consistent with the code above, which drops the value at compositing, not parsing).
- **`filter: url()` on an element is not composited.** `m_canCompositeFilters = graphicsLayer->setFilters(hasReferenceFilter ? FilterOperations { } : ...) && !hasReferenceFilter;` [W] Source/WebCore/rendering/RenderLayerBacking.cpp:L1025-L1031. The filtered content is painted in the web process on each repaint.
- **It renders on the CPU by default.** Page-level modes start at `FilterRenderingMode::Software`; `Accelerated` (Core Image) is added only when `acceleratedFiltersEnabled()`. [W] Source/WebCore/page/Page.cpp:L5281-L5303. That preference is `status: unstable`, `defaultValue: false`, "Accelerated CSS and SVG filter rendering". [W] Source/WTF/Scripts/Preferences/UnifiedWebPreferences.yaml:L167-L174. `feDisplacementMap` offers only Software plus that off-by-default Accelerated mode. [W] Source/WebCore/platform/graphics/filters/FEDisplacementMap.cpp:L113-L120. `feGaussianBlur` and `feColorMatrix` can also use a CoreGraphics `GraphicsContext` mode (symmetric blur; matrix/saturate/hueRotate). [W] .../filters/FEGaussianBlur.cpp:L169-L183, .../filters/FEColorMatrix.cpp:L145-L162. A chain drops that mode for any primitive not in sRGB, and the SVG default is linearRGB. [W] Source/WebCore/svg/graphics/filters/SVGFilterRenderer.cpp:L269-L295. Inference: a goo merge (blur, alpha-threshold matrix, composite) or a displacement lens is CPU-rasterised in production Safari, at the main-thread rendering cadence (§2.4).
- **Recent fixes in the area.** Safari 27.0: "Fixed an extremely slow page load on iPhone caused by blur filters with large radii"; "a CSS filter referencing an SVG filter via `url(#id)` was not invalidated". https://webkit.org/blog/18325/webkit-features-for-safari-27-0/. Safari 27.3 beta: "Added support for SVG external resources, allowing `url()` references to SVG resources such as filters". 27.3 beta notes, SVG.
- **An element filter cannot see the backdrop.** `filter` applies to the element's own painting; only `backdrop-filter` reads what is behind (MDN backdrop-filter, above). Inference: in Safari a refraction filter can distort a copy of the bar's own items, never the page under the bar.
- **Backdrop roots.** "The `backdrop-filter` property applies filter effects to the pixels painted behind an element, up to the nearest ancestor that is a backdrop root"; roots include elements with `filter`, `opacity` < 1, `mask`/`clip-path`, `backdrop-filter`, `mix-blend-mode`, or `will-change` on those. https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/backdrop-filter. WebKit implements the concept ("Fixed `will-change: view-transition-name` to create a stacking context and a backdrop root", Safari 26.0 post). Inference: nested web glass sees only its glass parent's painting, which matches Apple's "glass can not sample other glass" (323); sibling glass does see earlier siblings, which Apple's containers exist to make consistent.
- **Chromium's path.** kube.io builds refraction from a precomputed displacement map (Snell's law, index about 1.5) fed to `feDisplacementMap` in `backdrop-filter`; "Only Chrome currently supports using SVG filters as `backdrop-filter`"; dynamic shape or size changes are "currently costly" because they rebuild the map. https://kube.io/blog/liquid-glass-css-svg/ (2025-09-04).

### 2.3 What WebKit composites, and threaded animation

- **Accelerated properties.** `animation-wrapper-acceleration: always`: `opacity` (L7678), `filter` (L10936), `backdrop-filter` (L11333), `transform` (L12887), `translate` (L13035), `scale` (L13056), `rotate` (L13081); `threaded-only`: `offset-path`, `offset-distance`, `offset-position`, `offset-anchor`, `offset-rotate` (L7568-L7641). [W] Source/WebCore/css/CSSProperties.json. `threaded-only` resolves to `settings.threadedScrollDrivenAnimationsEnabled() || settings.threadedTimeBasedAnimationsEnabled()`. [W] Source/WebCore/css/scripts/process-css-properties.py:L4110-L4113. Custom properties return `false`. [W] Source/WebCore/style/StyleInterpolation.cpp:L255-L265. Backdrop-filter animations run on the backdrop layer's effect stack. [W] Source/WebCore/platform/graphics/ca/GraphicsLayerCA.cpp:L721-L726.
- **Not accelerated** (inference from the list): `width`, `height`, `border-radius`, `clip-path`, `background-color`, `box-shadow`, `mask`, and any `@property` value. Their animations run on the main thread. The view-transition group pseudo-element animates `width`/`height` as well as `transform` (inference from the View Transitions spec's default group animation; not re-read here), so the size part of a view-transition morph is main-thread work.
- **Scroll-driven, threaded.** Safari 26.4 (2026-03-24): "Safari 26.4 adds support for threaded Scroll-driven Animations." https://webkit.org/?p=17862. Flag history: enabled by default 2026-01-16 (bug 303641, 305744@main); preference "Run qualifying scroll-driven animations on a separate thread", default true. [W] .../UnifiedWebPreferences.yaml:L6593-L6602. Before that, "Scroll-driven Animations are main thread only at the moment" and "animations may only be performed at 60Hz since this is the page rendering update cadence for main thread content" (Antoine Quint, 2025-03-06), https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=288402 comment 10.
- **Time-based, threaded (27.3 beta).** "Added support for running accelerated time-based animations in the remote layer tree to match how accelerated Scroll-driven Animations are run since Safari 26.4. Accelerated animations are animations targeting `transform` and individual transform CSS properties, `opacity`, `filter` and `backdrop-filter`. Animations that target the `offset` property, composed animations, animations that have a `playbackRate` other than 1 and animations that use the `linear()` and `steps()` easing functions are now also accelerated. (190761783)". Safari 27.3 beta notes, Animations. Trunk history: "Threaded Time-based Animations" re-enabled 2026-09-26 after a YouTube fullscreen power regression (commit 4a435c45f0, bug 325354).
- **Production today (27.2).** Inference: a CSS or WAAPI animation of an accelerated property is still handed to the compositor (the `always` class predates the threaded work), but `linear()` easing and `playbackRate` other than 1 are accelerated only from 27.3. Spring curves written with `linear()` therefore run on the main thread in 27.2 and on the compositor in 27.3.

### 2.4 The 60 Hz cap, the flag, Low Power Mode

- **The preference.** `PreferPageRenderingUpdatesNear60FPSEnabled`, "Prefer page rendering updates near 60 frames per second rather than using the display's refresh rate", `status: stable`, default `true` except visionOS. [W] Source/WTF/Scripts/Preferences/UnifiedWebPreferences.yaml:L5142-L5150. On iOS the display link's nominal rate is `DisplayLinkFramesPerSecond = 60` unless that preference is off (or Web Animations custom frame rate, a testable flag, is on). [W] Source/WebKit/UIProcess/RemoteLayerTree/ios/RemoteLayerTreeDrawingAreaProxyIOS.mm:L47, L265-L277.
- **Where users turn it off.** "Settings → Apps → Safari → Advanced → Feature Flags", then "Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps". MacRumors, 2026-05-05, https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/enable-smoother-120hz-browsing-in-safari/. A WebKit engineer: "you can enable 120Hz page rendering updates on Pro Motion hardware (both macOS and iOS)" by disabling it. Bug 288402 comment 19.
- **Why.** WebKit's explainer, quoted in the bug: "WebKit chose to restrict web content updates to 60Hz for two reasons", first "we measured a significant increase in power usage", second "several examples of web pages that had incorrect behavior". https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=173434 comment 33 (bug NEW since 2017-06-15).
- **Input is held too.** "WebKit limits requestAnimationFrame to just 60 Hz on a 120 Hz screen, and on a regular page, touch and pointer events are held to that same rate." [blog] L83. Measured on an "iPhone Duo": scroll events "5 to 26 times per second during momentum scroll". [blog] L99. A passive `touchmove` listener "fires at the full 120 Hz". [blog] L110. These are the author's readings, not mine.
- **A trunk change.** A WebKit setting lets "remote layer tree monotonic animations ... update at a high frame rate" on iOS (commit 058fb043ac, 2026-02-10, bug 307423). It was enabled by default on iOS on 2026-10-26: "The performance gains obtained by the fix made for bug 325264 mean that we can enable `ThreadedTimeBasedAnimationsAtHighFrameRateEnabled` by default on iOS." (commit 933708cada, bug 328800, 324678@main). Preference: "Run qualifying time-based animations on a separate thread at a high frame rate", `PLATFORM(IOS_FAMILY)`, default `true`. [W] .../UnifiedWebPreferences.yaml:L6610-L6617. The rule: high frame rate when there are "high impact monotonic animations" and that setting is on, or when drawing needs display callbacks and the 60 fps preference is off. [W] .../RemoteLayerTreeDrawingAreaProxyIOS.mm:L492-L505. No release note through 27.3 beta mentions frame rate. Whether 27.3 carries this is unknown.
- **Low Power Mode.** WebKit halves the rendering-update rate: `halfSpeedThrottlingReasons` includes `LowPowerMode`, giving `HalfSpeedThrottlingFramesPerSecond = 30`. [W] Source/WebCore/platform/graphics/AnimationFrameRate.cpp:L33, L47-L65; [W] Source/WebCore/platform/graphics/AnimationFrameRate.h:L56-L57. Apple's own page lists "Turns off some visual effects" and does not mention refresh rate. https://support.apple.com/101604 (2026-09-21). The post's footer: "Turn off Low Power Mode, or use an iPhone with ProMotion to see these demos at 120 Hz." [blog] L172.

### 2.5 The post's 120 Hz paths, classified

- **Scroll containers as input "controllers".** Synchronous `scrollTop`/`scrollLeft` reads on a `MessageChannel` loop throttled by `setTimeout` and phase-locked to rAF timestamps [blog] L99–L103; "an empty `requestAnimationFrame` loop" so Safari "flushes the position without waiting" [blog] L105; "a tiny invisible scroller smooth-scrolls in the background" to hold ProMotion at 120 Hz [blog] L108; DPR scaling for sub-point precision [blog] L113; and the page itself throttled "to 10 Hz during the URL bar resize" unless given a scroll-driven animation of a non-GPU property [blog] L117. Kind: **workaround** (depends on IPC and display-link behaviour WebKit does not document). Scroll snapping and overscroll used for "settle" and "bounce" are idiomatic on their own.
- **OffscreenCanvas presenting at 120 Hz.** "if we create a canvas element and call `transferControlToOffscreen` ... WebKit ... skips waiting for the next rendering pass when an `OffscreenCanvas` pushes a frame." [blog] L133. The author: "it's completely within Apple's rights and would not violate the HTML specification if they just decided to make `OffscreenCanvas`' placeholder wait for the main thread rendering pass." [blog] L172. Kind: **workaround**. Safari 27.3 beta touches this path ("a placeholder `<canvas>` element with no pushed `OffscreenCanvas` frame would fail"), 27.3 beta notes, Canvas.
- **Glass from a page snapshot.** "When the page first loads, we take a snapshot and load it into a WebGL texture"; components then "sample the region beneath". [blog] L141–L143. Kind: **workaround** by the charter's bar (CHARTER.md L21): Safari has no API to read DOM pixels ([BCD] api/CanvasRenderingContext2D.json:L720), so the snapshot is a copy that must be kept live by hand.
