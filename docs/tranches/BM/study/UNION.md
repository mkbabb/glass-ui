## Discrepancies settled while writing

1. **Low Power Mode.** [RI §9] quotes Apple's support page as capping ProMotion at 60 Hz; [IOS §2.4] says the page says nothing on refresh rate. Fetched today, https://support.apple.com/en-us/101604 (published 2026-09-21) lists "Turns off some visual effects" and nothing on refresh rate. WebKit halves page rendering updates to 30 fps in Low Power Mode (`AnimationFrameRate.cpp:L33, L47-65`, [IOS §2.4]). rida's own banner says 60 Hz [RI §7]. This union designs for 30, and the iPhone reading settles it.
2. **What composites.** `motion-canon.md:171-174` (P5) and `dock/styles/index.css:92` call `clip-path`, paint properties and JS-written `--*` customs compositor-safe. WebKit accelerates the transform family, `opacity`, `filter` and `backdrop-filter`, and never a custom property (`CSSProperties.json`; `StyleInterpolation.cpp:L255-265`, [IOS §2.3]). This union uses WebKit's list.
3. **Keyboard motion.** `restraint.md:129-131` (2026-11-09) checks for "no motion on keyboard-triggered actions"; P0 (2026-11-12) says every change is tweened. The later ruling wins (`restraint.md:23`). Carried as Q16.
4. **`declared-decisions.md`** ("Safari is known-broken") reads as another app's file [GI §12.7]. It is not cited here as glass-ui's position.
5. **The eyeglass and O-4.** glass-ui built the iOS 27 tab lens to the owner's order of 2026-07-10 ("perfect, within the bounds of CSS and modern web design, the eye-glass ios27 tabs toggle select effect", [BG] directive:3-4), passed a paint read in a real Safari 26 (`EXECUTION-PROGRESS.md:367`), shipped it, and removed it on O-4, 2026-10-19: "The tabs animations on the filter on the homepage is flashy and too gaudy--more glass-ui and subtle" (`629773393`). The spec that replaced it: "one object moves, the indicator, at its own size, in one monotone leg; the strip does not move and no tab scales on hover or press" (`tests-visual/SegmentedTabs.glide.spec.ts:3-6`), asserted by tabs-seam arm (e) (`tests/gates/tabs-seam.test.ts:24-27`). The 2026-11-22 brief, which asks that "the dock lens" be generalized, is the later ruling (`restraint.md:23`). How much of O-4 survives is Q3, and arm (e) changes only on the owner's word (C-O1).
6. **One blur level.** `restraint.md:60-62`: "One blur level everywhere, and never a blur inside a blur … Check: one `backdrop-filter` value across the app". To the owner: "Your rule is one blur on every glass surface" (`RULINGS-8.md:722`). glass-ui's ladder varies the blur from 10 to 22 px by rung [GI §7], and the dark dock blurs about 11 px against 16 elsewhere (`RULINGS-8.md:722`). This union keeps the rule: a rung differs in veil, not in blur, and no motion changes the blur value. Whether light and dark may differ is Q10 (C-O4).
7. **Transform and opacity only.** `restraint.md:142-143` ("Choreographed, not batched … Transform and opacity only.") against P5's wider list (`motion-canon.md:171-174`) and Apple's materialize by "modulating the light bending" (219 1:29). The restraint line binds until the owner amends it; Q19 asks him (C-O4).
8. **Springs and the compositor.** P5 and P7 treat a spring transition on `transform` as compositor work. Every `--spring-*` curve is a 48-stop `linear()` (`scheme-spring.css:59`; `springProjection.ts:26-68`), and WebKit accelerates `linear()` and `steps()` easing, composed animations and a `playbackRate` other than 1 only from the 27.3 beta ([27.3], Animations, 190761783). In Safari 27.2, in every iOS 26 Safari and in a WKWebView on those, a spring leg therefore runs on the main thread whatever property it animates. This is an inference from the release note ([IOS §2.3]); R2 reads it (C-O6, R-O2).

## 2. The thesis, as checks

Every glass-ui part BM touches passes these checks, and each check names its measurement (R-O3).

1. **One backdrop per container, one blur value.** Count the `backdrop-filter`s per container: one. No blurred element has a blurred ancestor. One blur value across the app (`restraint.md:60-62`); a rung differs in veil only. Glass cannot sample glass, on iOS (323 17:57) or on the web (F5).
2. **One mark per selection group.** One node per group, `aria-hidden` and inert, moved by `translate`. The selection is a real state (`aria-selected`, `aria-current`) with a sign that survives forced colours (A4). The mark settles on the item under the release point (L15).
3. **Commit at the decision.** A tap commits at once; a drag commits the item under the release point; nothing commits for a slot or row passed; the settle plays after the commit (L15).
4. **Two per press, two per node.** A press starts at most two animations, and one node changes at most two properties in one change (`restraint.md:202`, `:204`). Count both for every part, per press.
5. **Accelerated properties, with the easing caveat.** Animate `translate`, `scale` and `opacity`, and `backdrop-filter` only if the owner amends `restraint.md:143` (Q19). Before 27.3 a `linear()` curve runs on the main thread whatever it animates (I22); the delivery is Q6.
6. **Nothing refracts.** Production Safari draws no backdrop displacement, and the only other route is a page snapshot, which the brief refuses (L11).
7. **P0a's amounts and clocks.** Every change is tweened in and out within P0a's amounts and clocks (`motion-canon.md` P0a). Reduced motion is a fade, never a cut (A1).
8. **A split starts under its source.** On frame 0 of a fission the second plate sits under the first (F3; 284 24:33).
9. **Additions are paid for.** Every ADAPT row names what it removes, and the ledger counts both sides in gzipped bytes per site entry (`restraint.md:210`; Q17).

Not in the thesis: the contact law (F1). It stays under Q2 until pass 7 shows a channel a person can see in Safari.

What BM replaces in glass-ui:

- **Removals that free consumer bytes.**
  - The run mark's per-frame length writes and SegmentedTabs' `width` and `height` glide: two writers for one job [GI §3.4, §5].
  - The capsule's blur inside its track's blur, and CommandDialog's plate in a plate [GI §12.4].
  - The menu's `clip-path` strip, which uncovers a plate where it should grow from its trigger [GI §4.3].
  - The grasp carriers and the full-viewport focus veil [GI §3.6, §6.1], and the specular catch-light [GI §7].
  - The luminance clamp's static walk, and the whole clamp (825 lines) if Q10's legibility test holds.
  - The slider loupe, if Q18 finds fewer than two consumers.
  - For releases, settles, entries and exits, the per-frame JS writes of registered custom properties, which WebKit never runs on the compositor [IOS §2.3]. What replaces them reaches the compositor in 27.2 only if R2 shows a curve family 27.2 accelerates (Q6).
- **Removals that cut source and public API but free no consumer bytes.** `useLiquidReveal` and `useDockCtaReceive`, which nothing mounts [GI §9]. The struck neck never shipped, so it frees nothing (R-O14).

The dock's fold stays BL's until 11.2.0 ships. By the charter, BM's new parts and options land in 11.3.0 and its removals in 12.0.0.

## 4. Ten design passes

The passes write one document, `study/DESIGN.md`. Each pass writes its own section, links its pages, and amends any earlier section it overturns, with a line saying so; pass 10 rewrites the whole. Two critics then challenge the pass, reading its section and its pages, and it folds them before the next begins (R-O1).

- **Critic A** reads against the owner's law and restraint: every change tweened in and out, intent first, one focal ink, the per-control ceiling counted per press, no workaround, no masking fallback, clean breaks, every addition paid for.
- **Critic B** reads against production iOS Safari and the evidence: every claim tied to a reading; Playwright WebKit is not Safari and draws no backdrop blur; headless Chromium has no GPU; the consumers' bounds in INBOUND hold.

**Prototypes and readings.** A prototype is a small page in a study worktree, run through `guarded` (WebKit runs with `--exclusive webkit`).

- Headless Chromium gives strips, and Playwright WebKit gives behaviour. A frame-rate claim needs the GPU lane, or it is not made.
- Neither engine draws WebKit's backdrop. Any question about the blur, its shape or its material is settled only on the owner's iPhone (C-O8, R-O1).
- Each prototype pass hosts its page where his iPhone opens it in Safari: a private published page, or a preview deploy where the page needs the library's build. The page shows the candidates side by side in light and dark. One line to the owner says what to look at and what to send back.
- A reading is the owner's report or his screen recording, marked as such.
- A pass whose reading has not returned says so in its first line, and its decision stays provisional until pass 9.
