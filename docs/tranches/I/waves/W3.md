## Lane I — Substrate-tier hierarchy

For each tier (paper / cream / glass):

1. Enumerate paths at HEAD
2. Decide: collapse to one canonical primitive, OR document a layered hierarchy where each path serves a distinct role

Recommended approach (subject to wave-time review):

- **Paper tier**: keep `<Card variant="paper">` as the chrome-aware entry; keep `.paper-N` as the un-chromed lined-paper utility (4-rung ladder). Retire `<PaperBackdrop>` (low usage; β flagged orphan). The two surviving paths serve distinct purposes: `<Card variant="paper">` = card semantics; `.paper-N` = composition-area lined paper.
- **Cream tier**: keep `<CreamSurface>` as the substrate component; keep `.cream-surface` as the underlying utility. Retire `<Card variant="cream">` (consume `<CreamSurface>` inside `<Card>` instead — less special-casing). The collapsed model: `<CreamSurface>` IS the warm-cream substrate; `<Card>` wraps with chrome.
- **Glass tier**: keep `<GlassPanel>` as the WebGL-detected substrate; keep `.glass-{tier}` as the four-tier opacity ladder; both paths are honest (GlassPanel handles the renderer-tier fallback, .glass-X is the static utility). Document the layered model in DESIGN.md.

Either path: write `DESIGN.md ## Substrate Hierarchy` section that names the canonical primitive per tier + the role of each surviving alternate.
